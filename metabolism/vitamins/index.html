Vitamins as cofactors

In brief

Most vitamins are turned into small helper molecules that particular enzymes need in order to work. Lack of one vitamin therefore slows a particular set of reactions and causes a recognisable disease, such as beriberi, pellagra, scurvy or bleeding. In large trials of adults without known deficiency, vitamin supplements gave little or no protection against heart disease, cancer or death.

Coenzymes from B vitamins

Most B vitamins are converted into coenzymes, small molecules that particular enzymes require 1234.

In the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, E1 decarboxylates pyruvate with thiamine pyrophosphate and reductively acetylates a lipoyl group of E2, E2 transfers the acetyl group to coenzyme A, and E3 reoxidises the dihydrolipoyl group with FAD and NAD+ 10.

B12 deficiency traps folate as 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, as the one-carbon chapter explains 8.

Vitamin C and collagen

Humans must obtain vitamin C from food, and it is needed to make collagen, the main protein of skin, bone and connective tissue 11.

Ascorbate keeps the metal ion of dioxygenases reduced, and the procollagen proline and lysine hydroxylases need it for maximal activity 11. Prolyl 4-hydroxylase, a non-haem iron enzyme, forms 4-hydroxyproline, which greatly raises the thermal stability of the collagen triple helix 12. In adults, scurvy develops below a plasma ascorbate of 10 µmol/L, with bleeding gums, bruising and poor wound healing; 10 mg daily prevents it 11.

Vitamin K and warfarin

Vitamin K is needed to activate several blood-clotting proteins, and warfarin acts by blocking its recycling 13.

γ-Glutamyl carboxylase converts glutamate residues of factors II, VII, IX and X and proteins C, S and Z to γ-carboxyglutamate while oxidising vitamin K hydroquinone to the epoxide 13. Vitamin K epoxide reductase reduces the epoxide to the quinone and then to the hydroquinone, and warfarin inhibits both steps 14.

VKORC1 encodes the reductase; its promoter variant c.-1639G>A lowers expression and the warfarin dose required 15. Vitamin K deficiency prolongs the prothrombin time and causes bleeding 13. Without prophylaxis, late vitamin K deficiency bleeding occurred in 4.4 to 7.2 per 100,000 births in western Europe; the estimated relative risk was 81 13.

Supplements in adults without deficiency

In trials of adults without known deficiency, vitamin supplements gave little or no protection against cardiovascular disease, cancer or death 16.

In an evidence review for the US Preventive Services Task Force, two trials of vitamin C 500 mg daily for 3 to 8 years in 15,031 participants found no benefit 16. Multivitamins were associated with less cancer (odds ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.87 to 0.99; 4 trials, 48,859 participants), with absolute differences of −0.2 to −1.2 percentage points in adequately powered trials 16.

Industry-funded results

The Physicians’ Health Study II was funded by NIH and by BASF, which supplied the vitamin C and had no role in the analysis 17. Among 14,641 male physicians followed for a mean 8.0 years, major cardiovascular events occurred in 619 of 7329 (8.45%) assigned vitamin C 500 mg daily and 626 of 7312 (8.56%) assigned placebo (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.89 to 1.11), a difference of −0.1 percentage points 17.

Consensus statements

The US Preventive Services Task Force found the evidence insufficient to assess multivitamins or single or paired nutrients other than β-carotene and vitamin E, and recommends against those two for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer 18.

Unresolved questions

Whether multivitamins lower cancer incidence is unsettled: the pooled estimate rested on three adequately powered trials, one with a median 3.6 years of use and one limited to antioxidants 16.

    Riboflavin is the integral part of the coenzymes FAD and FMN, cofactors of flavoprotein enzymes; flavokinase phosphorylates free riboflavin to FMN, which FAD synthetase converts to FAD.     Ducker GS, Rabinowitz JD. One-Carbon Metabolism in Health and Disease Cell Metabolism, 2017.

    doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2016.08.009PubMed 27641100

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    G.S.D. is supported by a postdoctoral fellowship from the American Cancer Society (PF-15-190-01-TBE). J.D.R. is supported by NIH grants R01CA163591 and P30DK019525 and Stand Up to Cancer.
    Finding
    Review. Animals require dietary folate. Tetrahydrofolate (THF) binds one-carbon units at N5 and N10 at three oxidation states: 5-methyl-THF, the most reduced, 5,10-methylene-THF and 10-formyl-THF, the most oxidised; folic acid is reduced to dihydrofolate (DHF) and then THF. SHMT1 (cytosol) and SHMT2 (mitochondria) interconvert serine and glycine with THF and 5,10-methylene-THF, reversibly; in rapidly proliferating cultured cells the main direct source of cytosolic one-carbon units is formate released from mitochondria, reassimilated by the trifunctional MTHFD1. TYMS methylates dUMP to dTMP with 5,10-methylene-THF and is the only folate enzyme that alters THF itself, taking two electrons from it and producing DHF; DHFR regenerates THF with NADPH and is the target of trimethoprim and methotrexate, which trap folate as DHF. Purine synthesis incorporates two 10-formyl-THF units; in proliferating cells each new DNA or RNA base needs on average one 10-formyl-THF, and 5,10-methylene-THF is needed for one in four DNA bases. 5-Fluorouracil is a suicide inhibitor of TYMS; methotrexate most potently blocks DHFR, whereas pemetrexed more potently inhibits TYMS and AICAR transformylase; 5-formyl-THF (folinic acid, leucovorin) is given as rescue. 5-Methyl-THF, made by cytosolic NADPH-dependent MTHFR, is consumed only by cobalamin-dependent methionine synthase; BHMT is limited to liver and kidney. SAM inhibits MTHFR and activates CBS; 5-methyl-THF inhibits GNMT. Transsulfuration is the only route that degrades homocysteine. Folate and cobalamin supplementation lower homocysteine without reducing cardiovascular events in the general population. Dietary folate deficiency in adults manifests as macrocytic anaemia.

    EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA Panel), Turck D, Bohn T, Castenmiller J, de Henauw S, Hirsch-Ernst KI, et al. Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for folate EFSA Journal, 2023.

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority, on request from the European Commission (question EFSA-Q-2021-00366). Preparatory systematic reviews were conducted by a contractor, Karolinska Institutet in collaboration with the University of Oslo, under an EFSA procurement procedure.
    Finding
    Folates consist of a pterin moiety linked to p-aminobenzoic acid and one or more glutamates, substituted at N5 or N10 by one-carbon units; natural folates are reduced polyglutamates usually with five to seven glutamates. A jejunal hydrolase (EC 3.4.17.21) converts polyglutamates to monoglutamates, which PCFT (SLC46A1), the most important intestinal transporter, and RFC (SLC19A1) move into enterocytes; 5-methyl-THF is the main circulating form; RFC, the main transporter from blood to tissues, has about 100 times lower affinity for folic acid than for reduced folates. Folylpolyglutamate synthase retains folates as THF polyglutamates; 5-methyl-THF monoglutamate and folic acid are poor substrates, and 5-methyl-THF is lost from cells unless methionine synthase converts it to THF. Folic acid, a fully oxidised monoglutamate, is reduced by DHFR via dihydrofolate to THF. The MTHFR reaction is essentially irreversible under physiological conditions and SAM is its allosteric inhibitor; in cobalamin deficiency 5-methyl-THF is metabolically trapped, SAM falls, MTHFR becomes fully active, and a functional folate deficiency develops with raised plasma 5-methyl-THF and homocysteine, depleted erythrocyte folate and fewer methyl groups for transmethylation. The megaloblastic anaemia of cobalamin deficiency is identical to that of folate deficiency; neurological manifestations (myeloneuropathy from demyelination) can occur with or without anaemia, are almost never seen in folate deficiency, and have an unknown mechanism, with congenital defects of MTHFR and methionine synthase pointing to methionine synthase rather than methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Folic acid can initiate DNA synthesis in cobalamin deficiency through DHFR but cannot correct the methylation defect. MTHFR 677TT is associated with lower serum folate and higher homocysteine; in a genome-wide study of 2232 Irish adults aged 18 to 28 years MTHFR 677C>T was the only significant modifier of red cell and serum folate. ULs for folic acid retained: 1000 ug/day for adults including pregnant and lactating women, with progression of neurological symptoms in cobalamin-deficient patients as critical effect.

    Finding
    Review. Mammals have two B12-dependent enzymes: methionine synthase, which uses methylcobalamin, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase, which uses adenosylcobalamin to convert methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. Methionine synthase works in two half-reactions: methylcobalamin methylates homocysteine to methionine, leaving cob(I)alamin, which removes the methyl group from 5-methyl-THF to give THF and methylcobalamin; occasional oxidation to cob(II)alamin is repaired by reductive methylation needing NADPH, methionine synthase reductase and S-adenosylmethionine. Food cobalamin binds haptocorrin; in the duodenum pancreatic proteases release it, intrinsic factor binds it, and intestinal cells take it up by cubilin/AMN receptor-mediated endocytosis. About 20% of circulating cobalamin is bound to transcobalamin, which delivers it to cells through the transcobalamin receptor. Mutations in the cblG (methionine synthase) and cblE (reductase) loci cause isolated hyperhomocysteinaemia without methylmalonic aciduria.

    Finding
    Review. The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex links glycolysis to the tricarboxylic acid cycle and comprises pyruvate dehydrogenase (E1), dihydrolipoamide acetyltransferase (E2) and dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase (E3), which work sequentially. E1, a thiamin diphosphate-dependent enzyme, decarboxylates pyruvate and reductively acetylates the lipoyl groups covalently attached to E2; E2 transfers the acetyl group to CoA, forming acetyl-CoA; E3 transfers electrons from the dihydrolipoyl groups of E2 to FAD and then to NAD+.

    EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (NDA). Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for vitamin C EFSA Journal, 2013.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2013.3418

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority, on request from the European Commission (question EFSA-Q-2011-01229).
    Finding
    Vitamin C is an essential component of the diet, as humans and other primates have lost the ability to synthesise it. It is a cofactor of monooxygenases, dioxygenases and mixed function oxygenases, reducing the active-centre metal ion and maintaining it in a reduced state. Procollagen-proline dioxygenase and procollagen-lysine 5-dioxygenase require vitamin C for maximal activity, and deficiency impairs the synthesis of collagen, the principal protein of skin, bones, teeth and connective tissues. Scurvy occurs in adults at a plasma ascorbate below 10 umol/L and a body pool below 300 mg, with tooth loss, joint pain, poor wound healing, petechiae, bruising and inflamed, bleeding gums; an intake of 10 mg/day prevents it.

    Funding

    Finding
    Vitamin K is the cofactor of gamma-glutamyl carboxylase (GGCX), which converts glutamic acid residues of vitamin K-dependent proteins, including coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X and the anticoagulant proteins C, S and Z, into calcium-binding gamma-carboxyglutamic acid residues; vitamin K hydroquinone is converted to vitamin K epoxide and reduced back by vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR), the step of the cycle blocked by warfarin. In adults, deficiency causes a bleeding tendency with a vitamin K-responsive increase in prothrombin time. In infants not given vitamin K prophylaxis, late vitamin K deficiency bleeding occurred in 4.4 to 7.2 per 100,000 births in Western European countries; its relative risk without prophylaxis is estimated at 81.

    Eleven crystal structures of human vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR) and a VKOR-like paralog with substrates and antagonists. Vitamin K antagonists, including warfarin, inhibit VKOR at both the epoxide and the quinone reduction steps.
