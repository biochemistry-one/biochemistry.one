Metabolism

How the body processes medicines

In brief

Medicines are chemicals that the body alters and removes, mostly in the liver and the wall of the gut. One enzyme family, cytochrome P450, performs most of the alteration, other enzymes attach water-soluble groups, and transport proteins move the products out. People inherit differences in these steps, so the effect of a dose of codeine or clopidogrel differs between people.

First pass

A swallowed tablet passes the gut wall and the liver before the rest of the body, and both remove part of the dose 12.

Enterocytes and hepatocytes both express CYP3A4 1.

Oxidation by cytochrome P450

One enzyme family, cytochrome P450, performs most of the oxidation of medicines 3.

Each P450 of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane inserts one oxygen atom into its substrate and reduces the second to water, with two electrons from NADPH through cytochrome P450 oxidoreductase 4. Of 4192 reported oxidation and reduction reactions of 860 drugs, about 96% were P450 reactions: CYP3A4 27%, CYP2D6 13%, CYP2C9 10%, CYP2C19 and CYP1A2 9% each, CYP2E1 3% 3. CYP2E1, with CYP1A2 and CYP2A6, oxidises paracetamol to the reactive imine NAPQI, 5% to 10% of its urinary metabolites 5.

Conjugation

Other enzymes attach a water-soluble group, so bile or urine can remove the drug 4.

UGTs transfer glucuronic acid from UDP-glucuronate, sulfotransferases a sulfonate group from PAPS, NAT2 an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA and TPMT a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine 4. TPMT methylates the mercaptopurine formed from azathioprine; 1 in 300 people of European or African ancestry have almost no TPMT activity 6.

Transporters

Transport proteins move drugs into and out of gut, liver and kidney cells 4.

OATP1B1 (SLCO1B1) takes organic anions, statins among them, into the hepatocyte across its basolateral membrane; P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) and BCRP (ABCG2) pump drugs out across apical membranes 4. In 8 volunteers, 10 days of rifampicin 600 mg daily raised duodenal P-glycoprotein 3.5-fold and lowered the peak concentration of 1 mg oral digoxin by 58% 2.

Induction and inhibition

Other medicines and some foods change the amount or activity of these proteins, and so the effect of a dose 71.

Rifampicin induces CYP1A2, CYP2C19, CYP3A4 and three further P450s, UGTs, sulfotransferases, carboxylesterases, P-glycoprotein and MRP2; oxycodone exposure falls by 86% 7. Grapefruit juice acts the other way: in 10 men, 8 oz of it three times daily for 6 days lowered enterocyte CYP3A4 protein by 62%, hepatic activity unchanged, and with the first glass felodipine peaked at 17.9 nmol/l against 5.5 with water 1. Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19 and lowers the active metabolite of clopidogrel, and the FDA advises against the pair 8.

Inherited differences

People differ in the speed of these steps, partly by inheritance 9.

Codeine is a prodrug: CYP2D6 O-demethylates 5% to 10% of a dose to morphine; UGT2B7 and UGT2B4 conjugate 70% to 80% to codeine-6-glucuronide 9. Duplication of CYP2D6 gives ultrarapid metabolism, 1% to 10% of white Europeans and North Americans; children have died of respiratory depression, and codeine is contraindicated below 12 years 9.

Clopidogrel is a prodrug too: esterases hydrolyse about 85% of a dose to an inactive acid, and two oxidations, CYP2C19 prominent in both, yield the thiol that blocks P2Y12 irreversibly 10. Among 227 patients after coronary intervention, an ischaemic event or cardiovascular death within 1 year occurred in 20.9% of CYP2C19*2 carriers against 10.0% of others (hazard ratio 2.42, 95% CI 1.18 to 4.99) 11.

Consensus statements

CPIC translates CYP2D6 diplotype into an activity score, above 2.25 ultrarapid and 0 poor, and advises against codeine in both: toxicity in the one, weak analgesia in the other 12. For CYP2C19 intermediate and poor metabolisers CPIC advises prasugrel or ticagrelor, which lowered major ischaemic events in those phenotypes across seven trials with 15,949 patients (risk ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.59 to 0.83) and not in non-carriers 13.

Industry-funded results

COGENT, funded by Cogentus Pharmaceuticals and stopped when the sponsor lost financing, randomised patients on aspirin and clopidogrel to omeprazole or placebo; among 3761 analysed, cardiovascular events at 180 days were 4.9% against 5.7% (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.44) and gastrointestinal events 1.1% against 2.9% 14. In 18 volunteers given isoniazid, NAT2 genotype accounted for 88% of the variance in apparent clearance, in a study funded by the maker of the preparations used 15.

Unresolved questions

Whether proton-pump inhibitors blunt clopidogrel is unsettled: among Ontario patients taking clopidogrel after myocardial infarction, use of one was associated with reinfarction within 90 days (adjusted odds ratio 1.27, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.57), and the one randomised comparison, stopped early, found no cardiovascular difference 1614. Morphine formation from codeine varies widely among people of one CYP2D6 diplotype, from unknown causes 12.

CYP3A4 (P08684), CYP2D6 (P10635), CYP2C9 (P11712), CYP2C19 (P33261), CYP1A2 (P05177) and CYP2E1 (P05181) are cytochrome P450 monooxygenases of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, CYP2E1 also of the mitochondrial inner membrane; each inserts one atom of molecular oxygen into a substrate and reduces the second to a water molecule, with two electrons provided by NADPH via cytochrome P450 reductase, and each catalyses the hydroxylation of carbon-hydrogen bonds. CYP2D6 is also involved in the oxidative metabolism of drugs such as antiarrhythmics, adrenoceptor antagonists and tricyclic antidepressants; CYP2C19 in that of S-mephenytoin, omeprazole, proguanil, certain barbiturates, diazepam, propranolol, citalopram and imipramine; CYP2E1 in that of xenobiotics (probable). POR (P16435), NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase, is required for electron transfer from NADP to cytochrome P450 in microsomes. UGT2B7 (P16662), of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, conjugates lipophilic substrates with glucuronic acid, which increases the water solubility of the metabolite and so its excretion into urine or bile: glucuronate acceptor + UDP-alpha-D-glucuronate = acceptor beta-D-glucuronoside + UDP + H+. SULT1A1 (P50225) uses 3'-phospho-5'-adenylyl sulfate (PAPS) as sulfonate donor for acceptors bearing a hydroxyl or amine group, a phenol + PAPS = an aryl sulfate + adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate + H+, with broad specificity for small phenolic compounds and sulfate conjugation of xenobiotics including acetaminophen and minoxidil (by similarity); sulfonation raises water solubility and renal excretion of most compounds but can also bioactivate, as with carcinogenic N-hydroxyarylamines. NAT2 (P11245) catalyses the N- or O-acetylation of arylamine and heterocyclic amine substrates, an arylamine + acetyl-CoA = an N-acetylarylamine + CoA, and participates in the detoxification of hydrazine and arylamine drugs while bioactivating several known carcinogens. TPMT (P51580) catalyses the S-methylation of thiopurine drugs such as mercaptopurine and 6-thioguanine with S-adenosyl-L-methionine as methyl donor, and its activity modulates the cytotoxic effects of thiopurine prodrugs; no natural substrate has been identified. SLCO1B1 (Q9Y6L6, OATP1B1), of the basolateral hepatocyte membrane, mediates Na+-independent uptake of organic anions, among them taurocholate, conjugated steroids, eicosanoids, thyroid hormones, bilirubin glucuronides and HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors such as pravastatin and pitavastatin, with hydrogencarbonate the probable counteranion. ABCB1 (P08183, P-glycoprotein), of the apical cell membrane, is an energy-dependent efflux pump responsible for decreased drug accumulation in multidrug-resistant cells, translocates phospholipids and drugs across the membrane, and may contribute to hepatobiliary and renal excretion of etiocholanolone-3-glucuronate. ABCG2 (Q9UNQ0, BCRP), also of the apical cell membrane, is an ATP-dependent transporter of broad substrate specificity that extrudes physiological compounds, dietary toxins and xenobiotics, as well as urate, sulfate conjugates and protoporphyrin IX; it operates at the apical membranes of polarised cells such as hepatocytes and intestinal epithelial cells, where it may contribute to the efflux of conjugated substrates from intestine and liver into the intestinal lumen, and it exports urate in both renal and extrarenal urate excretion.

    Finding
    Oral bioavailability of paracetamol is 88% and the plasma half-life 1.5 to 2.5 hours at recommended doses. After a therapeutic dose, urinary metabolites are the glucuronide (52 to 57%), the sulfate (30 to 44%) and products of oxidation to N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI, 5 to 10%); less than 5% is excreted unchanged. UGT1A1, UGT1A6, UGT1A9 and UGT2B15 catalyse glucuronidation; SULT1A1 and SULT1A3/4 catalyse sulfation, with SULT1E1 and SULT2A1 in fetal liver; CYP2E1, with CYP1A2 and CYP2A6, forms NAPQI. NAPQI is conjugated with glutathione spontaneously and by glutathione S-transferases, GSTP1 being the most effective catalyst, followed by GSTT1 and GSTM1, and is excreted as cysteine and mercapturic acid conjugates. ABCC2 and ABCG2 of the canalicular membrane move the glucuronide and the sulfate into bile; movement of the glucuronide into blood depends on ABCC3 and that of the sulfate on ABCC3 with ABCC4, both of the sinusoidal membrane. Above 4 g per day sulfation saturates, and after highly toxic doses more than 15% is oxidised and about 10% excreted unchanged. The contribution of CYP3A4 is reported as ranging from negligible to primary.

    Finding
    Azathioprine is cleaved to 6-mercaptopurine, which hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase and kinases convert to 6-thioguanine nucleotides; cytotoxicity follows in part from their incorporation into DNA. Mercaptopurine has two major inactivation routes: thiol methylation, catalysed by thiopurine S-methyltransferase (TPMT), to methyl-6-mercaptopurine, and oxidation, catalysed by xanthine oxidase, to 6-thiouric acid; NUDT15 converts 6-thioguanine nucleotides to inactive monophosphates. TPMT activity correlates inversely with 6-thioguanine nucleotide concentrations in erythrocytes, which have negligible xanthine oxidase activity. About 0.3%, or 1 in 300, of patients of European or African ancestry carry two loss-of-function TPMT alleles and have little or no TPMT activity, and about 10% carry one, giving intermediate activity. Patients with reduced TPMT or NUDT15 activity given usual doses accumulate 6-thioguanine nucleotides and are at higher risk of severe myelosuppression, and those with TPMT or NUDT15 deficiency require alternative therapy or dose modification.

    Finding
    Metabolism: about 70% to 80% of a codeine dose is conjugated with glucuronic acid to codeine-6-glucuronide, about 5% to 10% is O-demethylated to morphine and about 10% is N-demethylated to norcodeine. UGT2B7 and UGT2B4 are the major enzymes of the glucuronidation, CYP2D6 of the conversion to morphine and CYP3A4 of the conversion to norcodeine. Morphine and norcodeine are further conjugated with glucuronic acid; morphine-6-glucuronide has analgesic activity in humans and morphine-3-glucuronide is not considered to have it. About 90% of the dose is excreted renally, about 10% of it as unchanged codeine, and the plasma half-lives of codeine and its metabolites are about 3 hours. Peak plasma concentration occurs 60 minutes after an oral dose; plasma protein binding is 7% to 25%. Ultrarapid metabolisers carry CYP2D6 gene duplications such as *1/*1xN or *1/*2xN; the estimated prevalence of this phenotype is 1% to 10% for Whites (European, North American), 3% to 4% for Blacks (African Americans), 1% to 2% for East Asians (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) and above 10% in certain groups (Oceanian, Northern African, Middle Eastern, Ashkenazi Jews, Puerto Rican). Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children given codeine, most cases after tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy and many in ultrarapid metabolisers; the drug is contraindicated in children younger than 12 years and, for post-operative use, in those younger than 18 years after tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy, and breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.

    Funding

    Finding
    Clopidogrel is a prodrug absorbed in the intestine and activated in the liver by two sequential oxidative steps, the first forming 2-oxo-clopidogrel and the second the active metabolite. CYP1A2, CYP2B6 and CYP2C19 form 2-oxo-clopidogrel from clopidogrel in liver microsomes; with 2-oxo-clopidogrel as substrate, CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP2B6 produce the active metabolite. Kazui and colleagues concluded that CYP2C19 contributes substantially to both steps and CYP3A4 to the second. In a competing reaction about 85% of the drug is hydrolysed by esterases to an inactive carboxylic acid. The thiol group of the active metabolite binds a free cysteine of the P2Y12 receptor (P2RY12) and irreversibly blocks ADP binding, so the platelet is affected for its whole lifespan of about 7 to 10 days. Clopidogrel is not detected in human plasma. Between 20% and 40% of patients show low inhibition of ADP-induced platelet aggregation. ABCB1 takes part in intestinal absorption. Interactions have been reported with atorvastatin, verapamil and omeprazole.

    Finding
    Each CYP2D6 allele is assigned an activity value from 0 to 1, multiplied by copy number where a functional gene is duplicated, and the values of the two alleles are summed into an activity score, which typically ranges from 0 to 3. Phenotypes: ultrarapid metaboliser above 2.25, normal 1.25 to 2.25, intermediate above 0 and below 1.25, poor 0. Codeine and tramadol are O-demethylated by CYP2D6 to the more active metabolites morphine and O-desmethyltramadol. Pharmacokinetic studies found 96% lower mean serum morphine area under the curve and 95% lower peak concentration in poor metabolisers than in normal and intermediate metabolisers, and 45% higher median morphine area under the curve in ultrarapid than in normal metabolisers. Codeine recommendations: avoid in ultrarapid metabolisers because of the potential for serious toxicity and in poor metabolisers because of possible diminished analgesia, both strong; use label-recommended dosing in normal metabolisers (strong) and in intermediate metabolisers (moderate). Tramadol recommendations follow the same pattern. For hydrocodone in ultrarapid metabolisers no recommendation is given. Variation among individuals carrying the same diplotype is large and its genomic or environmental causes are unknown.

    Finding
    Clopidogrel is a thienopyridine prodrug. Only 15% of the dose is available for transformation to the active agent, the remaining 85% being hydrolysed by carboxylesterase-1 to inactive forms. Conversion requires two sequential oxidative steps involving CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5, with the greatest contribution from CYP2C19 in both steps; the active metabolite selectively and irreversibly inhibits the P2Y12 receptor for the platelet lifespan of about 10 days. For acute coronary syndrome or percutaneous coronary intervention, CYP2C19 intermediate and poor metabolisers should avoid standard-dose clopidogrel and receive prasugrel or ticagrelor where not contraindicated (strong for both phenotypes); normal, rapid and ultrarapid metabolisers should receive the standard 75 mg daily dose (strong). In a meta-analysis of seven randomised trials of genotype-guided antiplatelet therapy with 15,949 patients (77% percutaneous coronary intervention, 98% acute coronary syndrome), prasugrel or ticagrelor reduced major ischaemic events against clopidogrel in intermediate and poor metabolisers (risk ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.59 to 0.83) but not in non-carriers of no-function alleles (risk ratio 1.0, 95% CI 0.80 to 1.25), with a genotype-treatment interaction at P = 0.013.

    Both oral preparations given, ISOZID and ISOZID COMP. N, are products of the funding company.
    Finding
    Isoniazid is metabolised by the genetically polymorphic arylamine N-acetyltransferase type 2 (NAT2). NAT2 genotype, drug formulation and body weight together explained 93% of the variability in apparent isoniazid clearance, and NAT2 genotype alone 88%. Individual clearance was predicted as clearance in litres per hour = 10 + 9 × the number of high-activity NAT2*4 alleles. To reach similar exposure, standard doses suited to one high-activity allele would be lowered or raised by about 50% for carriers of none or of two. The authors state that prospective clinical trials are required to assess the merits of this approach.

  16. Independent research

    Juurlink DN, Gomes T, Ko DT, Szmitko PE, Austin PC, Tu JV, et al. A population-based study of the drug interaction between proton pump inhibitors and clopidogrel CMAJ (Canadian Medical Association Journal), 2009.

    doi:10.1503/cmaj.082001PubMed 19176635

    Funding and finding
    Participants
    13,636
    Follow-up
    90 days after discharge; secondary analysis 1 year
    Funding
    This study was supported by the Ontario Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care, the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) and a CIHR Team Grant in Cardiovascular Outcomes Research; authors held awards from CIHR, the Heart and Stroke Foundation of Ontario and a Canada Research Chair.
    Finding
    Clopidogrel is a prodrug converted in the liver, chiefly by CYP2C19, to a thiol metabolite that irreversibly inhibits the platelet P2Y12 receptor. Current PPI use was associated with reinfarction within 90 days (adjusted OR 1.27, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.57); pantoprazole was not (1.02, 0.70 to 1.47), whereas omeprazole, lansoprazole and rabeprazole together were (1.40, 1.10 to 1.77). H2-receptor antagonists and PPI use in patients not taking clopidogrel were not associated with reinfarction.
