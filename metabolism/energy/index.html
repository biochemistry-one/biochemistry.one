Metabolism

Energy and ATP

In brief

Cells oxidise food and hold the released energy briefly in ATP, in molecules that have accepted electrons, and in a proton gradient across the inner membrane of mitochondria. Heart and skeletal muscle contain a reserve for a few seconds as creatine phosphate. At rest, the heart, kidneys, brain and liver use far more energy per kilogram than muscle or fat.

Free energy and coupling

Cells drive many reactions by coupling them to ATP breakdown, which releases energy 1.

The free-energy change, ΔG = ΔG°′ + RT ln Q, is zero at equilibrium and grows in magnitude as concentrations depart from it 1. An ATPase is driven by the excess of ATP’s free energy of hydrolysis over its own requirement; the cardiac calcium pump SERCA has the smallest excess 2.

ATP in the cell

Inside cells, ATP hydrolysis releases about 1.5 to 1.8 times the energy it releases under standard conditions 1.

With all reactants at 1 M, ΔG°′ is −28 to −34 kJ/mol, depending on Mg2+ 1. At cellular concentrations, far below 1 M, ΔG is about −50 kJ/mol; about −70 kJ/mol was calculated for muscle of athletes recovering from exertion 1.

Daily ATP turnover

The heart and brain contain little ATP but make kilograms of it every day 23.

The adult heart contains about 0.6 g of ATP and requires about 6 kg a day 2. The brain contains about 2 g; ³¹P magnetic resonance gives its oxidative ATP synthesis as 7–8 kg a day 3. For the whole body, BioNumbers lists about 60 kg, one body weight, a day, calculated from oxygen consumption at 36 ATP per glucose 4.

NAD+, FAD and NADP+

Cells pass electrons from food to small molecules made from the B vitamins niacin and riboflavin 56.

NAD+ accepts two electrons and one proton; NAD, relatively oxidised, serves catabolism, and NADP, relatively reduced, biosynthesis 1. Succinate dehydrogenase, complex II, binds FAD covalently to a histidine and transfers electrons from succinate to ubiquinone 7. Hepatic oxidation of alcohol raises the NADH/NAD+ ratio, which stimulates fatty acid synthesis 8.

Proton-motive force

In mitochondria, electron flow to oxygen pumps protons across the inner membrane; their return turns ATP synthase, a rotary enzyme that makes ATP 97.

The mitochondrial membrane potential is about 160 mV; each pH unit adds about 60 mV to the proton-motive force 1. Bovine and human c-rings have eight subunits, so each turn moves eight protons and makes three ATP 97. The presumed ten protons pumped per NADH and six per succinate, plus one per ATP for transport, predict P/O ratios of 2.7 and 1.6, against measured 2.5 and 1.5 9.

Creatine phosphate

Muscle contains a small reserve of creatine phosphate (phosphocreatine), which rebuilds ATP in one step 10.

Creatine kinase catalyses MgADP + phosphocreatine + H+ ⇌ MgATP + creatine near equilibrium; the equilibrium constant is 100 at pH 7.0 and 1 mM Mg2+ 102. Phosphocreatine exceeds ATP only twofold in heart and fourfold in skeletal muscle, enough to maintain ATP for a few seconds 10.

Energy use by organ

At rest, a few small organs use much of the body’s energy 113.

MRI organ masses and Elia’s specific rates predicted the 1575 kcal a day measured by calorimetry in 131 healthy adults 11.

Unresolved questions

The importance of a creatine kinase shuttle from mitochondria to ATPases is disputed: one review states that it delivers 85% of cardiac oxidative energy 2, another notes very modest changes in knockout mice 10. A review gives 50 kg of ATP a day for a healthy adult without method or reference 12. For metformin, some reports find complex I inhibition only at millimolar concentrations, against 50–100 µM in plasma on 2 g daily 13.

ATP is often called the energy currency of the cell; hundreds of cellular reactions are coupled to its hydrolysis, with the membrane potential and reducing power the two other dominant energy sources. Each electron transfer of respiration must be thermodynamically favourable on its own for respiration to proceed. Figure 1 derives the free energy under physiological concentrations from the standard value as ΔG′ = ΔG′° + RT ln Q′. The absolute free-energy change of ATP hydrolysis is greater the farther concentrations lie from equilibrium, where it is zero; the standard value varies from -28 to -34 kJ/mol depending on the Mg2+ concentration; with millimolar ATP, ADP and phosphate it is about 50 kJ/mol under physiological conditions; the highest value, about -70 kJ/mol, was calculated for human muscle of athletes recovering after exertion; in cells ATP is worth about twice as much as under standard conditions. Individual transfers of the electron transport chain are not energetic enough to make ATP but are enough to pump a proton.     Riboflavin is the integral part of the coenzymes FAD and FMN, cofactors of flavoprotein enzymes; flavokinase phosphorylates free riboflavin to FMN, which FAD synthetase converts to FAD.     Teschke R. Alcoholic liver disease: alcohol metabolism, cascade of molecular mechanisms, cellular targets, and clinical aspects Biomedicines, 2018.

    doi:10.3390/biomedicines6040106PubMed 30424581

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    There was no funding of this article.
    Finding
    Narrative review. In the liver, ADH (cytosol, NAD+), the microsomal ethanol-oxidizing system with CYP2E1 (endoplasmic reticulum, NADPH and O2) and catalase (peroxisomes, H2O2) oxidise ethanol to acetaldehyde; ADH and MEOS, but not catalase, are relevant in vivo. Km for ethanol: ADH 0.5 to 2.0 mM (animal studies), MEOS 7 to 11 mM, catalase 0.6 to 10 mM. MEOS is induced by chronic alcohol consumption through increased CYP2E1 synthesis. Mitochondrial ALDH oxidises acetaldehyde to acetate, which is released into the blood and oxidised in extrahepatic tissues; ADH and ALDH both generate NADH. The raised NADH/NAD+ ratio stimulates hepatic fatty acid synthesis and alpha-glycerophosphate supply, and acetaldehyde impairs mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation.

    Watt IN, Montgomery MG, Runswick MJ, Leslie AG, Walker JE. Bioenergetic cost of making an adenosine triphosphate molecule in animal mitochondria Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 2010.

    doi:10.1073/pnas.1011099107PubMed 20847295

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    This work was funded by the Medical Research Council.
    Finding
    In the 3.5 Å crystal structure of the bovine F1-c-ring complex (PDB 2xnd), the c-ring has eight c subunits, so eight protons are translocated per 360° rotation, which produces three ATP: 2.7 protons per ATP. c-Subunit sequences are identical in almost all vertebrates. For each two electrons transferred to oxygen from NADH or succinate, ten or six protons are thought to be pumped out of the matrix; ADP/ATP exchange and phosphate-proton symport add one proton, a cost of 3.7 protons per ATP supplied to the cytoplasm, predicting P/O ratios of 2.7 and 1.6, close to experimental values of 2.5 and 1.5. The proton-motive force generated by respiration drives ATP synthesis.

    Glancy B, Balaban RS. Energy metabolism design of the striated muscle cell Physiological Reviews, 2021.

    doi:10.1152/physrev.00040.2020PubMed 33733879

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    This work was supported by funding from the Division of Intramural Research of the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute and the Intramural Research Program of the National Institute of Arthritis and Musculoskeletal and Skin Disease.
    Finding
    Narrative review. Creatine kinase catalyses MgADP + PCr + H+ = MgATP + Cr (Keq 100 at 1 mM Mg2+, pH 7.0) and is believed to operate near equilibrium in heart and skeletal muscle. The PCr pool serves as a short-term buffer for ATP, of small capacity because PCr exceeds ATP only twofold in heart and fourfold in muscle; creatine kinase can transiently maintain ATP using PCr for a few seconds. Bessman and co-workers proposed a creatine kinase shuttle carrying high-energy phosphate from mitochondria to myofibrils; many have questioned it, creatine kinase knockout mice show very modest changes, and the authors conclude the shuttle has a potentially secondary role.

    Petersen MC, Vatner DF, Shulman GI. Regulation of hepatic glucose metabolism in health and disease Nature Reviews Endocrinology, 2017.

    doi:10.1038/nrendo.2017.80PubMed 28731034

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    M.C.P. acknowledges grant support from the US National Institutes of Health (NIH; grants F30 DK-104596 and T32 GM-007205). D.F.V. acknowledges grant support from the NIH (grant K23 DK-102874). G.I.S. acknowledges grant support from the NIH (grants R01 DK40936, R01 DK113984 and P30 DK045735).
    Finding
    Narrative review. Glucagon receptor (GCGR) activation raises cAMP and activates PKA, which inhibits L-type pyruvate kinase, phosphorylates PFK-2/FBPase-2 to favour its phosphatase activity and lower fructose 2,6-bisphosphate, an allosteric inhibitor of FBPase-1 (printed as fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase 1), phosphorylates and inactivates glycogen synthase, and activates phosphorylase kinase, which phosphorylates glycogen phosphorylase at Ser15; PKA also activates the CREB-CBP-CRTC2 complex for gluconeogenic gene transcription. FOXO proteins with PGC1-alpha raise PCK1 and G6PC expression and are excluded from the nucleus after AKT phosphorylation. Glucose enters hepatocytes by facilitated diffusion through GLUT2; glucose allosterically inhibits glycogen phosphorylase; glucose 6-phosphate is the key allosteric activator of GYS2. In overnight-fasted humans under somatostatin, hyperinsulinaemia was required to stimulate hepatic glycogen synthesis and hyperglycaemia to suppress glycogenolysis. Hepatic glucose production and storage are dysregulated in type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, and this imbalance contributes to hyperglycaemia in the fasted and postprandial states. Metformin is used as first-line therapy for type 2 diabetes and acts primarily through the suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis, by a molecular mechanism that remains under investigation. Several lines of evidence cast doubt on the necessity of AMPK: metformin continues to inhibit glucose production in Ampk-knockout primary mouse hepatocytes and improves glucose tolerance in Ampk-knockout mice to a similar extent as in wild-type mice. Inhibition of mitochondrial complex I is observed only at suprapharmacological, millimolar concentrations, in contrast to the clinically relevant plasma concentration range of 50 to 100 µM in patients who take 2 g of metformin daily. Metformin non-competitively inhibits mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase in isolated hepatocytes with a clinically relevant Ki of about 50 µM, which predicts impaired gluconeogenesis only from the redox-dependent substrates lactate and glycerol and not from pyruvate, alanine or dihydroxyacetone phosphate; the importance of the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle in gluconeogenesis in the human liver is unclear, so studies in humans are required.
