Metabolism

Fats

In brief

Fat in food is split in the gut, rebuilt in the gut wall and carried in the blood inside particles of fat and protein called lipoproteins. Cells burn fatty acids inside mitochondria; liver and fat cells also make them from carbohydrate, and a molecule formed during this synthesis slows the entry of fatty acids into mitochondria. Two fatty acids, linoleic and α-linolenic acid, cannot be made in the body and must come from food.

Digestion and absorption

In the small intestine, enzymes from the pancreas split fat into fatty acids and monoacylglycerol, which the gut lining rebuilds into fat 12.

Pancreatic lipase prefers the sn-1 and sn-3 esters of triacylglycerol; colipase anchors it to the lipid–water interface against displacement by bile salts 2. In enterocytes, MGAT2 and DGAT1, both highly expressed in the intestine, resynthesise triacylglycerol 3.

Lipoproteins

Fat does not dissolve in blood, so it travels in lipoproteins, particles of fat coated with phospholipid, cholesterol and proteins 1.

Enterocytes secrete chylomicrons into lymph, one apoB-48 per particle; the liver secretes VLDL, one apoB-100 per particle 1. Lipoprotein lipase, anchored to capillary endothelium by GPIHBP1, hydrolyses their triacylglycerol; apoC-II activates it and apoC-III inhibits it 14. Loss-of-function variants in LPL, APOC2, GPIHBP1, LMF1 or APOA5 can cause familial chylomicronaemia syndrome 1.

The liver takes up chylomicron remnants and about half of VLDL remnants through apoE; hepatic lipase converts the rest to LDL 1. Hepatic LDL receptors clear about 70% of LDL, and statins increase their number 1. HDL forms as ABCA1 loads apoA-I with cholesterol and phospholipid, and LCAT esterifies that cholesterol 1; see cholesterol.

Carnitine shuttle and β-oxidation

Cells burn fatty acids inside mitochondria, two carbon atoms at a time 5.

CPT1 on the outer mitochondrial membrane transfers long-chain acyl groups from CoA to carnitine; SLC25A20 carries acylcarnitine into the matrix, and CPT2 restores acyl-CoA there 5. In each turn of β-oxidation, the acyl-CoA is dehydrogenated with FAD, hydrated, dehydrogenated with NAD+ and cleaved with CoA, releasing acetyl-CoA; palmitoyl-CoA takes seven turns 5.

CPT1A deficiency causes hypoketotic hypoglycaemia after fasting; myopathic CPT2 deficiency causes myoglobinuria with prolonged exercise or fasting 4.

Fatty acid synthesis and malonyl-CoA

The liver and fat tissue can make fatty acids from carbohydrate 5.

Mitochondrial acetyl-CoA reaches the cytosol as citrate, which ATP-citrate lyase cleaves 5. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase, a biotin enzyme activated by citrate and inactivated by AMPK phosphorylation, forms malonyl-CoA 4. Fatty acid synthase joins one acetyl-CoA and seven malonyl-CoA into palmitate, using 14 NADPH 5. Unlike β-oxidation, synthesis is cytosolic, uses an acyl-carrier domain and NADPH, and passes through (3R)- instead of (3S)-hydroxyacyl intermediates 4.

Malonyl-CoA also inhibits CPT1, which limits simultaneous synthesis and oxidation 56. In rat liver homogenates, McGarry and colleagues found in 1977 that malonyl-CoA inhibited ketogenesis from oleate but not from octanoate, which needs no carnitine 7. In young men, fructose or sucrose drinks for 7 weeks raised hepatic de novo lipogenesis 8; see sugar and fructose.

Essential fatty acids

The body cannot make linoleic acid (omega-6) or α-linolenic acid (omega-3), so both must come from food 9.

SCD desaturates at Δ9, FADS2 at Δ6 and FADS1 at Δ5; humans lack Δ12- and Δ15-desaturases 2109. Desaturases and elongases convert linoleic acid to arachidonic acid and α-linolenic acid to EPA, and one peroxisomal β-oxidation cycle completes DHA 10; see palm and seed oils and fish and omega-3. When linoleic acid is scarce, the same enzymes convert oleic acid to 5,8,11-eicosatrienoic acid 11. In four healthy men infused intravenously for 2 weeks with fat-free glucose and amino acids, linoleic acid fell from 48.8% to 9.8% of plasma cholesteryl-ester fatty acids, and eicosatrienoic acid appeared 11.

Consensus statements

The European Food Safety Authority set adequate intakes of 4% of energy for linoleic acid and 0.5% for α-linolenic acid 9.

Unresolved questions

How fatty acids and monoacylglycerols enter enterocytes is unsettled: lack of CD36 in humans and mice, or of FATP4 in mice, causes no fat malabsorption 1. So-called fatty acid transport proteins carry acyl-CoA synthetase motifs; their transport function is unproven 5. How hepatocytes secrete VLDL is unresolved 1.

    Finding
    Textbook chapter, updated 14 January 2024, read in the chapter XML of the NCBI Bookshelf open-access archive of Endotext. Lipids are insoluble in water and travel in lipoproteins, with a core of cholesteryl esters and triglycerides inside a surface of phospholipids, free cholesterol and apolipoproteins. Intestinal lipases hydrolyse dietary triglycerides to fatty acids and monoacylglycerol, which enterocytes resynthesise into triglycerides with MGAT and DGAT. Each chylomicron, made by the intestine, carries one apoB-48; each VLDL, made by the liver, and each IDL and LDL particle carries one apoB-100. MTTP transfers triglycerides to apoB-100 in the liver and to apoB-48 in the intestine; its absence prevents chylomicron formation (abetalipoproteinaemia). Chylomicrons are secreted into lymph and reach the systemic circulation through the thoracic duct. Triglyceride availability is the primary determinant of VLDL secretion. LPL, synthesised in muscle, heart and adipose tissue, is anchored to capillary endothelium by GPIHBP1 and hydrolyses the triglycerides of chylomicrons and VLDL; apoC-II is its cofactor, and apoC-III inhibits it. Loss-of-function mutations in LPL, APOC2, GPIHBP1, LMF1 and APOA5 can cause familial chylomicronaemia syndrome. The liver clears chylomicron remnants through apoE binding to the LDL receptor, LRP and syndecan-4, and clears about 50% (variable) of IDL; hepatic lipase hydrolyses the triglycerides of the remaining IDL, forming LDL. About 70% of circulating LDL is cleared by hepatocyte LDL receptors; statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bile acid sequestrants and bempedoic acid lower plasma LDL by increasing hepatic LDL receptors. Nascent HDL forms when ABCA1 effluxes cholesterol and phospholipids to lipid-poor apoA-I; LCAT, activated by apoA-I, esterifies HDL cholesterol. The pathway of fatty acid absorption is not well understood: humans and mice deficient in CD36 do not have fat malabsorption, mice deficient in FATP4 have no abnormality of fat absorption, and the pathways of monoacylglycerol uptake remain to be defined. The precise pathway of VLDL secretion from hepatocytes is not resolved.

Total ketone bodies in healthy adults oscillate between about 100 and 250 µM, rise to about 1 mM after 24 h of fasting or prolonged exercise and reach as high as 20 mM in diabetic ketoacidosis; the human liver produces up to 300 g per day. HMGCS2 catalyses the fate-committing condensation of acetoacetyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA; HMGCL releases acetyl-CoA and acetoacetate; BDH1 interconverts acetoacetate and D-3-hydroxybutyrate in a near-equilibrium NAD+/NADH-coupled reaction; acetoacetate decarboxylates spontaneously to acetone; MCT1 and MCT2 (SLC16A1, SLC16A7) release ketone bodies from cells.     Finding
    Malonyl-CoA inhibited ketogenesis from oleic acid but not from octanoic acid; the site of inhibition appeared to be the carnitine acyltransferase I reaction. The effect was specific and easily reversible, and inhibitory concentrations were in the range reported by others in livers of fed rats.     Finding
    Humans lack delta-12 and delta-15 desaturases, so linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid are essential; n-6 and n-3 fatty acids are desaturated and elongated by the same enzymes.     Finding
    In Study I, eight men received a eucaloric, fat-free diet of 80% glucose and 20% casein amino acid hydrolysate by constant 24-h infusion, 14 days intravenously and 14 days nasogastrically, in either order. In the four men fed intravenously first, linoleic acid fell from 48.8% to 9.8% of fatty acids in plasma cholesterol esters, from 21.2% to 3.2% in phospholipids, from 9.6% to 2.0% in free fatty acids and from 14.1% to 2.6% in triglycerides (P < 0.005); 5,8,11-eicosatrienoic acid, absent at baseline, reached 0.6%, 2.5%, 0.2% and 2.3%. Deficiency developed similarly with nasogastric feeding; dermatitis was not detected. In Study II, linoleic acid had fallen by day 3 of continuous feeding and rose during a 24-h fast; repletion with 2.6% of energy as linoleic acid corrected the deficiency by day 13. The authors state that, when linoleic acid is not present in adequate amounts, the same enzyme system elongates and desaturates oleic acid to 5,8,11-eicosatrienoic acid, a reaction competitively inhibited by linoleic acid.
