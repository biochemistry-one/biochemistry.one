Metabolism

Glucose

In brief

Digestion breaks starch and sugars down to glucose, which carrier proteins move from the gut into the blood and then into cells. Inside cells, glycolysis splits glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, and mitochondria burn pyruvate to carbon dioxide with oxygen. Complete breakdown of one glucose molecule yields about 30 molecules of ATP, the cell's energy currency.

Digestion and uptake

Gut enzymes split starch and sugars into single sugars, which carrier proteins move into the blood and then into cells 12.

Amylases cut starch into maltose, maltotriose and limit dextrins; brush-border enzymes release glucose and split lactose and sucrose 1. SGLT1 moves each glucose or galactose into enterocytes with 2 Na+, and GLUT2 releases them into the blood 2.

GLUT1 carries glucose across the blood–brain barrier, GLUT2 serves hepatocytes, and GLUT3 is abundant in brain; insulin moves GLUT4 to the surface of muscle and fat cells 2.

Glycolysis

Glycolysis splits each glucose into two molecules of pyruvate inside the cell, for a net gain of two ATP 1.

Hexokinases, phosphofructokinase-1 and pyruvate kinase catalyse its three physiologically irreversible steps 1. Glucose 6-phosphate inhibits hexokinases; glucokinase, in hepatocytes and β cells, has a higher Km and less product inhibition, and acts as the β-cell glucose sensor 12. GCK variants cause MODY2 and familial hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia 2. AMP, ADP and fructose 2,6-bisphosphate activate phosphofructokinase-1, which commits glucose to glycolysis, and ATP and citrate inhibit it 2; insulin raises and glucagon lowers fructose 2,6-bisphosphate 1. Fructose 1,6-bisphosphate activates the liver and red-cell pyruvate kinase, and PKLR variants cause haemolytic anaemia 12.

Lactate and the Cori cycle

Without oxygen, and in cells without mitochondria such as red blood cells, pyruvate is reduced to lactate, which the liver turns back into glucose 1.

This reduction reoxidises the NADH formed in glycolysis 1. Remaking one glucose by gluconeogenesis costs six high-energy phosphates, against two ATP from glycolysis 1. Blood lactate above 5 mmol/l marks metformin-associated lactic acidosis 3.

Pyruvate dehydrogenase and thiamine

In mitochondria, an enzyme complex that needs vitamin B1 converts pyruvate irreversibly into acetyl-CoA 1456.

E1 decarboxylates pyruvate with thiamine pyrophosphate 42. Four kinases inactivate E1 by phosphorylation, and two phosphatases reactivate it 12; fasting induces PDK4, and dichloroacetate inhibits PDK1–3 2. Thiamine deficiency causes beriberi and Wernicke encephalopathy, the latter associated with alcoholism 51.

Citric acid cycle

The citric acid cycle breaks the acetyl group down to carbon dioxide and passes its electrons to carriers 1.

Each acetyl-CoA yields three NADH, one enzyme-bound FADH2, one GTP or ATP and two CO2 1. ADP activates and NADH inhibits isocitrate dehydrogenase 1; Ca2+ and ADP stimulate 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase, and ATP and NADH restrain it 2.

Oxidative phosphorylation

Mitochondria pass these electrons to oxygen and use the released energy to make most ATP from glucose 1.

Complexes I, III and IV translocate protons out of the matrix; their return drives ATP synthase 7. Measured P/O ratios are 2.5 for NADH and 1.5 for succinate 8. Electrons from cytosolic NADH enter through the malate–aspartate or the glycerol-phosphate shuttle; the latter bypasses complex I 1.

ATP per glucose

Burning one glucose completely yields about 30 ATP 9.

At those ratios, 10 NADH and 2 FADH2 yield 28 ATP; glycolysis and the citric acid cycle add 2 each, 32 in all 1. Glycolytic NADH oxidised through the glycerol-phosphate shuttle yields 1.5 ATP instead of 2.5, which lowers the total to 30 1.

Unresolved questions

Tissue yields remain uncertain 1: the eight-subunit c-ring of ATP synthase predicts P/O ratios of 2.7 and 1.6, against measured 2.5 and 1.5 8, and proton leak uncouples about 20% of mitochondrial oxygen use at rest 10. Apical GLUT2 is disputed: in perfused rat jejunum, glucose recruited GLUT2 to the brush border, a route up to three times larger than SGLT1 11; in mice, apical GLUT2 was not seen after 4 g/kg glucose, and lack of SGLT1 cut intestinal tracer retention by 80% 12.

    US Food and Drug Administration. GLUCOPHAGE (metformin hydrochloride) tablets and GLUCOPHAGE XR extended-release tablets: full prescribing information US Food and Drug Administration, Drugs@FDA, NDA 020357 and NDA 021202, label revised May 2018, 2018.

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    US Food and Drug Administration
    Finding
    Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults and, for the immediate-release tablet, children of 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes. Mechanism of action: decreases hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal absorption of glucose and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake and utilisation; insulin secretion remains unchanged. Pharmacokinetics: absolute bioavailability of a 500 mg tablet under fasting conditions about 50 to 60%; steady-state plasma concentrations generally below 1 µg/mL; apparent volume of distribution 654 ± 358 L after 850 mg; negligible plasma protein binding; excreted unchanged in urine with no hepatic metabolism, no identified human metabolites and no biliary excretion; about 90% of absorbed drug eliminated renally within 24 hours; plasma elimination half-life about 6.2 hours and blood half-life about 17.6 hours; renal clearance about 3.5 times creatinine clearance, so tubular secretion is the major route. Single 850 mg dose: peak concentration 1.86 ± 0.52 µg/mL and renal clearance 384 ± 122 mL/min with creatinine clearance 61 to 90 mL/min, against 4.12 ± 1.83 µg/mL and 108 ± 57 mL/min at 31 to 60 mL/min and 3.93 ± 0.92 µg/mL and 130 ± 90 mL/min at 10 to 30 mL/min. Contraindicated at eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2; initiation not recommended at 30 to 45; benefit and risk assessed if eGFR falls below 45; discontinue below 30. Discontinue at or before iodinated contrast imaging when eGFR is 30 to 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, in liver disease, alcoholism or heart failure, or with intra-arterial contrast, and re-evaluate eGFR after 48 hours. Boxed warning: postmarketing metformin-associated lactic acidosis, characterised by blood lactate above 5 mmol/L, anion gap acidosis without ketonuria or ketonaemia, raised lactate to pyruvate ratio and plasma metformin generally above 5 µg/mL; metformin decreases liver uptake of lactate, raising blood lactate; prompt haemodialysis recommended, metformin being dialysable with a clearance up to 170 mL/min. Lactic acidosis reported in about 32% of overdose cases. Vitamin B12: in 29-week trials a decrease to subnormal levels of previously normal serum vitamin B12 occurred in about 7% of patients, possibly through interference with B12 absorption from the B12-intrinsic factor complex, may be associated with anaemia, and appears rapidly reversible on discontinuation or B12 supplementation; measure haematological parameters annually and B12 every 2 to 3 years. Drug interactions: inhibitors of organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2) and multidrug and toxin extrusion (MATE), among them ranolazine, vandetanib, dolutegravir and cimetidine, may increase systemic exposure; single-dose cimetidine 400 mg with metformin 850 mg gave geometric mean ratios of 1.40 for AUC and 1.61 for peak concentration; carbonic anhydrase inhibitors such as topiramate may increase the risk of lactic acidosis. No clinical study has established conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction. In a 29-week double-blind placebo-controlled US trial in obese patients with type 2 diabetes, fasting plasma glucose changed by −53.0 mg/dL with metformin up to 2550 mg daily (n = 141) and +6.3 mg/dL with placebo (n = 145), and HbA1c by −1.4% and +0.4% (both P = 0.001); mean weight change was −1.4 lb and −2.4 lb. Adverse reactions with metformin against placebo: diarrhoea 53% against 12%, nausea or vomiting 26% against 8%, flatulence 12% against 6%; diarrhoea led to discontinuation in 6%.

    Finding
    Free thiamin is the precursor of thiamin diphosphate (TDP, thiamin pyrophosphate), the coenzyme of pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and branched-chain alpha-keto acid dehydrogenase and of transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway; the three dehydrogenases also require cofactors derived from pantothenic acid, riboflavin and niacin. Thiamin deficiency causes several forms of beriberi, dry (sensory and motor peripheral neuropathy) and wet (with congestive heart failure), and Wernicke's encephalopathy and Korsakoff's syndrome.

    Finding
    Educational review. Insulin, released after eating, binds the insulin receptor, a tyrosine kinase that autophosphorylates and recruits insulin receptor substrate, leading to phosphorylation of AKT; in skeletal muscle, heart and adipose tissue insulin signalling translocates GLUT4 from internal vesicles to the plasma membrane; insulin inhibits hormone-sensitive lipase in adipose tissue; glucagon, released as blood glucose falls, acts mainly on the liver through a G-protein-coupled receptor that raises cAMP.     Watt IN, Montgomery MG, Runswick MJ, Leslie AG, Walker JE. Bioenergetic cost of making an adenosine triphosphate molecule in animal mitochondria Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 2010.

    doi:10.1073/pnas.1011099107PubMed 20847295

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    This work was funded by the Medical Research Council.
    Finding
    In the 3.5 Å crystal structure of the bovine F1-c-ring complex (PDB 2xnd), the c-ring has eight c subunits, so eight protons are translocated per 360° rotation, which produces three ATP: 2.7 protons per ATP. c-Subunit sequences are identical in almost all vertebrates. For each two electrons transferred to oxygen from NADH or succinate, ten or six protons are thought to be pumped out of the matrix; ADP/ATP exchange and phosphate-proton symport add one proton, a cost of 3.7 protons per ATP supplied to the cytoplasm, predicting P/O ratios of 2.7 and 1.6, close to experimental values of 2.5 and 1.5. The proton-motive force generated by respiration drives ATP synthesis.

    Finding
    After a 4 g/kg intragastric glucose bolus, giving about 160 mM luminal glucose, SGLT1-deficient mice retained 80% less glucose tracer in intestinal tissue and had 73% less tracer in plasma than wild-type mice (4 to 5 mice per group), and their GIP and GLP-1 responses were abolished.
