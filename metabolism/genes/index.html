From genes to proteins

In brief

A protein-coding gene is a stretch of DNA whose sequence of four chemical letters spells out a protein. A cell copies the gene into RNA, trims the copy and reads it three letters at a time to link amino acids. Differences in these letters cause inherited diseases such as phenylketonuria and sickle cell disease, and explain why some adults digest milk or flush after drinking alcohol.

DNA and the genome

Each strand of DNA is a chain of four kinds of unit, named by their bases A, C, G and T; in a protein-coding gene, their order specifies the order of amino acids 12.

Two antiparallel strands form a right-handed double helix; adenine pairs with thymine and guanine with cytosine, so each strand’s sequence fixes the other’s 13. Of over 44,400 human genes named by the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee, over 19,250 encode proteins 4.

Industry-funded results

A gapless assembly of one human genome spans 3,054,815,472 base pairs in 22 autosomes and chromosome X; authors included employees of three genomics companies 5.

From gene to protein

A cell copies a gene into RNA, cuts out stretches called introns, and reads the rest three bases at a time to join amino acids 672.

RNA polymerase II extends precursor mRNA at its 3′ end, complementary to the template strand 6. RNGTT adds GMP from GTP to the 5′ end, and RNMT methylates this guanosine at N7 with S-adenosylmethionine 6. The spliceosome excises introns; of 22,489 EST-supported mammalian splice-site pairs, 98.71% were GT–AG 7. Poly(A) polymerase adds the 3′ tail 6.

The 43S pre-initiation complex scans from the cap to the AUG start codon 6. Of 64 codons, 61 specify the 20 standard amino acids and three stop translation 2. eEF1A·GTP delivers aminoacyl-tRNAs, the ribosome forms each peptide bond and eEF2·GTP drives translocation 6.

Variants and single-gene traits

A variant is a difference in DNA sequence between people, from one base to whole exons 8.

About 85% of pathogenic LDLR variants are missense, nonsense, splice-site or small insertion–deletion variants detectable by sequencing; about 15% are larger intragenic deletions or duplications 8.

HBB c.20A>T replaces glutamate 6 of mature β-globin with valine, p.Glu7Val in HGVS numbering 10. In deoxygenated haemoglobin S, valine 6 binds complementary regions of adjacent molecules, forming insoluble polymers that distort red cells; carriers have a survival advantage in childhood malaria 10.

One ALDH2*2 subunit, with lysine for glutamate 504 of the precursor, renders the tetramer nearly inactive 14; about 36% of East Asians flush after alcohol 15.

LCT −13910C>T (rs4988235) is one of at least five independent lactase-persistence variants in an LCT enhancer within intron 13 of MCM6 1316.

Polygenic traits

Inherited differences in height, as in many traits, are spread across very many variants with tiny effects 17.

Genome-wide, the median SNP was associated with 0.14 mm of height, a tenth of the 1.43 mm median for genome-wide significant SNPs 17.

Mitochondrial DNA

Mitochondria have DNA of their own, and its variants pass to children only from the mother 18.

It encodes 13 respiratory-chain subunits, 2 ribosomal RNAs and 22 transfer RNAs 18. Cells hold thousands of copies; where mutant and normal copies coexist, a respiratory-chain defect appears only above a threshold mutant proportion 18.

Epigenetic marks

Cells with identical DNA keep different genes on or off through divisions, partly through chemical marks on DNA and histones 19.

DNMT1 methylates CpG cytosines, preferring hemimethylated DNA, and at replication sites maintains the methylation pattern on the new strand 6.

Unresolved questions

Chromatin is extensively reshaped in germ cells and after fertilisation, though some loci appear to escape; transmission of acquired marks to later human generations is not established 19. Links between grandparents’ food supply and grandchildren’s health await independent replication; most familial methylation defects in rare diseases proved secondary to genetic variants 19.

    Arnold G, Vockley J. Phenylalanine Hydroxylase Deficiency GeneReviews, University of Washington, Seattle, 2025.

    PubMed 20301677

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated in the chapter
    Finding
    Expert-authored, peer-reviewed chapter, first posted 2000, comprehensive update 13 March 2025, revised 20 November 2025. Autosomal recessive; biallelic PAH variants; PAH at 12q23.2. Phenylketonuria denotes untreated plasma phenylalanine above 1,000 umol/L; untreated or late-diagnosed disease causes irreversible intellectual disability, seizures and microcephaly. Defects of tetrahydrobiopterin synthesis or recycling (GCH1, PTS, QDPR, PCBD1) account for about 2% of raised blood phenylalanine. Newborn screening uses dried blood spots collected 24 to 72 hours after birth, with phenylalanine and tyrosine measured by tandem mass spectrometry; biochemical diagnosis requires blood phenylalanine above 120 umol/L and a phenylalanine-to-tyrosine ratio of 3 or more. Treatment: phenylalanine-restricted diet with phenylalanine-free protein supplements based on amino acids or glycomacropeptide; 25% to 50% respond to sapropterin, a synthetic form of tetrahydrobiopterin; pegvaliase, a PEGylated phenylalanine ammonia lyase given subcutaneously, is approved in the US for adults with phenylalanine above 600 umol/L. Aspartame is metabolised in the gut to phenylalanine and aspartate. Maternal phenylalanine consistently above 360 umol/L in pregnancy causes intellectual disability in more than 90% of offspring. The chapter attributes brain toxicity to phenylacetic, phenylpyruvic and phenyllactic acids and to tyrosine and neurotransmitter deficiency.

  10. Textbook or database

    Bender MA, Carlberg K. Sickle Cell Disease GeneReviews, University of Washington, Seattle, 2025.

    PubMed 20301551

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated in the chapter
    Finding
    Expert-authored, peer-reviewed chapter, first posted 15 September 2003, comprehensive update 17 November 2022, revised 13 February 2025. Sickle haemoglobin (HbS) results from HBB c.20A>T, p.Glu6Val in the hemoglobin convention that starts numbering after the initiator methionine (p.Glu7Val in standard HGVS numbering). Sickle cell disease is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner; Hb S/S is the most common form. In deoxygenated HbS, an interaction between Val6 and complementary regions on adjacent molecules forms highly ordered, insoluble polymers that distort red blood cells; HbS polymerises in the T conformation associated with deoxygenation but not in the R conformation. Heterozygotes (Hb A/S, sickle cell trait) are not anaemic, have normal red cell indices and HbS near 40%, and Hb A/S confers a survival advantage in children who contract malaria.

  11. Independent research

    Goldstein JL, Brown MS. A century of cholesterol and coronaries: from plaques to genes to statins Cell, 2015.

    doi:10.1016/j.cell.2015.01.036PubMed 25815993

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    The authors' experimental work over 40 years was supported by grants from the NIH (HL-20948), the Perot Foundation, the Moss Heart Foundation and the O'Donnell Foundation.
    Finding
    Narrative review. Cholesterol is a 27-carbon, four-ring molecule synthesised from acetate, a two-carbon unit attached to CoA; HMG-CoA reductase catalyses the rate-controlling step. An LDL particle contains about 1500 cholesteryl ester molecules in its core and a coat of about 800 phospholipid molecules, about 500 unesterified cholesterol molecules and one 500-kDa apoB. LDL receptors cluster in coated pits; internalised LDL reaches lysosomes, where cholesteryl esters are hydrolysed. The LDLR gene on the short arm of chromosome 19 has 18 exons and encodes a mature protein of 839 amino acids with seven cysteine-rich LDL-binding repeats and a 50-amino-acid cytoplasmic tail that directs it to coated pits. FH heterozygotes have half the functional receptors and about twofold LDL. Nuclear SREBP-2 activates the LDL receptor gene and the cholesterol biosynthetic genes; statins lower ER cholesterol, activate SREBP-2, raise hepatic LDL receptors and lower plasma LDL. Most LDL is removed by hepatic LDL receptors. PCSK9 binds hepatic LDL receptors and disrupts their recycling. African-Americans heterozygous for PCSK9 nonsense mutations Y142X or C679X had 28% lower LDL and 88% fewer heart attacks. Conclusions on human liver are extrapolated from rats, mice, hamsters and dogs.

    Boyle EA, Li YI, Pritchard JK. An Expanded View of Complex Traits: From Polygenic to Omnigenic Cell, 2017.

    Funding and finding

    Finding
    Perspective with new analysis. Fisher showed in 1918 that if many genes affect a trait, random sampling of alleles produces a continuous, normally distributed phenotype. Height illustrates the extreme polygenicity typical of many complex traits: across all SNPs genome-wide, the median SNP is associated with an effect of 0.14 mm on height, about one tenth of the median effect of genome-wide significant SNPs (1.43 mm).     PubMed 20301403

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated in the chapter
    Finding
    Expert-authored, peer-reviewed overview, posted 8 June 2000, comprehensive update 29 July 2021. More than 70 polypeptides form the respiratory chain on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Mitochondrial DNA encodes 13 essential respiratory-chain subunits (seven of complex I, one of complex III, three of complex IV and two of complex V), two ribosomal RNAs (12S and 16S) and 22 transfer RNAs for intra-mitochondrial protein synthesis; the remaining respiratory-chain polypeptides and the proteins for respiratory-chain assembly, mitochondrial structure and mtDNA maintenance and expression are encoded by the nuclear genome. Each human cell contains thousands of copies of mtDNA; in heteroplasmy, mutated and wild-type mtDNA coexist, and the proportion of mutated mtDNA must exceed a critical threshold before a cell expresses a biochemical defect of the respiratory chain. mtDNA pathogenic variants are transmitted by maternal inheritance; offspring of males with an mtDNA pathogenic variant do not inherit it.

    Horsthemke B. A critical view on transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in humans Nature Communications, 2018.

    doi:10.1038/s41467-018-05445-5PubMed 30061690

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Work in the author's laboratory has been funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft.
    Finding
    Comment. Within a cell lineage, gene activity states are maintained over cell divisions; this cellular inheritance is attributed to regulatory feedback loops, chromatin modifications (DNA methylation and histone modifications) and long-lived non-coding RNAs. Chromatin is extensively reshaped during germ-cell differentiation and in totipotent cells after fertilisation. Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in humans is a matter of controversial debate because genetic, ecological and cultural inheritance confound it; epidemiological links between grandparental food supply and grandchildren's health await independent replication; most families in which abnormal DNA methylation segregated with a rare disease proved to carry an underlying genetic variant; the excess cardiovascular and metabolic disease after prenatal exposure to the Dutch hunger winter is a direct consequence of exposure in utero, not germline transmission. The author concludes that the evidence is not (yet) conclusive.
