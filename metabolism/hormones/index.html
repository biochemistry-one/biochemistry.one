Metabolism

Hormones and signalling

In brief

Hormones are molecules released into the blood that change the activity of cells with a matching receptor. Some receptors lie in the cell membrane and act through small messenger molecules inside the cell; others lie inside the cell and switch genes on or off. Many hormones suppress their own release through feedback loops.

Receptors and second messengers

Hormones act through receptors in the cell membrane or inside the cell 12.

The insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase: its β subunits phosphorylate each other and then IRS proteins, which recruit phosphoinositide 3-kinase 3. Its product, the lipid second messenger PIP3, activates PDK1 and AKT 34.

G-protein-coupled receptors, the largest family of membrane proteins, cross the membrane seven times 1. The receptors for glucagon, GLP-1 and TSH and the β-adrenoceptors stimulate adenylyl cyclase, which forms cyclic AMP (cAMP) 45. cAMP activates protein kinase A, and phosphodiesterases degrade it 13. The ghrelin receptor activates Gq and phospholipase C, which forms diacylglycerol and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate; the latter opens endoplasmic reticulum calcium channels 4.

Cortisol and T3 bind nuclear receptors, ligand-dependent transcription factors 24. T3 binding to its receptor, which pairs with RXR on DNA, replaces a corepressor with a coactivator 6. The leptin receptor, a type I cytokine receptor, signals through the kinase JAK2 and STAT3 4.

Insulin and glucagon

Insulin, made by beta cells of the pancreas, lowers blood sugar; glucagon, made by alpha cells, raises it 4.

High glucose and insulin inhibit glucagon release, and low glucose stimulates it 4. In muscle, AKT promotes translocation of the glucose transporter GLUT4 to the plasma membrane 34. In hepatocytes, glucagon raises cAMP; protein kinase A then inactivates glycogen synthase and, through phosphorylase kinase, activates glycogen phosphorylase 7. Metformin acts mainly by suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis 7.

Adrenaline and cortisol

Adrenaline quickens the heart and widens the airways, and the adrenal cortex releases cortisol daily and under stress 452.

β1-Adrenoceptors stimulate heart muscle contraction, and β2-adrenoceptors relax airway smooth muscle through cAMP 45.

β2-selective agonists such as salbutamol followed nonselective ones such as adrenaline, which caused tachycardia and tremor 5. In asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with reversible obstruction, cardioselective beta-blockers given for 3 to 28 days left forced expiratory volume in one second unchanged (−0.42% of baseline, 95% CI −3.74 to 2.91), although a single dose lowered it by 7.46% (5.59 to 9.32) against placebo 8.

CRH from the hypothalamus stimulates ACTH secretion by the anterior pituitary, ACTH stimulates glucocorticoid production, and glucocorticoids inhibit both in negative feedback 2.

Thyroid hormones

The thyroid gland makes iodine-containing hormones; TSH, a pituitary hormone, stimulates their production, and they suppress TSH 6.

T4 is a prohormone; humans derive circulating T3 mainly from type 2 deiodinase 6. Thyroid hormone receptor β mediates the feedback: a boy and his father, heterozygous for a variant that abolished its T3 binding, had serum T4 of 24.7 and 17.9 µg/dl (normal 5.0 to 12.0) with normal TSH 96.

Leptin, ghrelin and GLP-1

Fat tissue, the stomach and the gut release hormones that adjust appetite and insulin secretion 4.

Leptin from adipose tissue reduces food intake through the hypothalamus; inherited lack of leptin or its receptor causes severe obesity from an early age 4. Ghrelin from the stomach stimulates appetite and requires octanoylation by MBOAT4 to activate its receptor 4. GLP-1 from intestinal L cells stimulates glucose-dependent insulin release and lowers plasma glucagon 4.

Industry-funded results

In a before-and-after study partly funded by Bristol-Myers Squibb, maker of Glucophage (metformin), 3 months of metformin in seven patients with type 2 diabetes lowered glucose production from 0.70 to 0.53 and gluconeogenesis, measured with deuterated water, from 0.42 to 0.28 mmol·m⁻²·min⁻¹ 10.

Consensus statements

The American Thyroid Association recommends levothyroxine for hypothyroidism, with TSH kept within the reference range of 0.4 to 4.0 mIU/L and measured 4 to 6 weeks after each dose change 11.

Unresolved questions

Metformin’s molecular target remains unsettled: it suppressed glucose production in mouse hepatocytes lacking AMPK; some reports found complex I inhibition only at millimolar concentrations, against 50 to 100 µM in plasma; and mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase inhibition lacks human data 7.

    Finding
    Review. Glucocorticoids are synthesised and released by the adrenal cortex in a circadian manner and in response to stress under control of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis: CRH from the hypothalamus stimulates synthesis and secretion of ACTH by the anterior pituitary, ACTH stimulates production and secretion of glucocorticoids, and glucocorticoids act on the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary to inhibit production and release of CRH and ACTH in a classic negative feedback loop.     Narrative review. INSR is a heterotetramer of two insulin-binding alpha subunits and two beta subunits with tyrosine kinase domains; trans-autophosphorylation of Tyr1162, Tyr1158 and Tyr1163 activates it, and it phosphorylates IRS1 and IRS2, which recruit PI3K (p85 regulatory, p110 catalytic). PIP3 recruits PDK1 and AKT, phosphorylated at Thr308 by PDK1 and at Ser473 by mTORC2. In primary human myotubes, knockdown of AKT2 but not AKT1 abrogated insulin-stimulated glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis; a partial loss-of-function AKT2 variant in about 1% of the Finnish population impairs insulin-stimulated glucose uptake and increases endogenous glucose production. AKT phosphorylation of TBC1D4 (AS160) blocks its inactivation of Rab GTPases and promotes GLUT4 storage-vesicle translocation, first reported in 1980; a truncating TBC1D4 mutation caused profound insulin resistance in one family. Glycogen synthesis accounts for about 75% of insulin-stimulated glucose disposal in human muscle; mice with GSK3 insensitive to insulin have normal insulin-stimulated glycogen synthesis, suggesting acute control mainly by glucose 6-phosphate allostery. Muscle stores energy mostly for its own use, releasing lactate and alanine. Hepatocyte glucose transport is not insulin-regulated; AKT phosphorylates FOXO1, excluding it from the nucleus and lowering G6pc and Pck1 transcription. Suppression of adipose lipolysis has an ED50 of about 20 µU/mL insulin, within a physiological range of about 5 to 60 µU/mL. Hepatic glycogen is nearly depleted after 48 h of fasting in humans, while gluconeogenic flux stays relatively constant for about 48 h. In rodents, acute insulin suppression of gluconeogenesis depends mainly on suppression of adipose lipolysis, hepatic acetyl-CoA and pyruvate carboxylase flux, and mice lacking hepatic AKT1, AKT2 and FOXO1 suppressed glucose production normally; the quantitative significance in humans remains to be explored. Insulin suppresses glucagon secretion through paracrine action in the islet.

    Billington CK, Penn RB, Hall IP. β2 Agonists Handbook of Experimental Pharmacology, 2017.

    doi:10.1007/164_2016_64PubMed 27878470

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    The authors are funded by grants HL58506 and A110007 (RBP) and MRC grant G1000861 (IPH).
    Finding
    Beta2 adrenoceptors are expressed on airway smooth muscle and also on airway epithelial cells, submucosal glands, vascular endothelium, vascular smooth muscle, mast cells, macrophages and eosinophils; the main adverse effects of the class, among them anxiety, tachycardia, tremor and sweating, are ascribed to cross-activation of beta1 adrenoceptors, and the accumulated evidence does not indicate anti-inflammatory effects of current therapeutic beta2 agonists. Loss of the bronchoprotective effect with chronic use has been observed in patients, consistent with receptor desensitisation in cell, tissue and mouse models. Salbutamol, the first β2-selective agonist, was synthesised in 1968 at Glaxo. Inhaled doses are 100-200 µg as required up to four times per day, with onset under 5 min, duration of effect 3-6 h and β2/β1 selectivity 27, against 63 for terbutaline, 150 for formoterol and 3000 for salmeterol. The β2 adrenoceptor was the first G-protein-coupled receptor to be cloned, has seven transmembrane domains and couples to Gs; adenylyl cyclase then catalyses formation of cAMP from ATP and cAMP activates protein kinase A, whose substrates in airway smooth muscle include myosin light chain kinase, KCa channels, heat shock protein 20, Gq-coupled receptors, Gq, phospholipase C and IP3 receptors. G-protein-coupled receptor kinases phosphorylate the receptor and promote β-arrestin binding, which uncouples it from Gs and diminishes cAMP production. Of the four coding variants of ADRB2, Arg16Gly and Gln27Glu are common, with allele frequencies between 0.3 and 0.7 and in linkage disequilibrium; Thr164Ile has an allele frequency of about 2% and alters catechol ligand binding and receptor trafficking in transfected cells; Val34Met is very rare and appears not to alter receptor function. Several studies found that frequent or regular administration of short-acting agonists impairs asthma control in Arg16Arg homozygotes, while most larger retrospective and prospective studies did not confirm these results and large genome-wide association studies of asthma identified no signal at the ADRB2 locus. Short-acting agonists are recommended on an as-required rather than a regular basis, and escalation of use should prompt review of management; widespread use of genetic profiles to dictate treatment with this drug class does not seem warranted.

  6. Independent research

    Brent GA. Mechanisms of thyroid hormone action Journal of Clinical Investigation, 2012.

    doi:10.1172/JCI60047PubMed 22945636

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    This work was supported by Veterans Affairs Merit Review Funds and NIH grant R01 CA89364.
    Finding
    Review. Thyroid hormone is synthesised in thyroid follicles by iodination of tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin; TSH, secreted by the anterior pituitary in response to feedback from circulating thyroid hormone, acts on the TSH receptor of follicular cells and regulates iodide uptake by the sodium/iodide symporter. T4 is a prohormone activated to T3; humans derive circulating T3 primarily from type 2 deiodinase and rodents from type 1; type 3 deiodinase inactivates T4 to reverse T3; selenium is required by all three deiodinases. Unliganded thyroid hormone receptor heterodimerises with RXR, binds a thyroid hormone response element and then a corepressor such as NCoR or SMRT; T3 binding disrupts corepressor binding and promotes coactivator binding. TR-beta mediates thyroid hormone feedback to TRH and TSH; resistance to thyroid hormone, caused by TR-beta mutations, features elevated circulating thyroid hormone and nonsuppressed serum TSH.

  7. Independent research

    Petersen MC, Vatner DF, Shulman GI. Regulation of hepatic glucose metabolism in health and disease Nature Reviews Endocrinology, 2017.

    doi:10.1038/nrendo.2017.80PubMed 28731034

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    M.C.P. acknowledges grant support from the US National Institutes of Health (NIH; grants F30 DK-104596 and T32 GM-007205). D.F.V. acknowledges grant support from the NIH (grant K23 DK-102874). G.I.S. acknowledges grant support from the NIH (grants R01 DK40936, R01 DK113984 and P30 DK045735).
    Finding
    Narrative review. Glucagon receptor (GCGR) activation raises cAMP and activates PKA, which inhibits L-type pyruvate kinase, phosphorylates PFK-2/FBPase-2 to favour its phosphatase activity and lower fructose 2,6-bisphosphate, an allosteric inhibitor of FBPase-1 (printed as fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase 1), phosphorylates and inactivates glycogen synthase, and activates phosphorylase kinase, which phosphorylates glycogen phosphorylase at Ser15; PKA also activates the CREB-CBP-CRTC2 complex for gluconeogenic gene transcription. FOXO proteins with PGC1-alpha raise PCK1 and G6PC expression and are excluded from the nucleus after AKT phosphorylation. Glucose enters hepatocytes by facilitated diffusion through GLUT2; glucose allosterically inhibits glycogen phosphorylase; glucose 6-phosphate is the key allosteric activator of GYS2. In overnight-fasted humans under somatostatin, hyperinsulinaemia was required to stimulate hepatic glycogen synthesis and hyperglycaemia to suppress glycogenolysis. Hepatic glucose production and storage are dysregulated in type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, and this imbalance contributes to hyperglycaemia in the fasted and postprandial states. Metformin is used as first-line therapy for type 2 diabetes and acts primarily through the suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis, by a molecular mechanism that remains under investigation. Several lines of evidence cast doubt on the necessity of AMPK: metformin continues to inhibit glucose production in Ampk-knockout primary mouse hepatocytes and improves glucose tolerance in Ampk-knockout mice to a similar extent as in wild-type mice. Inhibition of mitochondrial complex I is observed only at suprapharmacological, millimolar concentrations, in contrast to the clinically relevant plasma concentration range of 50 to 100 µM in patients who take 2 g of metformin daily. Metformin non-competitively inhibits mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase in isolated hepatocytes with a clinically relevant Ki of about 50 µM, which predicts impaired gluconeogenesis only from the redox-dependent substrates lactate and glycerol and not from pyruvate, alanine or dihydroxyacetone phosphate; the importance of the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle in gluconeogenesis in the human liver is unclear, so studies in humans are required.

    Salpeter S, Ormiston T, Salpeter E. Cardioselective beta-blockers for reversible airway disease Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2002.

    doi:10.1002/14651858.CD002992PubMed 12519582

    Funding and finding
    Participants
    381
    Follow-up
    single dose, or continued treatment for 3 to 28 days
    Funding
    Internal sources: Santa Clara Valley Medical Center, USA. External sources: No sources of support supplied.
    Finding
    Issue 4, 2002, of this Cochrane review: 19 trials of a single dose and 10 of continued treatment, all randomised, blinded and placebo-controlled. A single dose of a cardioselective beta-blocker reduced the forced expiratory volume in one second by 7.46% (95% CI 5.59 to 9.32), while the response to a beta2-agonist given afterwards rose by 4.63% (2.47 to 6.78) against placebo. Treatment lasting 3 to 28 days produced no change in that volume (-0.42% of baseline, 95% CI -3.74 to 2.91), in symptoms or in inhaler use, and a beta2-agonist response of 8.74% (1.96 to 15.52) persisted. In the subgroup with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease the treatment effect on that volume was -5.28% (-10.03 to -0.54) for single doses and 1.07% (-3.30 to 5.44) for continued treatment. The full text in PubMed Central carries the same effect estimates after a search to June 2011 that added no eligible study, and reports 240 patients in the single-dose trials and 141 in the continued-treatment trials, a risk difference for respiratory symptoms of 0.01 (-0.02 to 0.03) and 0.01 (-0.02 to 0.04) respectively, and cardioselectivity of at least 20-fold for beta1 over beta2 receptors.

    Jonklaas J, Bianco AC, Bauer AJ, Burman KD, Cappola AR, Celi FS, et al. Guidelines for the treatment of hypothyroidism: prepared by the American Thyroid Association Task Force on Thyroid Hormone Replacement Thyroid, 2014.

    doi:10.1089/thy.2014.0028PubMed 25266247

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    The task force members volunteered their time and expertise and did not receive funds from the ATA. No funds were received from commercial sources for the development of this document.     Finding
    Recommendation 1a: levothyroxine is recommended as the preparation of choice for treating hypothyroidism (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence); the rationale is peripheral conversion of the prohormone T4 into T3 by type 1 and type 2 deiodinases. Recommendation 1b: the goals include normalisation of serum thyrotropin; the discussion states that TSH is the most reliable marker of adequate replacement and that a value within the reference range (0.4-4.0 mIU/L) should be considered the therapeutic target. Recommendation 4b: the dose is titrated using serum thyrotropin as the goal, with thyrotropin assessment 4-6 weeks after any dosage change (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence).
