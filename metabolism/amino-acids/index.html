Metabolism

Amino acids and nitrogen

In brief

Proteins in food are broken down into amino acids, and the liver converts the nitrogen of those used for energy into urea. Inherited faults in these steps, such as phenylketonuria, are detected by testing a drop of blood from every newborn baby.

Digestion and absorption

Digestion cuts food proteins into amino acids and chains of two or three, which the small intestine absorbs 12.

The pancreas secretes its proteases as inactive zymogens 1. Mutations in SLC6A19, which encodes the neutral amino acid transporter B0AT1, cause Hartnup disorder 3.

Essential and non-essential amino acids

The body cannot make nine of the twenty amino acids in proteins, so they must come from food 1.

They are histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine 1.

Consensus statements

A joint WHO/FAO/UNU expert consultation puts the safe adult protein intake at 0.83 g/kg body weight per day 4.

Transamination and pyridoxal phosphate

Before most amino acids are broken down, enzymes that depend on a form of vitamin B6 move the amino group onto another molecule 1.

Aminotransferases transfer the α-amino group to 2-oxoglutarate, forming glutamate and the corresponding 2-oxo acid 1. Their cofactor is pyridoxal 5′-phosphate (PLP) 2.

Glutamate dehydrogenase

In mitochondria, one enzyme releases the collected amino groups from glutamate as ammonium 2.

Leucine stimulates insulin secretion in beta cells by allosterically activating this enzyme, GLUD1 5. Eight children with the hyperinsulinism–hyperammonaemia syndrome each had a heterozygous GLUD1 variant in the allosteric domain 5.

The urea cycle

The liver converts ammonium, which is toxic to the brain, into urea, which is excreted 2.

Inherited urea cycle disorders occur in at least 1 in 35,000 to 1 in 51,946 live births 6. Sodium benzoate, sodium phenylacetate and sodium phenylbutyrate allow excretion of nitrogen by alternative pathways 6.

Phenylketonuria and newborn screening

In phenylketonuria, the enzyme that turns phenylalanine into tyrosine is deficient 7.

Untreated, the phenylalanine that accumulates causes lasting brain damage 7. Global prevalence is estimated at 1 in 23,930 live births 8. Newborn screening measures phenylalanine in dried blood spots collected 24 to 72 hours after birth 7. Treatment combines a phenylalanine-restricted diet with phenylalanine-free protein supplements 7. Between 25% and 50% of patients respond to sapropterin, synthetic tetrahydrobiopterin 7. Aspartame is hydrolysed in the gut to phenylalanine 9. Its acceptable daily intake of 40 mg/kg body weight does not apply to people with phenylketonuria 9. When maternal blood phenylalanine stays above 360 µmol/L during pregnancy, more than 90% of the children have intellectual disability 7.

Consensus statements

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics recommends lifelong treatment for everyone with untreated phenylalanine above 360 µmol/L 10.

Industry-funded results

In a 6-week trial sponsored by BioMarin, blood phenylalanine fell by 30% or more in 18 of 41 patients (44%) on sapropterin and 4 of 47 (9%) on placebo 11. The absolute difference was 35 percentage points, and the number needed to treat about 3 11.

Unresolved questions

Arnold and Vockley attribute the brain damage to phenylacetic, phenylpyruvic and phenyllactic acids and to deficiency of tyrosine and catecholamine neurotransmitters 7. The European guideline authors call the precise pathogenesis of brain dysfunction unclear 12.

Carbon skeletons: glucose and ketone bodies

Without its nitrogen, the rest of an amino acid can become glucose or ketone bodies, or be oxidised for energy 1.

Skeletons that yield pyruvate, 2-oxoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, fumarate or oxaloacetate can supply gluconeogenesis 1. From acetyl-CoA and acetoacetyl-CoA, mammals make ketone bodies but no net glucose 1. Only leucine and lysine are exclusively ketogenic 1.

Unresolved questions

The human threonine dehydrogenase gene is an expressed pseudogene, with a premature stop codon in all 20 individuals sequenced 13. Yet in six men on a control diet, conversion to glycine, the product of the dehydrogenase route, accounted for 10% of threonine catabolism 14.

    doi:10.1038/ng1405PubMed 15286787

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Grants-in-aid from the Japanese Government to A.K. and Swiss National Science Foundation grants to F.V.
    Finding
    Hartnup disorder, autosomal recessive, results from impaired neutral amino acid transport across epithelial cells of renal proximal tubules and intestinal mucosa; symptoms include transient pellagra-like rashes, cerebellar ataxia and psychosis. Mutations in SLC6A19 were identified in the original 1956 family and in three Japanese families; its product B0AT1 is expressed mainly in intestine and renal proximal tubule and functions as a neutral amino acid transporter.

    Simpson KL, MacLeod EL, Kakajiwala A, Gropman AL, Ah Mew N. Urea Cycle Disorders Overview GeneReviews, University of Washington, Seattle, 2025.

    PubMed 20301396

    Funding and finding
    Funding

    Finding
    Expert-authored, peer-reviewed chapter, first posted 2003, comprehensive update 3 July 2025. The urea cycle comprises CPS1, OTC, ASS1, ASL and ARG1, the cofactor-producing enzyme NAGS and the transporters ORNT1 (SLC25A15) and citrin (SLC25A13); CPS1, NAGS and OTC act in mitochondria, ASS1, ASL and ARG1 in the cytosol.   7. Textbook or database

    Arnold G, Vockley J. Phenylalanine Hydroxylase Deficiency GeneReviews, University of Washington, Seattle, 2025.

    PubMed 20301677

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated in the chapter
    Finding
    Expert-authored, peer-reviewed chapter, first posted 2000, comprehensive update 13 March 2025, revised 20 November 2025. Autosomal recessive; biallelic PAH variants; PAH at 12q23.2. Phenylketonuria denotes untreated plasma phenylalanine above 1,000 umol/L; untreated or late-diagnosed disease causes irreversible intellectual disability, seizures and microcephaly. Defects of tetrahydrobiopterin synthesis or recycling (GCH1, PTS, QDPR, PCBD1) account for about 2% of raised blood phenylalanine. Newborn screening uses dried blood spots collected 24 to 72 hours after birth, with phenylalanine and tyrosine measured by tandem mass spectrometry; biochemical diagnosis requires blood phenylalanine above 120 umol/L and a phenylalanine-to-tyrosine ratio of 3 or more. Treatment: phenylalanine-restricted diet with phenylalanine-free protein supplements based on amino acids or glycomacropeptide; 25% to 50% respond to sapropterin, a synthetic form of tetrahydrobiopterin; pegvaliase, a PEGylated phenylalanine ammonia lyase given subcutaneously, is approved in the US for adults with phenylalanine above 600 umol/L. Aspartame is metabolised in the gut to phenylalanine and aspartate. Maternal phenylalanine consistently above 360 umol/L in pregnancy causes intellectual disability in more than 90% of offspring. The chapter attributes brain toxicity to phenylacetic, phenylpyruvic and phenyllactic acids and to tyrosine and neurotransmitter deficiency.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2013.3496

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority; on request from the European Commission (question number EFSA-Q-2011-00406).
    Finding
    Aspartame is rapidly and completely hydrolysed in the gastrointestinal tract to phenylalanine, aspartic acid and methanol. Developmental toxicity in animals was attributed to phenylalanine.     Smith WE, Berry SA, Bloom K, Brown C, Burton BK, Demarest OM, et al. Phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency diagnosis and management: A 2023 evidence-based clinical guideline of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) Genetics in Medicine, 2025.

    Treatment should be lifelong for individuals with untreated phenylalanine above 360 umol/L, and phenylalanine should be maintained at or below 360 umol/L for life (strong recommendations, high certainty).     van Wegberg AMJ, MacDonald A, Ahring K, Bélanger-Quintana A, Blau N, Bosch AM, et al. The complete European guidelines on phenylketonuria: diagnosis and treatment Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases, 2017.

    doi:10.1186/s13023-017-0685-2PubMed 29025426

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    A grant from the European Society for Phenylketonuria and Allied Disorders (ESPKU) funded a project assistant; the ESPKU had no opportunity to influence the writing process. One group meeting and one plenary meeting were supported by Excemed.
    Finding
    Treat untreated blood phenylalanine above 600 umol/L; treat 360 to 600 umol/L during the first 12 years of life; patients aged 12 years or more with untreated phenylalanine below 600 umol/L do not require treatment; upper target 360 umol/L before 12 years and 600 umol/L from 12 years, lower target 120 umol/L; treatment for life recommended.   13. Funding unknown

    Edgar AJ. The human L-threonine 3-dehydrogenase gene is an expressed pseudogene BMC Genetics, 2002.

    doi:10.1186/1471-2156-3-18PubMed 12361482

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated
    Finding
    Threonine is degraded either by L-threonine 3-dehydrogenase to 2-amino-3-ketobutyrate, which with CoA yields glycine and acetyl-CoA, or by serine/threonine dehydratase to NH4+ and 2-ketobutyrate. The human TDH gene at 8p23-22 has a premature stop codon in exon 6 (codon of arginine-214) and lacks the splice acceptor before exon 6 in all individuals examined (23 genotyped, 20 sequenced); its transcripts encode truncated proteins of 157 to 230 residues. The author suggests that the dehydratase pathway may be the only route of threonine catabolism in humans, asks which enzyme forms the small fraction of glycine from threonine, and notes that absence of threonine dehydrogenase activity in human liver has not been demonstrated directly.
