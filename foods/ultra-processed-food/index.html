Foods

Ultra-processed food

In brief

Ultra-processed foods are industrial products made mostly from substances extracted from foods and combined with additives, such as soft drinks, packaged snacks and ready meals. In two feeding trials, adults gained weight or lost less weight on them than on less processed diets, and observational studies link high intake with disease and earlier death, but the responsible properties remain unknown.

What it is

Ultra-processed foods are industrial products made mostly from substances extracted or derived from foods and combined with additives 12.

The NOVA system sorts foods into four groups by processing rather than nutrient content 3. Group 4 covers products containing a substance never or rarely used in kitchens, or an additive used to make them palatable or more appealing 1. In a US national survey, ultra-processed foods supplied 57.5% of energy intake 4.

What happens in the body

In the NIH inpatient trial, people ate ultra-processed meals faster than unprocessed meals 3. Eating rate was 17 ± 1 kcal/min faster 3. Energy density, drinks excluded, was 1.957 against 1.057 kcal/g 3.

Colonic bacteria ferment undigested polysaccharides to butyrate 5. Butyrate is the main energy source of colonocytes 5.

In the US survey, fibre fell from 9.6 to 6.7 g per 1000 kcal between the lowest and highest fifths of ultra-processed share 4.

What studies show

In two independent feeding trials, adults gained weight or lost less on ultra-processed diets than on less processed diets 36.

In the NIH trial, 20 adults ate each diet ad libitum for 14 days 3. Meals were matched for presented energy, macronutrients, sugars, sodium and fibre 3. Energy intake was 508 ± 106 kcal/day higher on the ultra-processed diet 3. Weight changed by +0.9 ± 0.3 kg on it and −0.9 ± 0.3 kg on the unprocessed diet 3.

In the UPDATE trial, 55 adults received each diet for 8 weeks 6. Both diets followed the UK Eatwell Guide 6. Weight changed by −2.06% on the minimally processed diet and −1.05% on the ultra-processed diet 6. The difference was −1.01 percentage points, or −0.96 kg 6.

An umbrella review covered 45 pooled observational analyses 2. Convincing associations included those with cardiovascular mortality (risk ratio 1.50) and type 2 diabetes (1.12 per 10% increment of intake) 2. By GRADE, 4 analyses were of moderate, 22 of low and 19 of very low quality 2.

In US cohorts, 114,064 health professionals were followed for a median of 31 to 34 years 7. All-cause mortality was 1536 deaths per 100,000 person-years in the highest quarter of intake and 1472 in the lowest 7. The difference was 64 per 100,000 person-years (hazard ratio 1.04, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.07) 7.

Industry-funded results

In a crossover trial, 41 adults ate two ultra-processed diets of different texture 8. The diets were matched for palatability and non-beverage energy density 8. Daily energy intake was 369 kcal lower on the diet eaten more slowly 8. Body weight did not differ 8.

Consensus statements

A 2026 European Society of Cardiology consensus statement advises cardiologists to assess ultra-processed food intake 9. The 2025 US Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee graded as limited the evidence linking diets higher in ultra-processed food with greater adiposity 10.

Unresolved questions

Independent analyses conflict on whether processing adds risk beyond diet quality 72. In the US cohorts, adjustment for diet quality attenuated the mortality association toward null 7. In a meta-analysis cited by the umbrella review, adjustment for diet quality left the associations unchanged 2.

In a meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials, the standardised mean difference in energy intake was 0.71 with more energy-dense ultra-processed diets and 0.02 with similar energy density 11. Randomised trials of reduced intake on hard cardiovascular endpoints are lacking 9.

    Finding
    Commentary by the authors of NOVA. Ultra-processed foods are formulations of ingredients, mostly of exclusive industrial use, that result from a series of industrial processes: fractioning of whole foods into substances, chemical modification of these substances, assembly of modified and unmodified food substances, frequent use of cosmetic additives and sophisticated packaging. A product is identified as ultra-processed when its ingredient list contains at least one food substance never or rarely used in kitchens (such as high-fructose corn syrup, hydrogenated or interesterified oils, hydrolysed proteins) or a class of additive designed to make the product palatable or more appealing (flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, emulsifying salts, sweeteners, thickeners, and anti-foaming, bulking, carbonating, foaming, gelling and glazing agents).

  5. Independent research

    Vital M, Howe AC, Tiedje JM. Revealing the bacterial butyrate synthesis pathways by analyzing (meta)genomic data mBio, 2014.

    doi:10.1128/mBio.00889-14PubMed 24757212

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Financial support was provided by the NIH Human Microbiome Project Demonstration Project (UH3 DK083993).
    Finding
    225 genomes carried a butyrate synthesis pathway. Four pathways (acetyl-CoA, glutarate, 4-aminobutyrate, lysine) converge on the conversion of crotonyl-CoA to butyryl-CoA by the butyryl-CoA dehydrogenase–electron-transferring flavoprotein complex, a step postulated to conserve energy through ferredoxin reduction. In the acetyl-CoA pathway, thiolase, β-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydrogenase and crotonase precede this step, and butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase or phosphate butyryltransferase with butyrate kinase forms butyrate. In stool metagenomes the acetyl-CoA pathway made up a mean 79.7% of butyrate pathways; for that pathway the terminal genes but and buk had median abundances of 77.2% and 21.8%. Butyrate is described as the main energy source for colonocytes.

  9. Expert consensus

    Guasti L, Bonaccio M, Abreu A, Asteggiano R, Bes-Rastrollo M, Christodorescu R, et al. Ultra-processed foods, lifestyle management, and cardiovascular diseases: a clinical consensus statement of the European Society of Cardiology Council for Cardiology Practice and the European Association of Preventive Cardiology of the European Society of Cardiology European Heart Journal, 2026.

    doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehag226PubMed 42091095

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Funding: nothing to declare.
    Finding
    Associations of ultra-processed food intake with cardiovascular risk factors, incidence and mortality derive predominantly from observational studies, to be interpreted with caution because residual confounding and reverse causation cannot be fully excluded; trials are scarce, short-term and limited to surrogate outcomes, and trials of reduced intake on hard cardiovascular endpoints are lacking. Proposed non-nutrient mechanisms (additives, processing contaminants, packaging migrants, altered food matrix) support biological plausibility without demonstrating causal effects. General cardiologists are advised to assess intake of ultra-processed food and include it in counselling.

  10. Expert consensus

    2025 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee. Scientific report of the 2025 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee: advisory report to the Secretary of Health and Human Services and Secretary of Agriculture US Department of Health and Human Services and US Department of Agriculture, 2024.

    doi:10.52570/dgac2025

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Federal advisory committee convened under the Federal Advisory Committee Act; HHS chartered the Committee and served as administrative lead, and HHS and USDA support development of the Dietary Guidelines. The Departments proposed the scientific questions and worked with the Committee to refine them.
    Finding
    Systematic review question on dietary patterns with varying amounts of ultra-processed food and growth, body composition and risk of obesity. Children and adolescents, and adults and older adults: patterns with more food classified as ultra-processed are associated with greater adiposity (fat mass, waist circumference, BMI) and greater risk of overweight or obesity (grade limited). Infants and young children, pregnancy and postpartum: grade not assignable. The evidence was difficult to assess because definitions of ultra-processed food varied between studies, many such foods were higher in saturated fat, sodium and added sugars, and food frequency questionnaires do not distinguish processing levels.
