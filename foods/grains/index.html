Foods

Grains, starch and fibre

In brief

Cereal grains consist mostly of starch, which the gut breaks down to glucose, and their outer layers supply fibre, which gut bacteria ferment. In observational studies, people who eat the most fibre have less heart disease, diabetes, bowel cancer and early death, and short trials show small falls in weight and cholesterol. In coeliac disease, an immune reaction to gluten inflames the small intestine of people with particular immune genes.

What it is

Cereal grains consist mostly of starch, made of glucose chains; their outer layers contain most of the fibre 123.

Per 100 g, whole-grain wheat flour contains 57.8 g starch and 10.7 g fibre, white flour 2.7 g fibre 1. Dietary starch is about 25% amylose, a linear α-1,4-glucan, and 75% amylopectin, branched by α-1,6 links 2. Fibre comprises lignin and non-digestible carbohydrates, such as oat β-glucan and retrograded amylose 3.

In the body

Digestive enzymes split starch into glucose; colonic bacteria ferment fibre into short-chain fatty acids 3.

α-Amylase hydrolyses internal α-1,4 links 2. Brush-border maltase-glucoamylase and sucrase-isomaltase release glucose; sucrase-isomaltase supplies almost all α-1,6 activity 2.

Glycaemic index varies with the amylose:amylopectin ratio and with gelatinisation, which makes starch more accessible to digestive enzymes 3. Intact grains, parboiled rice and the protein network of pasta lower it 3.

Colonic bacteria make butyrate from undigested polysaccharides; the acetyl-CoA pathway predominates in stool metagenomes 4.

Acetate and propionate are absorbed as fuels; butyrate is a main fuel of the colonic mucosa 3.

In coeliac disease, an immune response to gluten inflames the small intestine of genetically susceptible people 56.

Most patients carry HLA-DQ2, encoded by DQA1*0501 and DQB1*02; most others carry HLA-DQ8 6. Of 94 Norwegian children with the disease, 93 carried DQA1 and DQB1 genes that may encode DQ2, against 14 of 56 controls 56. Tissue transglutaminase, the main coeliac autoantigen, converts selected gliadin glutamines to glutamic acid 6. Two immunodominant α-gliadin peptides stimulated intestinal T cells only when deamidated, which raised their affinity for DQ2 6.

What studies show

In observational studies, people eating the most fibre had less heart disease, diabetes, colorectal cancer and early death 7.

Highest against lowest fibre intake was associated with an all-cause mortality risk ratio of 0.85 (95% CI 0.79 to 0.91), or 13 fewer deaths per 1000 participants during the studies 7. For coronary heart disease incidence, the risk ratio was 0.76 (0.69 to 0.83), 6 fewer cases per 1000 7. For whole grain, the all-cause mortality risk ratio was 0.81 (0.72 to 0.90), 26 fewer deaths per 1000 7. In trials of at least 4 weeks, more fibre lowered body weight by 0.37 kg and total cholesterol by 0.15 mmol/L 7. WHO commissioned the work and funded it with five New Zealand bodies; a WHO expert group set its questions and confirmed or modified its certainty ratings 7.

Industry-funded results

In a meta-analysis funded by General Mills and co-designed by its employee, weight change with whole grain did not differ significantly from control in 9 trials of 12 to 16 weeks (973 adults) 8. For an oat β-glucan claim by CreaNutrition AG, an unpublished meta-analysis it submitted reported LDL cholesterol −0.28 mmol/L in 18 studies of 2 to 12 weeks 9. EFSA accepted the effect and could have done so without the proprietary studies 9.

Consensus statements

EFSA considers 25 g of fibre a day adequate for normal laxation in adults 3. The European Society for the Study of Coeliac Disease recommends IgA anti-transglutaminase with total IgA as the first test, on a gluten-containing diet 10.

Unresolved questions

The pooled trials measured only risk factors; disease outcomes came from observational studies 7. Independent pooling of 11 whole-grain trials found 0.62 kg lower weight; only 2 of them were in the General Mills-funded analysis 78. In 26 adults, peak 2-hour rises in interstitial glucose after starch-matched rolls with 43.5% or 27.2% amylose were 1.73 and 1.79 mmol/L (P = 0.514) 11.

    Nichols BL, Avery S, Sen P, Swallow DM, Hahn D, Sterchi E. The maltase-glucoamylase gene: common ancestry to sucrase-isomaltase with complementary starch digestion activities Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 2003.

    doi:10.1073/pnas.0237170100PubMed 12547908

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Funded in part with federal funds from the US Department of Agriculture/Agricultural Research Service (Cooperative Agreement 58-6250-1-003 to B.L.N.) and the Swiss National Foundation (Grant 3200-052736.97 to E.S.); a student assistant was funded by a Rank Prize summer studentship.
    Finding
    Dietary starches are about 25% amylose, a linear alpha-1,4 glucan, and 75% amylopectin, whose additional alpha-1,6 links (about 4% of the total) form branches. Alpha-amylase of salivary and pancreatic secretions hydrolyses internal alpha-1,4 links and bypasses alpha-1,6 links, producing linear and branched malto-oligosaccharides, which brush-border maltase-glucoamylase (MGA, gene MGAM) and sucrase-isomaltase (SI) hydrolyse to glucose from the non-reducing ends. SI accounts for 80% of neutral maltase activity, all sucrase and almost all isomaltase activity; MGA accounts for all glucoamylase activity, 1% of isomaltase and 20% of neutral maltase activity. MGA is specific for the 95% alpha-1,4 and SI for the 5% alpha-1,6 glucose links of starch. MGAM spans about 82,000 bp at 7q34 with 48 exons, with an exon structure identical to that of SI.

  3. Regulator

    EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition, and Allergies (NDA). Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for carbohydrates and dietary fibre EFSA Journal, 2010.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1462

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority, on request from the European Commission (Question EFSA-Q-2008-467).
    Finding
    Dietary fibre is defined as non-digestible carbohydrates plus lignin: non-starch polysaccharides (cellulose, hemicelluloses, pectins, hydrocolloids such as gums, mucilages and beta-glucans), resistant oligosaccharides, resistant starch (physically enclosed starch, some raw starch granules, retrograded amylose, modified starches) and associated lignin. In cereals cellulose occurs with hemicelluloses; lignified outer layers are the predominant fibre source in whole-grain products; oats and barley contain high concentrations of water-soluble, viscous beta-glucan. The soluble/insoluble distinction is method-dependent and does not always predict physiological effects. Salivary and pancreatic amylase degrade starch mainly to maltose and oligosaccharides, brush-border disaccharidases release glucose, and glucose is absorbed by the sodium-coupled SGLT1; monosaccharide absorption is regarded as rate-limiting. GI: incremental area under the blood glucose curve over 1.5-3 h after a 50 g carbohydrate portion, as a percentage of the response to the same amount of carbohydrate from glucose or white bread in the same subject; GL = glycaemic carbohydrate x GI/100. Amylose/amylopectin ratio and gelatinisation affect GI; intact cereal grains, parboiled rice and the protein network of pasta lower it. Fibre enters the colon substantially unmodified and is fermented to short-chain fatty acids; acetate and propionate are absorbed and provide energy; butyrate is a main source of energy for the colonic mucosa. Inhibitory effects on iron, zinc and calcium absorption have been attributed to complexing compounds, notably phytic acid in cereals. Wheat bran fibre gives 5 to 6 g faecal bulk per g. Energy factor 8 kJ (2 kcal) per g fibre. Adequate intake: 25 g fibre per day for normal laxation in adults; 2 g per MJ for children from 1 year.

  4. Independent research

    Vital M, Howe AC, Tiedje JM. Revealing the bacterial butyrate synthesis pathways by analyzing (meta)genomic data mBio, 2014.

    doi:10.1128/mBio.00889-14PubMed 24757212

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Financial support was provided by the NIH Human Microbiome Project Demonstration Project (UH3 DK083993).
    Finding
    225 genomes carried a butyrate synthesis pathway. Four pathways (acetyl-CoA, glutarate, 4-aminobutyrate, lysine) converge on the conversion of crotonyl-CoA to butyryl-CoA by the butyryl-CoA dehydrogenase–electron-transferring flavoprotein complex, a step postulated to conserve energy through ferredoxin reduction. In the acetyl-CoA pathway, thiolase, β-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydrogenase and crotonase precede this step, and butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase or phosphate butyryltransferase with butyrate kinase forms butyrate. In stool metagenomes the acetyl-CoA pathway made up a mean 79.7% of butyrate pathways; for that pathway the terminal genes but and buk had median abundances of 77.2% and 21.8%. Butyrate is described as the main energy source for colonocytes.

    The great majority of coeliac patients are HLA-DQ2-positive (DQA1*0501/DQB1*02) and the remaining few normally express HLA-DQ8 (DQA1*03/DQB1*0302); tissue transglutaminase (tTG) is also the major target of coeliac autoantibodies.     EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (NDA). Scientific Opinion on the substantiation of a health claim related to oat beta-glucan and lowering blood cholesterol and reduced risk of (coronary) heart disease pursuant to Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 EFSA Journal, 2010.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1885

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority, on request from the Competent Authority of the United Kingdom following an application by CreaNutrition AG (Question EFSA-Q-2008-681). The evidence assessed was supplied by the applicant, which claimed proprietary rights and confidentiality for three unpublished studies and one unpublished meta-analysis.
    Finding
    Oat beta-glucan is a non-digestible linear glucose polymer with mixed beta-(1-4) and beta-(1-3) links in an approximate ratio of 70% to 30%, occurring naturally in oat bran. In the unpublished meta-analysis submitted by the applicant as proprietary (Whitehead 2008, MPS Research Unit, Lancaster University) of 18 studies (1080 subjects, 3 to 9 g/d, 2 to 12 weeks), total cholesterol changed by -0.34 mmol/L (95% CI -0.42 to -0.25) and LDL cholesterol by -0.28 mmol/L (-0.35 to -0.22); the fitted effect of 3 g/d was -0.21 mmol/L (-0.31 to -0.11) for LDL cholesterol. Proposed mechanism: increased small-intestinal viscosity reduces bile acid reabsorption. The Panel concluded that a cause and effect relationship is established between oat beta-glucan and lowering of blood LDL cholesterol, and that foods bearing the claim should provide at least 3 g oat beta-glucan per day. The Panel could have reached this conclusion without considering the studies claimed by the applicant as proprietary.

  10. Expert consensus

    Al-Toma A, Zingone F, Branchi F, Schiepatti A, Malamut G, Canova C, et al. European Society for the Study of Coeliac Disease 2025 Updated Guidelines on the Diagnosis and Management of Coeliac Disease in Adults. Part 1: Diagnostic Approach United European Gastroenterology Journal, 2025.

    doi:10.1002/ueg2.70119PubMed 40999951

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    The authors received no specific funding for this work. Development of these guidelines was not financially funded.
    Finding
    Coeliac disease is an inflammatory disorder of genetically predisposed persons triggered by an immune response to gluten, proteins of wheat, barley and rye. 90% to 95% of patients carry HLA-DQ2.5 (DQA1*05 and DQB1*02, in cis or trans); the remaining 5% to 10% carry HLA-DQ8 (DQA1*03 and DQB1*03:02) or HLA-DQ2.2; HLA risk alleles are necessary but not sufficient and confer about 35% to 40% of the genetic risk. Prevalence in western countries is about 0.7% histologically confirmed and 1% to 1.6% by serological screening. Recommendations: IgA anti-TG2 as the single initial test at any age, with total IgA, on a gluten-containing diet (moderate certainty, strong, 95% agreement); diagnosis without biopsy in adults when IgA anti-TG2 is at least 10 times the upper limit of normal (moderate certainty, conditional, 95%), confirmed in a second blood sample and, until more safety data are available, limited to patients under 45 years; HLA testing has a poor positive and a high negative predictive value, so HLA-DQ2/8 typing is not used routinely in initial diagnosis (moderate certainty, strong, 100%).
