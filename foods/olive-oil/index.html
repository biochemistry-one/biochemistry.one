Foods

Olive oil

In brief

Olive oil is the fat pressed from olives; most of its fatty acids have one double bond, and virgin oils also contain small amounts of phenolic compounds. In a Spanish trial, about 2 fewer people in 100 had a heart attack or stroke or died of cardiovascular disease over 5 years on a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil than on advice to eat less fat. The first report of that trial was withdrawn in 2018 because some participants had not been randomly assigned, and a reanalysis gave similar results.

What it is

Olive oil is the fat pressed from olives; most of its fatty acids have one double bond, and virgin oils contain small amounts of phenolic compounds 12.

Per 100 g, extra virgin olive oil contains on average 67.8 g cis-18:1 fatty acids, 15.4 g saturated fatty acids and 8.4 g linoleic acid 1. Its main phenolic compounds are the dialdehydes of oleuropein aglycone, mean 25.16 mg per 100 g (range 0.08 to 94.20), and of ligstroside aglycone, 14.28 mg, against 0.77 mg of free hydroxytyrosol 2.

In the body

Human cells make oleic acid from a saturated fatty acid, and the gut probably absorbs more than half of olive oil’s phenolic compounds 34.

Stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD) forms oleoyl-CoA by inserting a cis Δ9 double bond into stearoyl-CoA, with O2 and electrons from reduced cytochrome b5 3.

Absorbed tyrosol and hydroxytyrosol enter lipoprotein fractions, and hydroxytyrosol is excreted in urine as a glucuronide 4.

What studies show

In a Spanish trial, a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil was followed by fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths than low-fat advice 5.

PREDIMED assigned 7447 adults at high cardiovascular risk to a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil, 1 litre a week per household, a Mediterranean diet with nuts, or advice to reduce dietary fat 5. Over a median 4.8 years, myocardial infarction, stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 96 of 2543 participants on the olive oil diet and 109 of 2450 controls 5. The 5-year risks were 3.6% and 5.7% (adjusted hazard ratio 0.69, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.91), a number needed to treat of 48 for 5 years 5. Deaths from any cause numbered 118 and 114 (hazard ratio 0.90, 0.69 to 1.18) 5.

The 2013 report was withdrawn after deviations from randomisation emerged, including 425 household members and 467 participants at one site who were not randomised individually 5. After exclusion of 1588 participants with known or suspected deviations, the olive oil hazard ratio was 0.71 (0.52 to 0.97) 5. Public bodies, chiefly Spain’s Instituto de Salud Carlos III, and the Fundación Mapfre funded the trial 5. Olive oil and nut firms and trade bodies donated the foods; sponsors had no role in design, analysis or reporting 5.

Industry-funded results

In a crossover trial in 200 healthy European men, olive oils with 2.7, 164 or 366 mg/kg of phenolics, 25 mL a day for 3 weeks each, changed oxidised LDL by 1.21, −1.48 and −3.21 U/L 6. Only the last 95% CI, −5.1 to −0.8, excluded zero 6. The European Commission funded the trial; two authors listed at Oy Jurilab, the company that measured the oxidation markers, took part in its design and analysis 6.

Consensus statements

In 2011 the EFSA nutrition panel concluded, from that trial and smaller ones, that olive oil polyphenols protect LDL particles from oxidative damage at 5 mg of hydroxytyrosol and its derivatives a day 4. Some olive oils may supply too little of them within a balanced diet 4.

Unresolved questions

PREDIMED tested a dietary pattern; fish and legume intake also rose against control 5. The 2013 report, retracted in 2018, judged the oil and nuts probably responsible for most of the benefit 78. The 2018 report states that the largest between-group differences involved them 5.

In enzyme assays, oleocanthal, the deacetoxy-dialdehydic ligstroside aglycone, inhibited COX-1 and COX-2, as ibuprofen does (IC50 23 and 28 µM) 9. The funding statement of that study could not be read 9.

    Funding

    Finding
    A cause and effect relationship is established between consumption of olive oil polyphenols, standardised by their content of hydroxytyrosol and its derivatives, and protection of LDL particles from oxidative damage; proposed wording: 'Consumption of olive oil polyphenols contributes to the protection of blood lipids from oxidative damage'; condition: 5 mg of hydroxytyrosol and its derivatives (e.g. oleuropein complex and tyrosol) in olive oil daily; the concentrations in some olive oils may be too low to supply this amount within a balanced diet. Evidence: dose-dependent decreases in circulating oxidised LDL in one trial of 200 men over 3 weeks (oils with 2.7, 164 and 366 mg/kg phenolic compounds, 25 mL/day; lowest effective dose 5 mg/day) and in smaller trials of 4 days to 3 weeks, one including refined, common and virgin oils with 0 to 150 mg/kg; an acute study of 12 men. Absorption of olive oil phenolics probably exceeds 55-66 mol%; hydroxytyrosol absorption is dose-dependent; tyrosol and hydroxytyrosol are incorporated in lipoprotein fractions; hydroxytyrosol is excreted in urine as glucuronide; hydroxytyrosol monosulfate and homovanillic acid sulphate increased in LDL after high-phenolic oil. In olives, hydroxytyrosol occurs as its elenolic acid ester, oleuropein. Evidence insufficient for maintenance of normal HDL cholesterol; cause and effect not established for normal blood pressure.

    Finding
    Abstract: oleocanthal, a compound of newly pressed extra virgin olive oil whose pungency causes a stinging sensation in the throat, inhibits the same cyclooxygenase enzymes of the prostaglandin-biosynthesis pathway as ibuprofen, with a potency and profile the authors describe as strikingly similar. Supplementary methods: the irritant isolated from olive oil was identified by NMR as 2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)ethyl 4-formyl-3-(2-oxoethyl)-4-hexenoate, the deacetoxy-dialdehydic ligstroside aglycone; in commercial kit assays, (-)-oleocanthal inhibited ovine COX-1 and human recombinant COX-2 dose-dependently with IC50 values of 23 and 28 µM and did not affect soybean 15-lipoxygenase; the highest concentration among ten rated commercial oils was 200 µg/ml. The supplementary methods describe enzyme assays and sensory tests of throat irritation.
