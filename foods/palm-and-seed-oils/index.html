Foods

Palm and seed oils

In brief

Palm oil comes from the fruit of the oil palm, and about half of its fat is saturated; seed oils such as soybean and corn oil are rich in linoleic acid, an omega-6 fat that the body requires but cannot make. In feeding trials, palm oil raised LDL cholesterol more than oils low in saturated fat; replacing saturated fat with seed oils lowers blood cholesterol but has not been shown to reduce deaths. Red palm oil retains most of the fruit's carotenes, a source of vitamin A; conventional refining destroys them and forms contaminants that the European Food Safety Authority has assessed.

What they are

Palm oil comes from the pulp of the oil palm fruit; about half its fat is saturated 12. Seed oils such as soybean, canola and corn oil supply linoleic acid, an omega-6 fat 3.

In the body

Digestion frees palm oil’s fatty acids, which gut cells repackage into fat particles 5. The body cannot make linoleic or α-linolenic acid and converts little of either into longer fatty acids 6.

In palm oil, most palmitic acid is esterified at the sn-1 and sn-3 positions 1. Colipase-anchored pancreatic lipase hydrolyses these esters, leaving sn-2-monoacylglycerol 7.

Humans lack Δ12- and Δ15-desaturases 6. Under 1% of deuterated linoleic acid became arachidonic acid in one study 6. Conversion of α-linolenic acid is estimated at 8–12% to eicosapentaenoic and possibly under 1% to docosahexaenoic acid; more dietary linoleic acid lowers it 6.

In 36 studies of adults on Western-type diets, lowering dietary linoleic acid by up to 90% or raising it up to sixfold was not significantly correlated with changes in plasma phospholipid arachidonic acid 8.

Prostaglandin G/H synthases, the targets of acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), convert arachidonate to prostaglandin H2, precursor of 2-series prostaglandins and thromboxanes 9.

What studies show

In feeding trials, palm oil raised LDL cholesterol more than oils low in saturated fat 1. In 27 such trials (807 participants, 2 to 16 weeks), the difference was 0.24 mmol/L (95% CI 0.13 to 0.35), and 0.31 mmol/L (0.20 to 0.42) in randomised trials 1.

In Burkina Faso, 24 schools were randomised to usual lunches, one 60 mg vitamin A capsule, or 15 ml red palm oil three times a week 10. Over 12 months, serum retinol below 0.70 µmol/L fell from 40.4% to 14.9% of 114 pupils on the oil, 46.1% to 17.1% of 117 on the capsule and 23.6% to 14.2% of 106 controls 10. Controls had higher baseline retinol; endline retinol did not differ significantly 10.

Seed oils in place of saturated fat lower blood cholesterol, but trials have not shown fewer deaths 111213. In the Sydney Diet Heart Study, 458 men after a coronary event were randomised to safflower oil and safflower-oil margarine instead of saturated fat, or to no specific dietary instruction 14. Over a median 39 months, 17.6% of 221 against 11.8% of 237 died (hazard ratio 1.62, 95% CI 1.00 to 2.64; number needed to harm 17) 14.

The Minnesota Coronary Experiment randomised 9423 residents of mental hospitals and a nursing home to corn oil and corn-oil margarine instead of saturated fat, or to a control diet 13. Serum cholesterol fell 13.8% against 1.0% in the 2355 fed the diets for a year or more, but recovered life tables showed no mortality benefit 13.

In a Cochrane review of 19 trials of more omega-6 fat (6461 participants, 1 to 8 years), mortality was 167 per 1000 in both arms (risk ratio 1.00, 0.88 to 1.12; 10 trials) 11. In another, polyunsaturated instead of saturated fat for at least 2 years gave risk ratios of 0.79 (0.62 to 1.00; 8 trials) for cardiovascular events and 0.96 (0.82 to 1.13; 7 trials) for death 12.

In cohorts (median follow-up 4.9 to 30.2 years), the highest linoleic acid intake was associated with lower mortality than the lowest (relative risk 0.87, 0.81 to 0.94; 11 studies, 170,076 deaths) 15.

Industry-funded results

In a meta-analysis of 51 trials (1526 volunteers, 2 to 16 weeks), palm oil raised LDL cholesterol by 10.75 mg/dL against diets rich in monounsaturated fat 16. The Malaysian Palm Oil Board supported 19 trials, in which cholesterol changes were smaller than in others 16. Soremartec Italia, with a financial interest in products containing palm oil, co-funded the analysis 16.

Unilever partly funded a pooled analysis of 30 cohorts (68,659 participants; median follow-up 2.5 to 31.9 years) 17. Higher linoleic acid biomarkers were associated with lower cardiovascular mortality (hazard ratio per interquintile range 0.78, 0.70 to 0.85) 17.

Consensus statements

A 2017 American Heart Association advisory lists palm oil among the main sources of saturated fat to decrease 18. It pooled four trials it judged core: polyunsaturated vegetable oil, mainly soybean oil, instead of saturated fat lowered coronary heart disease (relative risk 0.71, 0.61 to 0.83) 18.

Unresolved questions

In the independent analysis, smaller doses explained weaker industry-funded results 1. In the co-funded one, board funding was closely related to Asian settings, young normocholesterolaemic volunteers and lower-fat diets 16. Data on palm oil and cardiovascular disease are sparse 1.

The advisory excluded the Sydney trial for a margarine it describes as high in trans fat 18. The reanalysts state that trans fat was not recorded 14.

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) found human data insufficient to recommend an omega-6 to omega-3 ratio independent of absolute intakes 6.

Processing

Refining makes palm oil nearly colourless and forms contaminants from its fat 419.

Conventional refining, with high-temperature deodorisation, removes some tocopherols and tocotrienols and destroys all carotenoids; a milder patented process gives red palm oil with 80% of them 4. Glycidyl esters form from diacylglycerols, up to 12% of palm oil, during deodorisation above 200 °C; 3-monochloropropanediol (3-MCPD) esters form on refining 19. Palm oil averaged 2,912 µg/kg 3-MCPD and 3,955 µg/kg glycidol from esters, rapeseed oil 232 and 166 µg/kg 19. In animals, ingested esters are extensively hydrolysed; glycidol, an epoxide, caused tumours at several sites in rats and mice, probably by genotoxicity 19.

Only high consumers in younger age groups slightly exceed the EFSA tolerable daily intake for 3-MCPD and its esters, 2 µg/kg body weight 20. For glycidol, margins of exposure below 25,000 indicate a health concern; for infants fed only formula, the margin was 2,100 at 95th-percentile occurrence 19.

    Safflower oil and safflower margarine replacing saturated fat; median polyunsaturated fat intake rose from 6.1% to 15.4% of energy. All-cause death 17.6% (intervention, n=221) versus 11.8% (control, n=237), HR 1.62 (95% CI 1.00 to 2.64); cardiovascular death 17.2% versus 11.0%, HR 1.70 (1.03 to 2.80); coronary death 16.3% versus 10.1%, HR 1.74 (1.04 to 2.92). Serum cholesterol fell 13.3% versus 5.5% at 12 months. Updated meta-analysis: linoleic-acid-selective trials, coronary death HR 1.33 (0.99 to 1.79); mixed omega-3 and omega-6 trials, cardiovascular death HR 0.79 (0.63 to 0.99). Trans fat content of the diets was not recorded; the authors state the margarine was selected for a high PUFA to SFA ratio, a characteristic of margarines with comparatively low trans fat.

  18. Expert consensus

    Sacks FM, Lichtenstein AH, Wu JHY, Appel LJ, Creager MA, Kris-Etherton PM, et al. Dietary fats and cardiovascular disease: a presidential advisory from the American Heart Association Circulation, 2017.

    doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000000510PubMed 28620111

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated; the advisory was approved by the American Heart Association Science Advisory and Coordinating Committee and Executive Committee
    Finding
    Fixed-effects meta-analysis of four core trials (Dayton et al, Oslo Diet-Heart Study, Medical Research Council soybean oil trial, Finnish Mental Hospital Study): replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated vegetable oil, primarily soybean oil, lowered coronary heart disease by 29% (random-effects RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.83). The Minnesota Coronary Survey was excluded from the core group because of short duration (average 384 days), a large percentage of withdrawals and intermittent treatment, with concern about lightly hydrogenated corn oil margarine; the Sydney Diet Heart Study was not included because a margarine high in trans unsaturated fat was described as a major component of the high polyunsaturated diet. Conclusion: lowering saturated fat and replacing it with unsaturated fats, especially polyunsaturated fats, will lower the incidence of cardiovascular disease.

  19. Regulator

    EFSA Panel on Contaminants in the Food Chain (CONTAM). Risks for human health related to the presence of 3- and 2-monochloropropanediol (MCPD), and their fatty acid esters, and glycidyl fatty acid esters in food EFSA Journal, 2016.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4426

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority, on request from the European Commission (Question number EFSA-Q-2014-00535).
    Finding
    3- and 2-MCPD fatty acid esters are produced in vegetable oils on refining; glycidyl esters form from diacylglycerols when oils are heated above 200 °C, for example during deodorisation. Mean middle-bound concentrations (µg/kg) of 3-MCPD from esters: sunflower seed oil 521, maize oil 503, soya bean oil 394, rapeseed oil 232, palm oil/fat 2,912; of glycidol from esters: sunflower 269, maize 650, soya bean 171, rapeseed 166, palm 3,955. Glycidol increased tumour incidence in several tissues of rats and mice, likely via a genotoxic mode of action. Animal studies show extensive hydrolysis of esterified 3-MCPD and glycidol after oral administration; with no in vivo data for glycidyl esters, dose-response considerations assumed complete hydrolysis of the esters to free glycidol after ingestion. There is strong evidence from in vitro data and some evidence from in vivo studies that glycidol is genotoxic.
