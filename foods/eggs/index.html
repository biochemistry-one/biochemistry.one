Foods

Eggs

In brief

Egg yolk is rich in cholesterol and also supplies choline and the yellow pigments lutein and zeaxanthin. In feeding trials, eggs raise blood cholesterol a little, and some people respond more than others. Studies that follow large groups of people for decades disagree on whether eating eggs is linked to heart disease.

What it is

Egg white is mostly water and protein; the yolk holds nearly all the fat, cholesterol, choline and yellow pigments 1.

Per 100 g, whole raw egg contains 372 mg cholesterol, 294 mg choline and 503 µg lutein plus zeaxanthin; the white contains no cholesterol, lutein or zeaxanthin 1. A large egg weighs 50 g and supplies 186 mg cholesterol and 147 mg choline 1. EFSA’s Adequate Intake of choline for adults is 400 mg a day 2.

In the body

Gut cells take up egg cholesterol through a transporter; blood cholesterol rises more in some people than others 34.

NPC1L1, an apical enterocyte protein with a sterol-sensing domain, is critical for cholesterol uptake 35.

When egg yolk raised Dutch volunteers’ cholesterol intake from 129 to 989 mg a day, serum cholesterol rose by 0.82 mmol/L in 17 previously selected hyper-responders and 0.47 mmol/L in 15 hypo-responders 4. The differences were reproducible but modest: responses were unimodal, with a between-person standard deviation of 0.29 mmol/L 4.

Choline is a component of phospholipids and a precursor of acetylcholine and betaine, a methyl donor for homocysteine remethylation; dietary deficiency can cause fatty liver 2. Lutein and zeaxanthin travel in chylomicrons, then partly in HDL, and colour the macula lutea 6.

What studies show

Egg cholesterol raises blood cholesterol modestly; long-term studies of large groups disagree on heart disease 789.

In 17 controlled trials (556 participants, at least 14 days), each extra 100 mg cholesterol a day raised total cholesterol by 0.056 mmol/L (95% CI 0.046 to 0.065) and HDL cholesterol by 0.008 mmol/L 7. One extra egg a day, taken as 200 mg, was predicted to raise LDL cholesterol by 0.100 mmol/L 7.

A review by a group promoting plant-based diets found egg- or poultry-industry funding in 0 of 9 studies from the 1950s and 25 of 42 from 2010–2019 10. Most intervention studies reported net cholesterol rises, which conclusions called favourable in 49% of industry-funded and 13% of other studies 10.

Industry-funded results

In an American Egg Board-funded crossover trial in 51 premenopausal women, eggs adding 640 mg cholesterol a day for 30 days raised LDL and HDL cholesterol in hyper-responders; the authors judged the profile not more atherogenic because the LDL/HDL ratio was maintained 11. In another Board-supported crossover trial, one egg a day for 5 weeks raised serum lutein by 26% and zeaxanthin by 38% in 33 older adults without significant lipid change 12.

Consensus statements

In 2020 the American Heart Association judged up to one egg a day acceptable for healthy people and up to two for older people with normal cholesterol, and advised caution with cholesterol-rich foods in dyslipidaemia 13.

Unresolved questions

In six pooled US cohorts (29,615 adults, median 17.5 years), each extra half egg a day was associated with incident cardiovascular disease (hazard ratio 1.06, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.10; absolute risk difference 1.11 percentage points at 31.3 years); with dietary cholesterol adjusted, the hazard ratio was 0.99 (0.93 to 1.05) 8. In three cohorts of US health professionals (215,618 adults, up to 32 years), one or more eggs a day against less than one a month gave crude rates of 318 and 231 per 100,000 person-years and an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.93 (0.82 to 1.05) 9. Diet was assessed every 2 to 4 years in the professional cohorts, once in the pooled cohorts 98.

    Katan MB, Beynen AC, de Vries JH, Nobels A. Existence of consistent hypo- and hyperresponders to dietary cholesterol in man American Journal of Epidemiology, 1986.

    doi:10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a114231PubMed 3946372

    Funding and finding
    Participants
    94
    Follow-up
    Three controlled feeding experiments in 1982, each a low-cholesterol period followed by a high-cholesterol period of 13 to 28 days
    Funding
    Supported by the Netherlands Heart Foundation, grants no. 31.013, 78.093, 79.045 and 26.003. M. B. Katan is an established investigator of the Netherlands Heart Foundation.
    In-kind support
    The methods name a special bread from Crock-Laan and a special margarine from Van den Bergh & Jurgens B.V.; the paper does not state whether they were donated.
    Finding
    Egg yolk supplied the added cholesterol, the only dietary variable. In experiment 1, 94 adults whose intake rose from 121 to 625 mg/day showed a rise in serum cholesterol of 0.50 ± 0.39 mmol/L. Seventeen putative hyperresponders and 15 hyporesponders selected in experiment 1 showed responses of 0.28 ± 0.38 and 0.06 ± 0.35 mmol/L in experiment 2 (106 to 673 mg/day) and 0.82 ± 0.35 and 0.47 ± 0.26 mmol/L in experiment 3 (129 to 989 mg/day). After correction for within-person fluctuation, the between-subject standard deviation of true responsiveness was about 0.29 mmol/L; mean individual responses formed a unimodal distribution without discrete subgroups. The authors conclude that modest differences in responsiveness exist and that the wide scatter of responses in single experiments is largely due to chance fluctuations.

  5. Independent research

    Brown MS, Radhakrishnan A, Goldstein JL. Retrospective on Cholesterol Homeostasis: The Central Role of Scap Annual Review of Biochemistry, 2018.

    doi:10.1146/annurev-biochem-062917-011852PubMed 28841344

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    National Institutes of Health (HL20948 to J.L.G., M.S.B. and A.R.) and The Welch Foundation (I-1793 to A.R.).
    Finding
    Review. SREBPs are ER membrane proteins with an NH2-terminal bHLH-Zip domain, two transmembrane helices and a COOH-terminal domain that binds Scap; SREBP-2 is specialised for cholesterol homeostasis. In cholesterol-deprived cells the MELADL sequence in cytosolic Loop 6 of Scap binds Sec23-Sec24 of COPII and Scap carries SREBPs to the Golgi, where S1P cleaves the luminal loop and S2P cleaves within the transmembrane helix, releasing the bHLH-Zip domain to the nucleus. Cholesterol binds luminal Loop 1 of Scap (apparent Kd 67 nM for the purified loop); Scap then binds Insig-1 or Insig-2 and MELADL becomes inaccessible to COPII. 25-Hydroxycholesterol binds Insig, not Scap. Insig-1 and Insig-2 also bind the membrane domain of HMG CoA reductase, the rate-controlling enzyme of cholesterol biosynthesis, and trigger its ubiquitination and degradation. Sterol-sensing domains occur in Scap, HMG CoA reductase, 7-dehydrocholesterol reductase (last step of cholesterol synthesis), NPC1 (export of LDL-derived cholesterol from lysosomes) and NPC1L1 (intestinal cholesterol absorption).

  13. Expert consensus

    Carson JAS, Lichtenstein AH, Anderson CAM, Appel LJ, Kris-Etherton PM, Meyer KA, et al. Dietary Cholesterol and Cardiovascular Risk: A Science Advisory From the American Heart Association Circulation, 2020.

    doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000000743PubMed 31838890

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    not stated; the advisory was approved by the American Heart Association Science Advisory and Coordinating Committee and Executive Committee
    Finding
    Observational studies generally do not indicate a significant association of dietary cholesterol with cardiovascular risk; most meta-analyses of intervention studies associate intakes above current averages with higher total or LDL cholesterol. In the AHA meta-regression of 11 controlled feeding studies (25 comparisons) with similar polyunsaturated-to-saturated fat ratios in the compared diets, dietary cholesterol significantly increased total cholesterol, but the findings were not significant for LDL or HDL cholesterol. Given the cholesterol content of egg yolks, it remains advisable to limit intake to current levels; healthy individuals can include up to a whole egg or equivalent daily; lacto-ovo vegetarians may include more; patients with dyslipidaemia, particularly those with diabetes mellitus or at risk for heart failure, should be cautious with cholesterol-rich foods; for older normocholesterolaemic patients up to 2 eggs per day is acceptable within a heart-healthy dietary pattern. A large egg contains about 186 mg cholesterol.
