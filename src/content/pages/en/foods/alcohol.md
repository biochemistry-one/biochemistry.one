## What it is

The alcohol in drinks is ethanol, formed when glucose is fermented [@teschke-2018-alcoholic-liver-disease], absorbed mainly from the small intestine and distributed throughout body water [@edenberg-2018-adh-aldh].

## In the body

The liver converts ethanol to acetaldehyde, which damages DNA, and acetaldehyde to acetate [@edenberg-2018-adh-aldh; @brooks-2009-flushing].

::pathway{id="ethanol-oxidation"}

Class I alcohol dehydrogenases catalyse most hepatic ethanol oxidation; ADH1B\*2 (rs1229984) has a Vmax of 190 min⁻¹ in vitro against 5.2 min⁻¹ for ADH1B\*1, yet carriers of European descent eliminate ethanol only about 14% faster [@edenberg-2018-adh-aldh]. The CYP2E1-dependent microsomal system, with a Km for ethanol of 7–11 mM against 0.5–2.0 mM for ADH in animal studies, is induced by chronic drinking [@teschke-2018-alcoholic-liver-disease].

Mitochondrial ALDH2 oxidises acetaldehyde with a Km of 0.2 µM, and a single ALDH2\*2 subunit (rs671, Glu504Lys) renders the tetramer nearly inactive; the allele is common in East Asia and rare elsewhere [@edenberg-2018-adh-aldh]. In carriers, blood acetaldehyde can reach 30–75 µM, against 3 µM or less in non-carriers, causing flushing, nausea, headache and tachycardia [@edenberg-2018-adh-aldh].

Both steps reduce NAD⁺ to NADH, and the raised hepatic NADH/NAD⁺ ratio stimulates fatty acid synthesis [@teschke-2018-alcoholic-liver-disease]. Ethanol given with an adequate diet produced hepatic fat accumulation in all five previously abstinent alcoholics [@lieber-1965-fatty-liver].

## What studies show

Drinkers with the flushing variant have a much higher risk of oesophageal cancer than other drinkers [@brooks-2009-flushing]. In Japanese and Taiwanese case-control studies, heterozygous drinkers had odds ratios of 3.7 to 18.1 for oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma after adjustment for intake; in non-drinkers, ALDH2 deficiency does not alter the risk [@brooks-2009-flushing].

Former drinkers, whose relative risk of death was 1.22 (95% CI 1.14 to 1.31), were counted as abstainers in 65 of 87 mortality cohorts [@stockwell-2016-mortality]. Unadjusted, the pooled curve was J-shaped: relative risk 0.86 (0.83 to 0.90) at 1.3–24.9 g ethanol a day, against 0.97 (0.88 to 1.07) after adjustment for abstainer biases and study characteristics [@stockwell-2016-mortality]. In an update of 107 cohorts, fully adjusted relative risks against lifetime non-drinkers were 0.93 (0.85 to 1.01) at 1.3–24.0 g a day and 1.35 (1.23 to 1.47) at 65 g or more [@zhao-2023-mortality].

:::industry
In the China Kadoorie Biobank (512,715 adults, follow-up about 10 years), men's mean intake was 3, 37 and 157 g a week with rs671 AA, AG and GG [@millwood-2019-ckb]. Self-reported intake had U-shaped associations with stroke; genotype-predicted intake did not; per 280 g a week, its relative risks were 1.38 (1.26 to 1.51) for stroke and 0.96 (0.78 to 1.18) for myocardial infarction [@millwood-2019-ckb]. Its funders, GlaxoSmithKline among them, had no role, according to the paper [@millwood-2019-ckb].
:::

:::consensus
The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies alcohol consumption, ethanol in alcoholic beverages and the associated acetaldehyde as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1); alcohol consumption causes cancers of the oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, colorectum, liver and female breast [@iarc-2012-personal-habits].
:::

:::unresolved
Whether alcohol protects the heart is unresolved [@mitchell-2020-mach]. In 599,912 current drinkers (median follow-up 7.5 years), the hazard ratio for myocardial infarction was 0.94 (0.91 to 0.97) per 100 g a week [@wood-2018-risk-thresholds]; in Chinese men, genotype-predicted intake was not associated with it [@millwood-2019-ckb]. In 2018 the US National Institutes of Health ended MACH, a trial of one drink a day against none, after its reviewers found that officials had sought industry funding inappropriately; five alcohol producers provided about two thirds of the $100 million [@mitchell-2020-mach].
:::
