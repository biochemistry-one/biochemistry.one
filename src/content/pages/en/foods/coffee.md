## What it is

Brewed coffee supplies caffeine and chlorogenic acids, and unfiltered coffee also cafestol, which raises cholesterol [@fukagawa-2022-coffee; @neveu-2010-coffee; @urgert-1996-cafetiere].

A 237 g cup contains about 95 mg caffeine [@fukagawa-2022-coffee]. Filter coffee averages 70 mg of 5-caffeoylquinic acid, or chlorogenic acid, per 100 ml [@neveu-2010-coffee]. Cafetière coffee, 0.9 litres a day, supplied 38 mg of the diterpene cafestol, paper-filtered coffee less than 1 mg [@urgert-1996-cafetiere].

## In the body

Caffeine is absorbed completely, blocks adenosine receptors in the brain and is broken down in the liver, mainly by one enzyme, [CYP1A2](/metabolism/drug-metabolism/) [@efsa-2015-caffeine].

CYP1A2 performs over 95% of primary caffeine metabolism, mostly N3-demethylation to paraxanthine, 70% to 80% of a dose [@thorn-2012-caffeine]. The half-life is about 4 hours (range 2 to 8), and 6 to 16 hours in pregnancy, when CYP1A2 activity falls [@efsa-2015-caffeine].

::pathway{id="caffeine-paraxanthine"}

Smoking induces CYP1A2 and raises caffeine clearance up to 1.72-fold [@efsa-2015-caffeine]. In two studies, CYP1A2 activity was higher with the −163AA genotype (rs762551) in smokers only [@efsa-2015-caffeine]. Caffeine antagonises adenosine A1 and A2A receptors [@efsa-2015-caffeine].

## What studies show

Unfiltered coffee raises LDL [cholesterol](/metabolism/cholesterol/); coffee drinkers die at slightly lower rates, but gene variants that raise coffee intake are not associated with lower mortality [@urgert-1996-cafetiere; @loftfield-2018-uk-biobank; @nordestgaard-2016-mendelian].

In a randomised trial in 46 adults, 24 weeks of cafetière coffee raised LDL cholesterol by 0.26 mmol/L (95% CI 0.04 to 0.47) against filtered coffee [@urgert-1996-cafetiere].

In UK Biobank, 14,225 of 498,134 adults died over a median 7 years; against non-drinkers, the adjusted hazard ratio at 2 to 3 cups a day was 0.88 (0.84 to 0.93), similar for decaffeinated coffee and across genetic caffeine-metabolism scores [@loftfield-2018-uk-biobank]. In Copenhagen, coffee intake had a U-shaped association with death, but alleles near *AHR* and *CYP1A1/2* that raise intake were not associated with it (hazard ratio per allele 1.01, 0.99 to 1.03; 12,656 deaths among 112,509) [@nordestgaard-2016-mendelian].

Among 2635 pregnant women in England, fetal growth restriction occurred in 10.9% below 100 mg caffeine a day and 18.2% at 300 mg or more (adjusted odds ratio 1.4, 1.0 to 2.0); tea supplied 62% of the caffeine [@care-2008-fetal-growth].

:::industry
In a non-randomised trial funded by the Institute for Scientific Information on Coffee (ISIC), a consortium of European coffee companies, 47 coffee drinkers had 12% higher total and 7% higher HDL cholesterol after a month at 8 cups of filtered coffee a day than after a month without; LDL cholesterol did not change significantly, and the authors called the HDL effect beneficial [@kempf-2010-filtered-coffee]. The paper reports no funder role in design or analysis [@kempf-2010-filtered-coffee].
:::

:::consensus
EFSA judged habitual caffeine intake up to 400 mg a day of no safety concern for non-pregnant adults and, for the fetus, up to 200 mg a day in pregnancy; it treated as causal a cohort risk judged clinically relevant from about 200 mg [@efsa-2015-caffeine]. IARC classed drinking coffee as not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity (Group 3); observers for ISIC and the US National Coffee Association took no part in the evaluation [@iarc-2018-coffee].
:::

:::unresolved
Cohort and genetic mortality findings conflict. In Copenhagen, 36% of those drinking over 5 cups a day smoked, against 12% of non-drinkers, and the genetic analysis lacked power to exclude the cohort estimate [@nordestgaard-2016-mendelian]. Genetically predicted maternal coffee intake was not associated with miscarriage, stillbirth or preterm birth [@brito-nunes-2023-pregnancy].

How cafestol raises LDL cholesterol in humans is unestablished; it activates FXR and PXR in cell assays and represses hepatic *Cyp7a1*, rate-limiting in bile acid synthesis, in wild-type mice without raising their cholesterol [@ricketts-2007-cafestol].
:::
