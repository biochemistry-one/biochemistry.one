## What it is

Table sugar, or sucrose, is made of glucose and fructose in equal parts [@hannou-2018-fructose]. Glucose–fructose syrup, called high-fructose corn syrup in the United States, is made from starch and mostly contains 42% or 55% free fructose [@turck-2022-sugars]. Free sugars include sugars added to foods and those in honey, syrups and fruit juices [@turck-2022-sugars].

## In the body

The liver takes up most dietary fructose and breaks it down by a route bypassing the main control point of glucose breakdown [@hannou-2018-fructose; @turck-2022-sugars].

Ketohexokinase C, aldolase B and triokinase convert fructose to triose phosphates, which enter glycolysis below phosphofructokinase, the step inhibited by ATP and citrate, and yield fatty acids [@hannou-2018-fructose; @turck-2022-sugars].

::pathway{id="hepatic-fructose-metabolism"}

Large fructose loads transiently lower hepatic ATP and phosphate; falling phosphate activates AMP deaminase, and AMP is degraded to uric acid [@hannou-2018-fructose; @turck-2022-sugars].

::pathway{id="fructose-uric-acid"}

Loss of ketohexokinase causes benign essential fructosuria, and loss of aldolase B hereditary fructose intolerance [@hannou-2018-fructose; @gaughan-2021-hfi].

## What studies show

In a double-blind trial in 641 Dutch children, a daily sugar-free drink led to less weight gain over 18 months than one with 26 g sucrose [@de-ruyter-2012-drink].

Weight rose by 6.35 kg and 7.37 kg (difference −1.01 kg, 95% CI −1.54 to −0.48) [@de-ruyter-2012-drink]. In 224 overweight or obese adolescents, a 1-year intervention including home delivery of non-caloric drinks reduced weight gain at 1 year by 1.9 kg; BMI change at 2 years, the primary outcome, did not differ (P = 0.46) [@ebbeling-2012-beverages].

In 32 overweight or obese adults given drinks supplying 25% of energy requirements as fructose or glucose for 10 weeks, weight rose similarly, but only fructose raised postprandial fractional hepatic de novo lipogenesis (11.4% to 16.9%), intra-abdominal fat (+14.0%) and fasting apoB (+27.2%) [@stanhope-2009-fructose-glucose]. Groups were matched, and the US National Institutes of Health funded the study [@stanhope-2009-fructose-glucose]. In a randomised trial in 94 young men, 80 g a day of fructose or sucrose in drinks for 7 weeks doubled the fractional secretion rate of newly synthesised VLDL palmitate (median 19.7 and 20.8 against 9.1%/day without drinks); glucose did not (11.0%/day) [@geidl-flueck-2021-lipogenesis].

The Sugar Research Foundation funded a review in 1965, set its objective and received drafts; in the review, published in 1967 without disclosure of this role, studies linking sucrose to coronary heart disease were discounted [@kearns-2016-sugar-industry]. Evidence linking sugary drinks to weight gain was judged insufficient in 5 of 6 conclusions of systematic reviews with food-industry conflicts of interest and in 2 of 12 without (relative risk 5.0, 95% CI 1.3 to 19.3) [@bes-rastrollo-2013-beverages].

:::industry
In 46,393 US men followed for 12 years, 2 or more servings of sugar-sweetened soft drinks a day, against less than 1 a month, were associated with gout (16 cases in 7392 person-years against 279 in 158,891; relative risk 1.85, 95% CI 1.08 to 3.16); TAP Pharmaceuticals co-funded the study [@choi-2008-gout].
:::

:::consensus
WHO strongly recommends free sugars below 10% of energy, on moderate-quality evidence about dental caries, and conditionally below 5% [@who-2015-sugars]. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) could not set a tolerable upper intake level and advises intake of added and free sugars as low as possible within a nutritionally adequate diet [@turck-2022-sugars].
:::

:::unresolved
Effects of fructose beyond its energy are unsettled: in trials reviewed by EFSA, effects of free fructose and glucose on weight, liver fat and blood lipids did not appear to differ, but fructose appeared to raise hepatic insulin resistance and uric acid more [@turck-2022-sugars]. Large long-term trials of added sugars on cardiometabolic risk factors are lacking [@hannou-2018-fructose].
:::
