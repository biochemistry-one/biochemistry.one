## What it is

Egg white is mostly water and protein; the yolk holds nearly all the fat, cholesterol, choline and yellow pigments [@fukagawa-2022-eggs].

Per 100 g, whole raw egg contains 372 mg cholesterol, 294 mg choline and 503 µg lutein plus zeaxanthin; the white contains no cholesterol, lutein or zeaxanthin [@fukagawa-2022-eggs]. A large egg weighs 50 g and supplies 186 mg cholesterol and 147 mg choline [@fukagawa-2022-eggs]. EFSA's Adequate Intake of choline for adults is 400 mg a day [@efsa-2016-choline].

## In the body

Gut cells take up egg cholesterol through a transporter; blood cholesterol rises more in some people than others [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb; @katan-1986-responders].

NPC1L1, an apical enterocyte protein with a sterol-sensing domain, is critical for [cholesterol](/metabolism/cholesterol/) uptake [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb; @brown-2018-scap].

::pathway{id="dietary-cholesterol-absorption"}

When egg yolk raised Dutch volunteers' cholesterol intake from 129 to 989 mg a day, serum cholesterol rose by 0.82 mmol/L in 17 previously selected hyper-responders and 0.47 mmol/L in 15 hypo-responders [@katan-1986-responders]. The differences were reproducible but modest: responses were unimodal, with a between-person standard deviation of 0.29 mmol/L [@katan-1986-responders].

Choline is a component of phospholipids and a precursor of acetylcholine and betaine, a methyl donor for homocysteine remethylation; dietary deficiency can cause fatty liver [@efsa-2016-choline]. Lutein and zeaxanthin travel in chylomicrons, then partly in HDL, and colour the macula lutea [@efsa-2010-lutein].

## What studies show

Egg cholesterol raises blood cholesterol modestly; long-term studies of large groups disagree on heart disease [@weggemans-2001-eggs; @zhong-2019-eggs; @drouin-chartier-2020-eggs].

In 17 controlled trials (556 participants, at least 14 days), each extra 100 mg cholesterol a day raised total cholesterol by 0.056 mmol/L (95% CI 0.046 to 0.065) and HDL cholesterol by 0.008 mmol/L [@weggemans-2001-eggs]. One extra egg a day, taken as 200 mg, was predicted to raise LDL cholesterol by 0.100 mmol/L [@weggemans-2001-eggs].

A review by a group promoting plant-based diets found egg- or poultry-industry funding in 0 of 9 studies from the 1950s and 25 of 42 from 2010–2019 [@barnard-2019-industry]. Most intervention studies reported net cholesterol rises, which conclusions called favourable in 49% of industry-funded and 13% of other studies [@barnard-2019-industry].

:::industry
In an American Egg Board-funded crossover trial in 51 premenopausal women, eggs adding 640 mg cholesterol a day for 30 days raised LDL and HDL cholesterol in hyper-responders; the authors judged the profile not more atherogenic because the LDL/HDL ratio was maintained [@herron-2002-responders]. In another Board-supported crossover trial, one egg a day for 5 weeks raised serum lutein by 26% and zeaxanthin by 38% in 33 older adults without significant lipid change [@goodrow-2006-lutein].
:::

:::consensus
In 2020 the American Heart Association judged up to one egg a day acceptable for healthy people and up to two for older people with normal cholesterol, and advised caution with cholesterol-rich foods in dyslipidaemia [@carson-2020-aha].
:::

:::unresolved
In six pooled US cohorts (29,615 adults, median 17.5 years), each extra half egg a day was associated with incident cardiovascular disease (hazard ratio 1.06, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.10; absolute risk difference 1.11 percentage points at 31.3 years); with dietary cholesterol adjusted, the hazard ratio was 0.99 (0.93 to 1.05) [@zhong-2019-eggs]. In three cohorts of US health professionals (215,618 adults, up to 32 years), one or more eggs a day against less than one a month gave crude rates of 318 and 231 per 100,000 person-years and an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.93 (0.82 to 1.05) [@drouin-chartier-2020-eggs]. Diet was assessed every 2 to 4 years in the professional cohorts, once in the pooled cohorts [@drouin-chartier-2020-eggs; @zhong-2019-eggs].
:::
