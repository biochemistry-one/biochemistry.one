## What it is

Ultra-processed foods are industrial products made mostly from substances extracted or derived from foods and combined with additives [@monteiro-2019-nova; @lane-2024-umbrella].

The NOVA system sorts foods into four groups by processing rather than nutrient content [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Group 4 covers products containing a substance never or rarely used in kitchens, or an additive used to make them palatable or more appealing [@monteiro-2019-nova]. In a US national survey, ultra-processed foods supplied 57.5% of energy intake [@martinez-steele-2017-nutrient-quality].

## What happens in the body

In the NIH inpatient trial, people ate ultra-processed meals faster than unprocessed meals [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Eating rate was 17 ± 1 kcal/min faster [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Energy density, drinks excluded, was 1.957 against 1.057 kcal/g [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf].

Colonic bacteria ferment undigested polysaccharides to butyrate [@vital-2014-butyrate]. Butyrate is the main energy source of colonocytes [@vital-2014-butyrate].

In the US survey, fibre fell from 9.6 to 6.7 g per 1000 kcal between the lowest and highest fifths of ultra-processed share [@martinez-steele-2017-nutrient-quality].

## What studies show

In two independent feeding trials, adults gained weight or lost less on ultra-processed diets than on less processed diets [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf; @dicken-2025-update].

In the NIH trial, 20 adults ate each diet ad libitum for 14 days [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Meals were matched for presented energy, macronutrients, sugars, sodium and fibre [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Energy intake was 508 ± 106 kcal/day higher on the ultra-processed diet [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf]. Weight changed by +0.9 ± 0.3 kg on it and −0.9 ± 0.3 kg on the unprocessed diet [@hall-2019-inpatient-upf].

In the UPDATE trial, 55 adults received each diet for 8 weeks [@dicken-2025-update]. Both diets followed the UK Eatwell Guide [@dicken-2025-update]. Weight changed by −2.06% on the minimally processed diet and −1.05% on the ultra-processed diet [@dicken-2025-update]. The difference was −1.01 percentage points, or −0.96 kg [@dicken-2025-update].

An umbrella review covered 45 pooled observational analyses [@lane-2024-umbrella]. Convincing associations included those with cardiovascular mortality (risk ratio 1.50) and type 2 diabetes (1.12 per 10% increment of intake) [@lane-2024-umbrella]. By GRADE, 4 analyses were of moderate, 22 of low and 19 of very low quality [@lane-2024-umbrella].

In US cohorts, 114,064 health professionals were followed for a median of 31 to 34 years [@fang-2024-mortality]. All-cause mortality was 1536 deaths per 100,000 person-years in the highest quarter of intake and 1472 in the lowest [@fang-2024-mortality]. The difference was 64 per 100,000 person-years (hazard ratio 1.04, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.07) [@fang-2024-mortality].

:::industry
In a crossover trial, 41 adults ate two ultra-processed diets of different texture [@forde-2026-eating-rate]. The diets were matched for palatability and non-beverage energy density [@forde-2026-eating-rate]. Daily energy intake was 369 kcal lower on the diet eaten more slowly [@forde-2026-eating-rate]. Body weight did not differ [@forde-2026-eating-rate].
:::

:::consensus
A 2026 European Society of Cardiology consensus statement advises cardiologists to assess ultra-processed food intake [@guasti-2026-esc-consensus]. The 2025 US Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee graded as limited the evidence linking diets higher in ultra-processed food with greater adiposity [@dgac-2024-upf].
:::

:::unresolved
Independent analyses conflict on whether processing adds risk beyond diet quality [@fang-2024-mortality; @lane-2024-umbrella]. In the US cohorts, adjustment for diet quality attenuated the mortality association toward null [@fang-2024-mortality]. In a meta-analysis cited by the umbrella review, adjustment for diet quality left the associations unchanged [@lane-2024-umbrella].

In a meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials, the standardised mean difference in energy intake was 0.71 with more energy-dense ultra-processed diets and 0.02 with similar energy density [@robinson-2026-rct-meta]. Randomised trials of reduced intake on hard cardiovascular endpoints are lacking [@guasti-2026-esc-consensus].
:::
