## What it is

Red meat is unprocessed mammalian muscle, such as beef, pork or lamb; processed meat has been salted, cured, fermented or smoked [@turesky-2018-meat-mechanisms].

Potassium or sodium nitrite (E 249, E 250) is added in curing to fix colour and inhibit microbial growth [@mortensen-2017-nitrite].

## In the body

Substances in meat, or formed when it is cured or cooked hot, can damage DNA [@turesky-2018-meat-mechanisms].

Haem catalyses nitrosation and lipid peroxidation in the gut [@turesky-2018-meat-mechanisms]. In 21 volunteers given 420 g of red meat daily for 15 to 21 days, against a vegetarian diet, faecal N-nitroso compounds rose from 193 to 667 μg/day and O6-carboxymethylguanine-positive colonocytes from 22.7% to 55.7% [@lewin-2006-o6cmg].

Meat and meat products are the main dietary source of carcinogenic N-nitrosamines; EFSA judges 95th-percentile intake a health concern [@schrenk-2023-nitrosamines].

Heterocyclic aromatic amines such as PhIP form in meat cooked at high temperature [@bellamri-2021-haa]. Hepatic [CYP1A2](/metabolism/drug-metabolism/) N-hydroxylates PhIP; NAT2 or SULT1A1 esterifies the product, which forms the DNA adduct dG-C8-PhIP [@bellamri-2021-haa; @uniprot-2025-meat].

::pathway{id="phip-bioactivation"}

Gut bacteria cleave carnitine, abundant in red meat, to trimethylamine, which hepatic FMO3 oxidises to trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) [@zhu-2014-carnitine; @wang-2019-tmao; @uniprot-2025-meat]. In 113 adults, plasma TMAO rose about threefold after 4 weeks of a red-meat diet against white-meat or meat-free diets [@wang-2019-tmao].

::pathway{id="carnitine-tmao"}

## What studies show

People who eat more red and processed meat have slightly higher rates of heart disease, bowel cancer and death [@zhong-2020-meat-cvd; @bradbury-2020-colorectal].

In six US cohorts (29,682 adults, median 19 years), each 2 extra weekly servings (2 against 0 for processed meat and cardiovascular disease) were associated with 30-year absolute risk increases in cardiovascular disease and death of 0.62 and 0.76 percentage points for unprocessed red meat (hazard ratios 1.03 each) and 1.74 and 0.90 for processed meat (1.07 and 1.03) [@zhong-2020-meat-cvd].

In UK Biobank (mean 5.7 years), colorectal cancer occurred in 274 of 68,359 people (0.40%) eating red and processed meat less than twice a week and 1209 of 192,600 (0.63%) eating it four or more times (adjusted hazard ratio 1.20, 95% CI 1.04 to 1.37), an association confined to men [@bradbury-2020-colorectal].

:::industry
In a meta-analysis of 27 cohorts, partly funded by Meat & Livestock Australia, high against low red meat intake gave a relative risk of colorectal cancer of 1.11 (1.03 to 1.19); the authors concluded that red meat is not an independent predictor [@alexander-2015-colorectal].
:::

:::consensus
In 2015 the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classified processed meat as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), by majority on sufficient evidence for colorectal cancer, and red meat as probably carcinogenic (Group 2A), on limited human and strong mechanistic evidence [@bouvard-2015-iarc]. A cited meta-analysis of ten cohorts gave relative risks of 1.17 (1.05 to 1.31) per 100 g/day of red meat and 1.18 (1.10 to 1.28) per 50 g/day of processed meat [@bouvard-2015-iarc].

In 2019 the NutriRECS panel suggested continuing current consumption of both (weak recommendations, low-certainty evidence) [@johnston-2019-nutrirecs]. For 3 fewer weekly servings of unprocessed red meat it estimated 1 to 6 fewer cardiometabolic events per 1000 over 10.8 years and 7 fewer lifetime cancer deaths per 1000 [@johnston-2019-nutrirecs].
:::

:::unresolved
IARC judged chance, bias and confounding unlikely explanations for processed meat; NutriRECS declined to upgrade the cohort evidence for dose–response, citing possible residual confounding [@bouvard-2015-iarc; @johnston-2019-nutrirecs].

The guideline declared no primary funding source [@johnston-2019-nutrirecs]. A Texas A&M AgriLife Research grant and earlier funding from the International Life Sciences Institute (ILSI) North America were later added to the lead author's disclosures [@annals-2020-nutrirecs-correction]. An industry-supported ILSI committee funded his 2017 review of sugar guidelines [@erickson-2017-guidelines].

Whether TMAO contributes to cardiovascular disease in humans is unclear [@wang-2019-tmao].
:::
