## What they are

Palm oil comes from the pulp of the oil palm fruit; about half its fat is saturated [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl; @fukagawa-2022-palm-oil]. Seed oils such as soybean, canola and corn oil supply linoleic acid, an omega-6 fat [@fukagawa-2022-seed-oils].

| Oil | Per 100 g |
|---|---|
| Palm | 43.5 g palmitic (16:0), 4.3 g stearic (18:0), 36.6 g oleic (cis-18:1 n-9), 9.1 g linoleic (18:2 n-6) and 15.9 mg α-tocopherol [@fukagawa-2022-palm-oil] |
| Soybean | 50.4 g linoleic, 6.79 g α-linolenic (18:3 n-3) [@fukagawa-2022-seed-oils] |
| Canola | 18.6 g linoleic, 9.14 g α-linolenic [@fukagawa-2022-seed-oils] |
| Corn | 53.2 g linoleic, 1.16 g α-linolenic [@fukagawa-2022-seed-oils] |

Carotenoids, chiefly α- and β-carotene, colour crude palm oil orange-red [@souganidis-2013-red-palm-oil].

## In the body

Digestion frees palm oil's fatty acids, which gut cells repackage into fat particles [@yen-2015-triacylglycerol]. The body cannot make linoleic or α-linolenic acid and converts little of either into longer fatty acids [@efsa-2010-dietary-fats].

In palm oil, most palmitic acid is esterified at the sn-1 and sn-3 positions [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl]. Colipase-anchored pancreatic lipase hydrolyses these esters, leaving sn-2-monoacylglycerol [@uniprot-2025-knowledgebase].

::pathway{id="monoacylglycerol-pathway"}

Humans lack Δ12- and Δ15-desaturases [@efsa-2010-dietary-fats]. Under 1% of deuterated linoleic acid became arachidonic acid in one study [@efsa-2010-dietary-fats]. Conversion of α-linolenic acid is estimated at 8–12% to eicosapentaenoic and possibly under 1% to docosahexaenoic acid; more dietary linoleic acid lowers it [@efsa-2010-dietary-fats].

In 36 studies of adults on Western-type diets, lowering dietary linoleic acid by up to 90% or raising it up to sixfold was not significantly correlated with changes in plasma phospholipid arachidonic acid [@rett-2011-linoleic-arachidonic].

::pathway{id="linoleic-acid-to-prostaglandin-h2"}

Prostaglandin G/H synthases, the targets of acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), convert arachidonate to prostaglandin H2, precursor of 2-series prostaglandins and thromboxanes [@uniprot-2025-eicosanoids].

## What studies show

In feeding trials, palm oil raised LDL cholesterol more than oils low in saturated fat [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl]. In 27 such trials (807 participants, 2 to 16 weeks), the difference was 0.24 mmol/L (95% CI 0.13 to 0.35), and 0.31 mmol/L (0.20 to 0.42) in randomised trials [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl].

In Burkina Faso, 24 schools were randomised to usual lunches, one 60 mg vitamin A capsule, or 15 ml red palm oil three times a week [@zeba-2006-red-palm-oil]. Over 12 months, serum retinol below 0.70 µmol/L fell from 40.4% to 14.9% of 114 pupils on the oil, 46.1% to 17.1% of 117 on the capsule and 23.6% to 14.2% of 106 controls [@zeba-2006-red-palm-oil]. Controls had higher baseline retinol; endline retinol did not differ significantly [@zeba-2006-red-palm-oil].

Seed oils in place of saturated fat lower blood cholesterol, but trials have not shown fewer deaths [@hooper-2018-omega-6; @hooper-2020-saturated-fat; @ramsden-2016-minnesota-coronary]. In the Sydney Diet Heart Study, 458 men after a coronary event were randomised to safflower oil and safflower-oil margarine instead of saturated fat, or to no specific dietary instruction [@ramsden-2013-sydney-diet-heart]. Over a median 39 months, 17.6% of 221 against 11.8% of 237 died (hazard ratio 1.62, 95% CI 1.00 to 2.64; number needed to harm 17) [@ramsden-2013-sydney-diet-heart].

The Minnesota Coronary Experiment randomised 9423 residents of mental hospitals and a nursing home to corn oil and corn-oil margarine instead of saturated fat, or to a control diet [@ramsden-2016-minnesota-coronary]. Serum cholesterol fell 13.8% against 1.0% in the 2355 fed the diets for a year or more, but recovered life tables showed no mortality benefit [@ramsden-2016-minnesota-coronary].

In a Cochrane review of 19 trials of more omega-6 fat (6461 participants, 1 to 8 years), mortality was 167 per 1000 in both arms (risk ratio 1.00, 0.88 to 1.12; 10 trials) [@hooper-2018-omega-6]. In another, polyunsaturated instead of saturated fat for at least 2 years gave risk ratios of 0.79 (0.62 to 1.00; 8 trials) for cardiovascular events and 0.96 (0.82 to 1.13; 7 trials) for death [@hooper-2020-saturated-fat].

In cohorts (median follow-up 4.9 to 30.2 years), the highest linoleic acid intake was associated with lower mortality than the lowest (relative risk 0.87, 0.81 to 0.94; 11 studies, 170,076 deaths) [@li-2020-linoleic-mortality].

:::industry
In a meta-analysis of 51 trials (1526 volunteers, 2 to 16 weeks), palm oil raised LDL cholesterol by 10.75 mg/dL against diets rich in monounsaturated fat [@fattore-2014-palm-oil]. The Malaysian Palm Oil Board supported 19 trials, in which cholesterol changes were smaller than in others [@fattore-2014-palm-oil]. Soremartec Italia, with a financial interest in products containing palm oil, co-funded the analysis [@fattore-2014-palm-oil].

Unilever partly funded a pooled analysis of 30 cohorts (68,659 participants; median follow-up 2.5 to 31.9 years) [@marklund-2019-omega-6]. Higher linoleic acid biomarkers were associated with lower cardiovascular mortality (hazard ratio per interquintile range 0.78, 0.70 to 0.85) [@marklund-2019-omega-6].
:::

:::consensus
A 2017 American Heart Association advisory lists palm oil among the main sources of saturated fat to decrease [@sacks-2017-dietary-fats]. It pooled four trials it judged core: polyunsaturated vegetable oil, mainly soybean oil, instead of saturated fat lowered coronary heart disease (relative risk 0.71, 0.61 to 0.83) [@sacks-2017-dietary-fats].
:::

:::unresolved
In the independent analysis, smaller doses explained weaker industry-funded results [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl]. In the co-funded one, board funding was closely related to Asian settings, young normocholesterolaemic volunteers and lower-fat diets [@fattore-2014-palm-oil]. Data on palm oil and cardiovascular disease are sparse [@sun-2015-palm-oil-ldl].

The advisory excluded the Sydney trial for a margarine it describes as high in trans fat [@sacks-2017-dietary-fats]. The reanalysts state that trans fat was not recorded [@ramsden-2013-sydney-diet-heart].

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) found human data insufficient to recommend an omega-6 to omega-3 ratio independent of absolute intakes [@efsa-2010-dietary-fats].
:::

## Processing

Refining makes palm oil nearly colourless and forms contaminants from its fat [@souganidis-2013-red-palm-oil; @efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl].

Conventional refining, with high-temperature deodorisation, removes some tocopherols and tocotrienols and destroys all carotenoids; a milder patented process gives red palm oil with 80% of them [@souganidis-2013-red-palm-oil]. Glycidyl esters form from diacylglycerols, up to 12% of palm oil, during deodorisation above 200 °C; 3-monochloropropanediol (3-MCPD) esters form on refining [@efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl]. Palm oil averaged 2,912 µg/kg 3-MCPD and 3,955 µg/kg glycidol from esters, rapeseed oil 232 and 166 µg/kg [@efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl]. In animals, ingested esters are extensively hydrolysed; glycidol, an epoxide, caused tumours at several sites in rats and mice, probably by genotoxicity [@efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl].

Only high consumers in younger age groups slightly exceed the EFSA tolerable daily intake for 3-MCPD and its esters, 2 µg/kg body weight [@knutsen-2018-3-mcpd]. For glycidol, margins of exposure below 25,000 indicate a health concern; for infants fed only formula, the margin was 2,100 at 95th-percentile occurrence [@efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl].
