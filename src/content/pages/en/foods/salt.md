## What it is

Table salt consists mainly of sodium chloride [@turck-2019-sodium].

Per 100 g, US iodised table salt averages 38.7 g sodium and 5080 µg iodine [@fukagawa-2022-salt].

## In the body

The kidneys filter over 500 g of sodium a day and return almost all of it to the blood [@palmer-2015-nephron].

The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase exports 3 Na⁺ and imports 2 K⁺ per ATP hydrolysed [@gadsby-2009-pumps]. It powers tubular sodium reabsorption and accounts for two-thirds or more of renal oxygen uptake [@palmer-2015-nephron]. The proximal tubule reabsorbs 60–70% of filtered sodium (mainly NHE3), the thick ascending limb 25–30% (NKCC2, NHE3) and the distal nephron at most 5–10% (NCC, ENaC), which matches excretion to intake [@palmer-2015-nephron].

Renin, released when afferent arteriolar pressure or distal tubular sodium chloride falls, cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I; angiotensin-converting enzyme then forms angiotensin II [@turck-2019-sodium].

::pathway{id="renin-angiotensin"}

Angiotensin II stimulates proximal sodium reabsorption, arteriolar constriction and aldosterone release; aldosterone stimulates distal sodium recovery in exchange for potassium [@turck-2019-sodium].

The sodium–iodide symporter (*SLC5A5*), driven by the Na⁺ gradient of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, moves one I⁻ with two Na⁺ into thyroid cells [@ravera-2017-nis].

## What studies show

Eating less sodium lowers blood pressure [@sacks-2001-dash-sodium].

In DASH-Sodium, 412 adults were fed a typical US or DASH diet at three sodium levels, 30 days each [@sacks-2001-dash-sodium]. From high to low sodium, systolic pressure fell by 6.7 mm Hg on the typical diet and 3.0 mm Hg on DASH [@sacks-2001-dash-sodium].

In SSaSS, 600 rural Chinese villages (20,995 people with prior stroke, or aged 60 or more with poorly controlled blood pressure) were randomised to a 75% sodium chloride, 25% potassium chloride substitute or regular salt [@neal-2021-ssass]. Over a mean 4.74 years, stroke rates per 1000 person-years were 29.14 against 33.65, 4.51 fewer (rate ratio 0.86, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.96) [@neal-2021-ssass]. Per 1000 person-years, death rates were 39.28 against 44.61 (0.88, 0.82 to 0.95), and rates of serious adverse events attributed to hyperkalaemia 3.35 against 3.30 [@neal-2021-ssass]. Australia's National Health and Medical Research Council funded the trial; a manufacturer donated the substitute in years 3–4 [@neal-2021-ssass].

In a Cochrane review of six controlled studies, iodine status improved with iodised salt in most, and goitre tended to decrease [@wu-2002-iodised-salt].

:::industry
In PURE, 101,945 adults in 17 countries were followed for a mean 3.7 years; sodium excretion was estimated from one morning urine sample [@odonnell-2014-pure]. Death or major cardiovascular events occurred in 4.3% of 10,810 below 3.00 g/day, 3.1% of 46,663 at 4.00–5.99 g/day (reference) and 3.3% of 11,017 at 7.00 g/day or more (adjusted odds ratios 1.27 and 1.15) [@odonnell-2014-pure]. Funders included seven pharmaceutical companies, the Unilever Health Institute and the South Africa Sugar Association; the authors report no funder role [@odonnell-2014-pure].
:::

:::consensus
WHO strongly recommends less than 2 g sodium (5 g salt) a day for adults [@who-2012-sodium]. It conditionally suggests potassium-containing substitutes for table salt, except in children, pregnancy and impaired potassium excretion [@who-2025-salt-substitutes]. It recommends iodising all food-grade salt, judging this compatible with salt reduction [@who-2014-salt-iodine].
:::

:::unresolved
PURE's J-shaped association is disputed [@odonnell-2014-pure; @cook-2016-tohp-mortality]. In TOHP (2974 adults, 272 deaths), sodium averaged over up to seven 24-hour urines was related to death linearly over a median 24 years (hazard ratio 1.12 per 1000 mg/day, 1.00 to 1.26) [@cook-2016-tohp-mortality]. With Kawasaki-formula estimates from the same urines, intake was overestimated by 1297 mg/day and the relation appeared J-shaped [@he-2018-kawasaki].

People at known risk from extra potassium were excluded from all 26 salt-substitute trials in a Cochrane review [@brand-2022-salt-substitutes]. Child development and mortality were not assessed in the iodised-salt studies [@wu-2002-iodised-salt].
:::
