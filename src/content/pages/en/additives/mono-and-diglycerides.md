## What it is

Mono- and diglycerides are glycerol bound to one or two fatty acids; ordinary fat has three [@younes-2017-glycerides; @yen-2015-triacylglycerol].

E471 (INS 471), an emulsifier, is a mixture of glycerol mono-, di- and triesters of fatty acids, usually made by glycerolysis of fats and oils, which may be hydrogenated [@ec-2023-e471; @nhc-2024-gb2760; @younes-2017-glycerides]. It is used mainly in desserts, ice cream, bread and margarine [@younes-2017-glycerides].

## How often it is used

E471 is the tenth most frequent additive in French packaged foods, listed in 6,060 of 126,556 products in 2019 [@chazelas-2020-additives].

Of 106,489 adults, 78.07% consumed it, at a cohort mean of 160.8 mg/day [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

Digestive enzymes very likely split E471 into glycerol and fatty acids [@younes-2017-glycerides].

Enterocytes re-esterify absorbed fatty acids to triacylglycerol, mostly by the monoacylglycerol pathway [@yen-2015-triacylglycerol]. EFSA's mean adult intake estimates equal 0.8 to 3.5% of reference fat intake [@younes-2017-glycerides].

In one donor's faecal cultures, several measures were altered irreversibly by glyceryl stearate and one or none by two other E471 products [@naimi-2021-emulsifiers].

## What studies show

In a French cohort, higher E471 intake was linked to slightly more cancer [@sellem-2024-cancer]; see also [emulsifiers](/additives/emulsifiers/).

Among 92,000 adults over a mean 6.7 years, hazard ratios, highest against lowest third of intake, were 1.15 (95% CI 1.04 to 1.27) overall, 1.24 (1.03 to 1.51) for breast and 1.46 (1.09 to 1.97) for prostate cancer; modelled absolute risk at age 60 was 12.4% against 11.2% [@sellem-2024-cancer]. E471 was not associated with type 2 diabetes over a mean 6.8 years [@salame-2024-diabetes].

EFSA judged a numerical acceptable daily intake unnecessary; JECFA set one "not limited" in 1973 [@younes-2017-glycerides].

Above 200 °C, diglycerides form glycidyl esters, hydrolysed in the gut to glycidol, a genotoxic carcinogen in rodents [@younes-2017-glycerides; @efsa-2016-mcpd-glycidyl].

:::industry
An emulsifier manufacturers' association reported up to 111.25 mg/kg glycidyl esters, as glycidol, and up to 59.92% trans fatty acids where fats were partially hydrogenated [@younes-2021-glycerides].
:::

:::unresolved
Crude cancer counts were 790 of 30,667 and 796 of 30,604 in the lowest and highest thirds [@sellem-2024-cancer]. Over a median 7.4 years, cardiovascular disease was associated with E471 plus E472 esters (1.07 per SD, 1.04 to 1.11) but not with E471 alone [@sellem-2023-cardiovascular]. Intakes were estimated as doses, from diets of 2009–2021, before the EU limits [@sellem-2024-cancer; @ec-2023-e471]. Mechanisms are yet to be elucidated [@sellem-2024-cancer].
:::

## Regulation

Four jurisdictions permit E471 [@younes-2017-glycerides; @fda-2026-mono-diglycerides; @mhlw-2024-jsfa; @nhc-2024-gb2760].

| Jurisdiction | Rule |
|---|---|
| European Union | Quantum satis in most food categories; since January 2024, glycidyl esters at most 5 mg/kg as glycidol [@younes-2017-glycerides; @ec-2023-e471] |
| United States | Generally recognised as safe, limited by good manufacturing practice [@fda-2026-mono-diglycerides] |
| Japan | Designated, within glycerol esters of fatty acids [@mhlw-2024-jsfa] |
| China | As production requires, except in 59 listed food categories [@nhc-2024-gb2760] |
