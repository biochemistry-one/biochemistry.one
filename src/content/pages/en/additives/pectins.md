## What it is

Pectins are gelling substances from plants, extracted mainly from citrus peel and apple pomace [@mortensen-2017-pectins].

Pectin (E 440i) consists mainly of partial methyl esters of α-1,4-polygalacturonic acid; in amidated pectin (E 440ii), up to 25% of carboxyl groups are amides; both are INS 440 [@mortensen-2017-pectins; @younes-2021-pectins]. They gel, thicken and stabilise fermented milks, drinks and jams [@mortensen-2017-pectins; @fda-2025-pectins].

## How often it is used

Pectins rank ninth among additives in French foods [@chazelas-2020-additives].

In 2019, 6,504 of 126,556 products listed them [@chazelas-2020-additives]; 78.98% of 106,489 NutriNet-Santé adults consumed them, a mean 199.74 mg/day [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

Pectins reach the large intestine mostly intact, where bacteria ferment nearly all of them [@mortensen-2017-pectins].

In 7 men, 88.4% of 5 g reached the terminal ileum; faeces held 3.5–4.8% of intake [@mortensen-2017-pectins]. Cultured *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* degrades homogalacturonan to galacturonate, which *Escherichia coli* enzymes convert to pyruvate [@luis-2018-pectin; @uniprot-2025-pectin].

Fermentation yields short-chain fatty acids, including butyrate; pectins are dietary fibre [@mortensen-2017-pectins; @efsa-2010-carbohydrates; @vital-2014-butyrate].

## What studies show

Pectin at 12 to 15 g/day lowered blood cholesterol 5 to 8%; at 36 g/day for 6 weeks, some of 5 men reported abdominal distension and flatus, judged not adverse [@mortensen-2017-pectins].

A NutriNet-Santé additive mixture including pectin was associated with type 2 diabetes, hazard ratio 1.08 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.15) per standard deviation; over a mean 7.7 years, 465 of 36,214 (1.28%) in the top third and 260 of 36,215 (0.72%) in the bottom third developed it [@payen-de-la-garanderie-2025-mixtures].

EFSA set no numerical acceptable daily intake; brand-loyal toddlers reached 442 mg/kg body weight/day at the 95th percentile [@mortensen-2017-pectins]. Infants under 16 weeks fed special medical formulae at the former 10,000 mg/kg limit would receive 2,000–2,600 mg/kg/day, above the piglet no-adverse-effect level of 1,069 mg/kg/day, and possibly harmful methanol [@younes-2021-pectins].

:::industry
In Cargill-funded trials with two Cargill authors, 15 g/day of 70%-esterified citrus or apple pectin for 4 weeks lowered LDL cholesterol 7–10% [@brouns-2012-pectin].
:::

:::consensus
EFSA substantiated claims for cholesterol from 6 g/day and glycaemia from 10 g per meal, not satiety [@efsa-2010-pectins].
:::

:::unresolved
In a meta-analysis stating no funding, 2–10 g/day soluble fibre lowered LDL cholesterol 0.057 mmol/L per gram, pectin like oat and psyllium [@brown-1999-fibre]. Two studies of undetermined funding found 5.2–15 g slowed gastric emptying [@di-lorenzo-1988-pectin; @sanaka-2007-pectin]. Cited cholesterol trials used 2.2 to 15 g/day; mean additive intake was 199.74 mg/day [@brown-1999-fibre; @brouns-2012-pectin; @chazelas-2021-mixtures].
:::

## Regulation

All four jurisdictions permit pectins [@ec-2011-additives; @fda-2025-pectins; @jfcrf-2025-existing-additives; @nhc-2024-gb2760].

| Jurisdiction | Rule |
|---|---|
| European Union | Quantum satis; 4,000 mg/kg in special medical foods for infants and young children from 18 August 2026 [@ec-2011-additives; @ec-2026-hydrocolloids] |
| United States | Generally recognised as safe, amidated pectins included [@fda-2025-pectins] |
| Japan | Existing additive [@jfcrf-2025-existing-additives] |
| China | As production requires, except listed foods including infant formulae; juices up to 3.0 g/kg [@nhc-2024-gb2760] |
