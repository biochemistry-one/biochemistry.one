## What it is

Diphosphates are phosphate salts that make dough rise and keep acidity stable, widely used in biscuits and cakes [@younes-2019-phosphates; @chazelas-2020-additives].

E 450 comprises disodium (i), trisodium (ii), tetrasodium (iii), tetrapotassium (v), dicalcium (vi), calcium dihydrogen (vii) and magnesium dihydrogen (ix) salts of diphosphoric acid, also called pyrophosphates; INS numbers are identical [@younes-2019-phosphates].

## How often it is used

In April 2019, 6,529 of 126,556 French packaged foods listed diphosphates, eighth among additives [@chazelas-2020-additives].

Of 106,489 NutriNet-Santé adults, 64.36% consumed them, mean 178.33 mg/day; 0.02% exceeded the acceptable daily intake [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

In the gut, diphosphates become ordinary phosphate, mostly absorbed, then excreted in urine [@younes-2019-phosphates].

In rats, dietary tetrasodium diphosphate was almost completely hydrolysed; in humans, 80–90% of additive phosphorus is absorbed as orthophosphate [@younes-2019-phosphates]. Proximal tubules reabsorb 80% or more of filtered phosphorus, regulated by parathyroid hormone and FGF23 [@younes-2019-phosphates].

## What studies show

In short human studies, extra phosphate raised a phosphate-regulating hormone and briefly impaired artery widening [@gutierrez-2015-additives; @shuto-2009-endothelium].

In 10 adults, a week of additive-rich food (606 mg/day more phosphorus, 58 mmol/day more sodium) raised FGF23 by 23% against a low-additive week [@gutierrez-2015-additives]. In 11 men, flow-mediated dilation 2 h after 1200 mg phosphorus was 5.02% against 8.42% after 400 mg [@shuto-2009-endothelium]. In 279 hyperphosphataemic haemodialysis patients, cluster-randomised advice to avoid phosphate additives changed serum phosphorus by −0.6 mg/dL (95% CI −1.0 to −0.1) against usual care over 3 months [@sullivan-2009-additives].

In NutriNet-Santé, diphosphate intake was not associated with cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes [@sellem-2023-cardiovascular; @salame-2024-diabetes]. Over a mean 6.7 years, premenopausal breast cancer occurred in 113 of 19,382 people in the top intake third and 61 of 16,560 in the bottom (HR 1.45, 1.04 to 2.02; modelled risk at age 40, 2.5% against 1.7%) [@sellem-2024-cancer].

EFSA's group acceptable daily intake for phosphates, 40 mg phosphorus/kg body weight/day, counts all dietary phosphorus and does not apply in moderate to severe renal impairment; estimated whole-diet intake exceeded it at the mean in infants, toddlers and children [@younes-2019-phosphates].

:::consensus
KDIGO suggests limiting dietary phosphate for hyperphosphataemia in chronic kidney disease G3a–G5D (2D), considering its source, additives included (not graded) [@kdigo-2017-ckd-mbd].
:::

:::unresolved
After false-discovery-rate correction, the breast cancer trend and links of trisodium phosphate to coronary disease and tripotassium phosphate to diabetes lost significance [@sellem-2024-cancer; @sellem-2023-cardiovascular; @salame-2024-diabetes].
:::

## Regulation

All four jurisdictions permit diphosphates [@younes-2019-phosphates; @fda-2026-pyrophosphates; @ffcr-2024-designated-additives; @nhc-2024-gb2760].

| Jurisdiction | Rule |
|---|---|
| European Union | Phosphate group, 500 to 20,000 mg/kg as P₂O₅ or quantum satis (2019) [@younes-2019-phosphates] |
| United States | Sodium acid, tetrasodium and calcium pyrophosphate generally recognised as safe [@fda-2026-pyrophosphates] |
| Japan | Four E 450 salts designated [@ffcr-2024-designated-additives] |
| China | Phosphate group, 15.0 g/kg as PO₄³⁻ in bakery wares; excluded from infant formulae [@nhc-2024-gb2760] |
