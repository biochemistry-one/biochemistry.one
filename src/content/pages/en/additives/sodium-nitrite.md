## What it is

Sodium nitrite keeps cured meat pink and inhibits growth of the bacterium that causes botulism [@mortensen-2017-nitrite; @ec-2023-nitrites].

E 250 (INS 250) is NaNO2 [@mortensen-2017-nitrite; @nhc-2024-gb2760]. Sodium erythorbate, when added, speeds nitrite reduction to nitric oxide [@chazelas-2020-additives].

## How often it is used

Sodium nitrite was listed on 7,144 of 126,556 French packaged foods in 2019, seventh among additives [@chazelas-2020-additives].

Of 106,489 French adults, 73.93% consumed it, population mean 0.28 mg/day; 0.45%, or 0.61% of consumers, exceeded the acceptable daily intake [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

Swallowed nitrite is absorbed almost completely, then excreted mostly as urinary nitrate [@mortensen-2017-nitrite].

Oral bacteria reduce 5 to 36% of salivary nitrate to nitrite [@mortensen-2017-nitrite]. Nitrite oxidises haemoglobin to methaemoglobin, which impairs oxygen delivery [@mortensen-2017-nitrite]. In the stomach, nitrite-derived dinitrogen trioxide nitrosates secondary amines [@mortensen-2017-nitrite]. CYP2E1 α-hydroxylates N-nitrosodimethylamine (NDMA); the product decomposes to DNA-methylating methyldiazonium ions [@schrenk-2023-nitrosamines].

## What studies show

French adults eating more sodium nitrite developed prostate cancer and type 2 diabetes more often [@chazelas-2022-cancer; @srour-2023-diabetes].

Over a median 6.8 years, prostate cancer developed in 163 of 8157 high-intake men and 56 of 5456 non-consumers (hazard ratio 1.62, 95% CI 1.17 to 2.25); colorectal cancer in both sexes, 1.22 (0.85 to 1.75) [@chazelas-2022-cancer]. Over a median 7.3 years, type 2 diabetes cases were 450 in 268,950 person-years with higher exposure, 154 in 153,593 without (hazard ratio 1.54, 1.26 to 1.90) [@srour-2023-diabetes].

EFSA set the acceptable daily intake, 0.07 mg nitrite ion/kg body weight, by benchmark dose for doubled methaemoglobin in rats [@mortensen-2017-nitrite]. At that intake, NDMA formed in the body had a margin of exposure above 10,000; NDMA and NDEA preformed in meat, below 10,000 at high intake [@mortensen-2017-nitrite].

:::consensus
IARC rated ingested nitrate or nitrite "under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation" probably carcinogenic (Group 2A) [@iarc-2010-nitrate-nitrite], and processed meat carcinogenic (Group 1) [@bouvard-2015-iarc].
:::

:::unresolved
In both cohorts, intake was estimated from diet records without nitrite biomarkers [@chazelas-2022-cancer; @srour-2023-diabetes]. EFSA could not distinguish nitrosamines from added nitrite from those formed without it [@mortensen-2017-nitrite]. In a 1972 in-vitro study of undetermined funding, ascorbic acid blocked nitrosation of morpholine, piperazine and other compounds by nitrous acid [@mirvish-1972-ascorbate].
:::

## Regulation

Each jurisdiction limits it mainly to cured meat [@ec-2023-nitrites; @fsis-2026-curing; @mhlw-2024-jsfa; @nhc-2024-gb2760].

| Jurisdiction | Limit |
|---|---|
| EU | Since 9 October 2025, 80 mg/kg nitrite ion added to most meat products, 55 if sterilised [@ec-2023-nitrites] |
| US | 200 ppm in cured meat; pumped bacon 120 ppm with ascorbate or erythorbate [@fsis-2026-curing] |
| Japan | Only meat products, whale bacon, fish sausage and ham, and roe; nitrite ion residues ≤0.070 g/kg [@mhlw-2024-jsfa] |
| China | 0.15 g/kg in meat products; residues 30 to 70 mg/kg as sodium nitrite [@nhc-2024-gb2760] |
