## What it is

Citric acid occurs in many foods; lemon juice contains about 7% [@jecfa-1974-citric-acid].

It is 2-hydroxy-1,2,3-propanetricarboxylic acid, INS 330, used as an acidulant, antioxidant synergist, sequestrant and flavouring agent [@jecfa-2001-citric-acid]. Commercial production includes fermentation with *Aspergillus niger* [@efsa-2015-citric-acid; @fda-2026-citric-acid].

## How often it is used

Citric acid is the most frequent additive in French packaged foods [@chazelas-2020-additives].

In April 2019 it was listed in 18,302 of 126,556 products [@chazelas-2020-additives]. Between 2009 and 2020, 91.15% of 106,489 NutriNet-Santé adults consumed it, at a weighted mean of 1956.51 mg/day [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

Citrate is made in the body, and eaten citric acid is broken down to carbon dioxide and water [@jecfa-1974-citric-acid; @efsa-2015-citric-acid].

The sodium-coupled transporter SLC13A2 moves divalent citrate into intestinal and renal proximal tubule cells [@uniprot-2025-citrate]. Citrate is an intermediate of the mitochondrial citric acid cycle [@ragueneau-2026-glucose].

## What studies show

Teeth can be eroded by frequent or large intakes of citric acid [@jecfa-1974-citric-acid].

In a meta-analysis of nine paediatric studies, dental erosion was associated with soft drinks (odds ratio 2.41, 95% CI 2.03 to 2.85) and vitamin C (1.16, 1.10 to 1.22); citric acid itself was not analysed [@li-2012-erosion].

In NutriNet-Santé, a soft-drink additive mixture led by citric acid was associated with type 2 diabetes (hazard ratio 1.13, 1.08 to 1.18, per standard deviation of score) [@payen-de-la-garanderie-2025-mixtures]. Over a mean 7.7 years, diabetes was diagnosed in 384 of 36,214 adults in the top third and 355 of 36,215 in the bottom third [@payen-de-la-garanderie-2025-mixtures].

In 1973 JECFA set a group acceptable daily intake "not limited" for citric acid and its calcium, potassium, sodium and ammonium salts [@jecfa-2001-citric-acid].

:::unresolved
In ten men, in a fixed-order study, mean blood aluminium was 12 µg/L after an aluminium hydroxide antacid and 23 µg/L when lemon juice was added [@slanina-1986-aluminium]. In four case reports, fatigue and joint pain were linked to manufactured citric acid, with *Aspergillus niger* residues proposed as the cause [@sweis-2018-citric-acid]. Both have undetermined funding [@slanina-1986-aluminium; @sweis-2018-citric-acid]. In commercial batches assessed by EFSA, malformin C, aflatoxins and ochratoxin A were undetectable [@efsa-2015-citric-acid].
:::

## Regulation

Citric acid is permitted in food in all four jurisdictions below [@ec-2011-additives; @fda-2026-citric-acid; @jfcrf-2024-designated-additives; @usda-2024-gb-2760].

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|---|---|
| EU | E330, Group I, quantum satis [@ec-2011-additives; @efsa-2015-citric-acid] |
| US | Generally recognised as safe; no limit beyond good manufacturing practice [@fda-2026-citric-acid] |
| Japan | Designated additive [@jfcrf-2024-designated-additives] |
| China | As production requires, except 61 categories such as pasteurised milk and raw meat [@usda-2024-gb-2760] |
