## What it is

Lecithins are fatty substances, mainly from soya, that keep oil and water mixed in food [@mortensen-2017-lecithins].

E322 (INS 322) comprises phosphatide mixtures; oil-free soya lecithin is 21% phosphatidylcholine [@mortensen-2017-lecithins].

## How often it is used

Lecithins are the second most common additive in French packaged food [@chazelas-2020-additives].

13,102 of 126,556 products listed them in 2019 [@chazelas-2020-additives]. Of 106,489 adults, 86.57% consumed them, cohort mean 53.94 mg/day [@chazelas-2021-mixtures].

## In the body

Choline is freed from lecithins in digestion [@mortensen-2017-lecithins].

Phospholipases hydrolyse phosphatidylcholine, which is absorbed intact or as lysophosphatidylcholine or choline [@mortensen-2017-lecithins]. Gut bacteria convert choline to trimethylamine, which hepatic FMO3 oxidises to trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) [@tang-2013-phosphatidylcholine; @uniprot-2025-meat]. A week of antibiotics nearly abolished TMAO formation from labelled phosphatidylcholine in 6 adults; withdrawal restored it [@tang-2013-phosphatidylcholine].

## What studies show

Higher blood TMAO was linked to heart attacks, strokes and deaths [@tang-2013-phosphatidylcholine; @heianza-2017-tmao].

Of 4007 angiography patients, 513 died or had a myocardial infarction or stroke within 3 years; the top TMAO quartile against the bottom gave hazard ratios of 2.54 (95% CI 1.96 to 3.28) and, fully adjusted, 1.43 (1.05 to 1.94) [@tang-2013-phosphatidylcholine]. Across clinical cohorts over 1 to 8 years, relative risks for high TMAO were 1.62 (1.45 to 1.80) for 3315 cardiovascular events among 19,256 participants, and 1.63 (1.36 to 1.95) for death in 12 of the articles [@heianza-2017-tmao]. In 95,442 French adults over a median 7.4 years, lecithin intake was not associated with cardiovascular disease [@sellem-2023-cardiovascular].

In atherosclerosis-prone mice, 1% dietary choline nearly tripled aortic lesions, and the increase was absent under antibiotics [@wang-2011-phosphatidylcholine]. EFSA set no numerical acceptable daily intake [@mortensen-2017-lecithins].

:::unresolved
Genetically predicted TMAO was not associated with coronary disease, stroke or type 2 diabetes, whereas TMAO was higher with genetic liability to type 2 diabetes and kidney disease; the instruments reached only suggestive significance [@jia-2019-tmao]. EFSA states that gut bacteria cannot cleave lecithin esters and so form no major amounts of trimethylamine; neither opinion mentions TMAO [@mortensen-2017-lecithins; @younes-2020-lecithins]. The challenge dose was 250 mg phosphatidylcholine with two eggs, against mean additive intake of 53.94 mg/day [@tang-2013-phosphatidylcholine; @chazelas-2021-mixtures].
:::

## Regulation

Four jurisdictions permit lecithins [@mortensen-2017-lecithins; @fda-2026-lecithin; @jfcrf-2025-existing-additives; @nhc-2024-gb2760].

| Jurisdiction | Rule |
|---|---|
| European Union | Quantum satis in most foods; 1,000 mg/L in infant formulae, no safety concern below 16 weeks [@mortensen-2017-lecithins; @younes-2020-lecithins] |
| United States | Generally recognised as safe under good manufacturing practice [@fda-2026-lecithin] |
| Japan | Five lecithins listed as existing additives [@jfcrf-2025-existing-additives] |
| China | Phospholipids, quantum satis outside listed foods such as milk and oils [@nhc-2024-gb2760] |
