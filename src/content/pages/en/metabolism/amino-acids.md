## Digestion and absorption

Digestion cuts food proteins into amino acids and chains of two or three, which the small intestine absorbs [@berg-2019-biochemistry; @uniprot-2025-knowledgebase].

The pancreas secretes its proteases as inactive zymogens [@berg-2019-biochemistry]. Mutations in SLC6A19, which encodes the neutral amino acid transporter B0AT1, cause Hartnup disorder [@kleta-2004-hartnup].

## Essential and non-essential amino acids

The body cannot make nine of the twenty amino acids in proteins, so they must come from food [@berg-2019-biochemistry].

They are histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine [@berg-2019-biochemistry].

:::consensus
A joint WHO/FAO/UNU expert consultation puts the safe adult protein intake at 0.83 g/kg body weight per day [@who-2007-protein].
:::

## Transamination and pyridoxal phosphate

Before most amino acids are broken down, enzymes that depend on a form of vitamin B6 move the amino group onto another molecule [@berg-2019-biochemistry].

Aminotransferases transfer the α-amino group to 2-oxoglutarate, forming glutamate and the corresponding 2-oxo acid [@berg-2019-biochemistry]. Their cofactor is pyridoxal 5′-phosphate (PLP) [@uniprot-2025-knowledgebase].

## Glutamate dehydrogenase

In mitochondria, one enzyme releases the collected amino groups from glutamate as ammonium [@uniprot-2025-knowledgebase].

Leucine stimulates insulin secretion in beta cells by allosterically activating this enzyme, GLUD1 [@stanley-1998-glud1]. Eight children with the hyperinsulinism–hyperammonaemia syndrome each had a heterozygous GLUD1 variant in the allosteric domain [@stanley-1998-glud1].

## The urea cycle

The liver converts ammonium, which is toxic to the brain, into urea, which is excreted [@uniprot-2025-knowledgebase].

::pathway{id="urea-cycle"}

Inherited urea cycle disorders occur in at least 1 in 35,000 to 1 in 51,946 live births [@simpson-2025-urea-cycle]. Sodium benzoate, sodium phenylacetate and [sodium phenylbutyrate](/medicines/sodium-phenylbutyrate/) allow excretion of nitrogen by alternative pathways [@simpson-2025-urea-cycle].

## Phenylketonuria and newborn screening

In phenylketonuria, the enzyme that turns phenylalanine into tyrosine is deficient [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency].

Untreated, the phenylalanine that accumulates causes lasting brain damage [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency]. Global prevalence is estimated at 1 in 23,930 live births [@hillert-2020-pku]. Newborn screening measures phenylalanine in dried blood spots collected 24 to 72 hours after birth [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency]. Treatment combines a phenylalanine-restricted diet with phenylalanine-free protein supplements [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency]. Between 25% and 50% of patients respond to [sapropterin](/medicines/sapropterin/), synthetic tetrahydrobiopterin [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency]. [Aspartame](/additives/aspartame/) is hydrolysed in the gut to phenylalanine [@efsa-2013-aspartame]. Its acceptable daily intake of 40 mg/kg body weight does not apply to people with phenylketonuria [@efsa-2013-aspartame]. When maternal blood phenylalanine stays above 360 µmol/L during pregnancy, more than 90% of the children have intellectual disability [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency].

:::consensus
The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics recommends lifelong treatment for everyone with untreated phenylalanine above 360 µmol/L [@smith-2025-acmg-pah].
:::

:::industry
In a 6-week trial sponsored by BioMarin, blood phenylalanine fell by 30% or more in 18 of 41 patients (44%) on sapropterin and 4 of 47 (9%) on placebo [@levy-2007-sapropterin]. The absolute difference was 35 percentage points, and the number needed to treat about 3 [@levy-2007-sapropterin].
:::

:::unresolved
Arnold and Vockley attribute the brain damage to phenylacetic, phenylpyruvic and phenyllactic acids and to deficiency of tyrosine and catecholamine neurotransmitters [@arnold-2025-pah-deficiency]. The European guideline authors call the precise pathogenesis of brain dysfunction unclear [@van-wegberg-2017-pku].
:::

## Carbon skeletons: glucose and ketone bodies

Without its nitrogen, the rest of an amino acid can become glucose or ketone bodies, or be oxidised for energy [@berg-2019-biochemistry].

Skeletons that yield pyruvate, 2-oxoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, fumarate or oxaloacetate can supply gluconeogenesis [@berg-2019-biochemistry]. From acetyl-CoA and acetoacetyl-CoA, mammals make ketone bodies but no net glucose [@berg-2019-biochemistry]. Only leucine and lysine are exclusively ketogenic [@berg-2019-biochemistry].
