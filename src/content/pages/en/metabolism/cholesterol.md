## What cholesterol is for

Cholesterol is a fat-like molecule that human cells need for their membranes and that the body converts into steroid hormones, bile acids and vitamin D [@goldstein-2015-century; @mitsche-2015-flux].

## Synthesis: the mevalonate pathway

Cells build cholesterol from acetyl-CoA, a two-carbon unit, in more than 30 enzymatic reactions [@goldstein-2015-century; @mitsche-2015-flux].

::pathway{id="mevalonate"}

HMG-CoA reductase, an enzyme of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane, catalyses the rate-limiting step [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb; @brown-2018-scap]. [Statins](/medicines/statins/) inhibit this reaction [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb].

## Sterol sensing: SREBP-2, SCAP and INSIG

Cells switch the genes for cholesterol synthesis and uptake on or off according to the cholesterol content of an internal membrane, the ER [@radhakrishnan-2008-threshold; @brown-2018-scap].

When ER cholesterol is low, SCAP transports the transcription factor SREBP-2 to the Golgi [@brown-2018-scap]. There, two proteases release a domain of SREBP-2 that activates the genes for the LDL receptor and the biosynthetic enzymes [@brown-2018-scap; @goldstein-2015-century]. When ER cholesterol rises, SCAP binds cholesterol and INSIG, and the complex stays in the ER [@brown-2018-scap]. In Chinese hamster ovary cells, SREBP-2 processing fell abruptly once ER cholesterol exceeded about 5 mol% of ER lipids [@radhakrishnan-2008-threshold]. In a model based on hamster and mouse experiments, statins lower hepatic ER cholesterol, activate SREBP-2, raise LDL receptors and lower plasma LDL [@goldstein-2015-century].

## Uptake: the LDL receptor

Cholesterol travels in the blood mainly inside low-density lipoprotein (LDL) particles, which cells take up through a surface receptor [@goldstein-2015-century; @uniprot-2025-uniprotkb].

Hepatic receptors clear most LDL [@goldstein-2015-century]. Heterozygotes for familial hypercholesterolaemia have half the functional receptors and about twice the normal LDL [@goldstein-2015-century].

## PCSK9

PCSK9, a protein released by the liver, lowers the number of LDL receptors on liver cells and so raises blood LDL [@goldstein-2015-century; @lagace-2006-pcsk9].

PCSK9 binds the LDL receptor, which is then degraded instead of returning to the cell surface [@lagace-2006-pcsk9; @goldstein-2015-century; @uniprot-2025-uniprotkb]. African Americans heterozygous for the nonsense mutation Y142X or C679X had 28% lower LDL and an 88% reduction in heart attacks [@goldstein-2015-century].

## Absorption: NPC1L1

Cells lining the small intestine absorb part of the cholesterol in the gut through a membrane protein, NPC1L1 [@brown-2018-scap; @uniprot-2025-uniprotkb].

## Disposal: bile acids and reverse cholesterol transport

Most tissues cannot break cholesterol down [@rader-2009-rct]. In reverse cholesterol transport, it returns on high-density lipoprotein (HDL) to the liver [@rader-2009-rct]. The liver secretes it into bile unchanged or as bile acids [@rader-2009-rct].

Conversion to bile acids accounts for about 90% of cholesterol breakdown [@russell-2009-bile-acids]. Cholesterol 7α-hydroxylase (*CYP7A1*) catalyses the first and rate-limiting step of the classic pathway [@russell-2009-bile-acids; @uniprot-2025-uniprotkb]. Bile acid-binding resins increase faecal bile acid excretion and lower plasma cholesterol [@russell-2009-bile-acids]; see [bile acid sequestrants](/medicines/bile-acid-sequestrants/).

:::industry
Employees of Merck and Schering-Plough, which co-market [ezetimibe](/medicines/ezetimibe/), identified NPC1L1 as its direct target [@garcia-calvo-2005-ezetimibe]. A genetic study was partly funded by an investigator-initiated grant from Merck [@stitziel-2014-npc1l1]. In it, carriers of one inactivating *NPC1L1* mutation had LDL cholesterol 12 mg/dl (0.31 mmol/l) lower than non-carriers [@stitziel-2014-npc1l1]. Their odds ratio for coronary heart disease was 0.47 (95% CI 0.25 to 0.87) [@stitziel-2014-npc1l1].
:::

:::consensus
The European Atherosclerosis Society Consensus Panel concluded in 2017 that LDL causes atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease [@ference-2017-ldl-causes]. In meta-analyses with more than 2 million participants, risk rose log-linearly with the absolute magnitude of exposure to LDL cholesterol [@ference-2017-ldl-causes].
:::

:::unresolved
Whether HDL protects human arteries through reverse cholesterol transport is not established [@goldstein-2015-century; @rader-2009-rct]. Carriers of the *LIPG* variant Asn396Ser had HDL cholesterol 0.14 mmol/l higher than non-carriers but no lower risk of myocardial infarction (odds ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.11) [@voight-2012-hdl]. Cholesterol efflux capacity is an ex vivo measure of one step of reverse cholesterol transport [@rohatgi-2014-efflux]. In the Dallas Heart Study, over a median 9.4 years, atherosclerotic cardiovascular events occurred in 49 of 602 participants (8.1%) in the lowest quartile of efflux capacity and 22 of 602 (3.7%) in the highest [@rohatgi-2014-efflux]. The unadjusted difference was 4.5 percentage points, and the adjusted hazard ratio 0.33 (0.19 to 0.55) [@rohatgi-2014-efflux].
:::
