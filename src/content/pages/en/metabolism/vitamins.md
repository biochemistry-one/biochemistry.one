## Coenzymes from B vitamins

Most B vitamins are converted into coenzymes, small molecules that particular enzymes require [@turck-2016-thiamin; @turck-2017-riboflavin; @efsa-2014-niacin; @efsa-2016-vitamin-b6].

| Vitamin | Coenzyme | Enzymes or reactions | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thiamine (B1) | Thiamine pyrophosphate | Pyruvate, 2-oxoglutarate and branched-chain 2-oxo acid dehydrogenases; transketolase [@turck-2016-thiamin] | Beriberi, Wernicke encephalopathy [@turck-2016-thiamin] |
| Riboflavin (B2) | FMN, FAD | Glutathione reductase, MTHFR [@turck-2017-riboflavin] | Ariboflavinosis: cheilosis, glossitis, anaemia [@turck-2017-riboflavin] |
| Niacin (B3) | NAD+, NADP+ | Dehydrogenases [@efsa-2014-niacin] | Pellagra [@efsa-2014-niacin] |
| Pantothenic acid (B5) | Coenzyme A | Acyl-group transfer [@efsa-2014-pantothenic-acid] | Rare [@efsa-2014-pantothenic-acid] |
| Vitamin B6 | Pyridoxal 5′-phosphate | Aminotransferases, decarboxylases, glycogen phosphorylase [@efsa-2016-vitamin-b6] | Microcytic anaemia, seizures [@efsa-2016-vitamin-b6] |
| Biotin (B7) | Enzyme-bound biotin | Acetyl-CoA, pyruvate, propionyl-CoA and β-methylcrotonyl-CoA carboxylases [@efsa-2014-biotin] | Dermatitis, hair loss, ataxia [@efsa-2014-biotin] |
| Folate (B9) | Tetrahydrofolate | One-carbon transfers for DNA bases and methionine [@ducker-2017-one-carbon] | Megaloblastic anaemia [@turck-2023-folate] |
| Vitamin B12 | Methylcobalamin, adenosylcobalamin | Methionine synthase, methylmalonyl-CoA mutase [@gherasim-2013-cobalamin] | Megaloblastic anaemia, myeloneuropathy [@turck-2023-folate] |

In the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, E1 decarboxylates pyruvate with thiamine pyrophosphate and reductively acetylates a lipoyl group of E2, E2 transfers the acetyl group to coenzyme A, and E3 reoxidises the dihydrolipoyl group with FAD and NAD+ [@patel-2014-pdh].

B12 deficiency traps folate as 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, as the [one-carbon chapter](/metabolism/one-carbon/) explains [@turck-2023-folate].

## Vitamin C and collagen

Humans must obtain vitamin C from food, and it is needed to make collagen, the main protein of skin, bone and connective tissue [@efsa-2013-vitamin-c].

Ascorbate keeps the metal ion of dioxygenases reduced, and the procollagen proline and lysine hydroxylases need it for maximal activity [@efsa-2013-vitamin-c]. Prolyl 4-hydroxylase, a non-haem iron enzyme, forms 4-hydroxyproline, which greatly raises the thermal stability of the collagen triple helix [@shoulders-2009-collagen]. In adults, scurvy develops below a plasma ascorbate of 10 µmol/L, with bleeding gums, bruising and poor wound healing; 10 mg daily prevents it [@efsa-2013-vitamin-c].

## Vitamin K and warfarin

Vitamin K is needed to activate several blood-clotting proteins, and [warfarin](/medicines/warfarin/) acts by blocking its recycling [@turck-2017-vitamin-k].

γ-Glutamyl carboxylase converts glutamate residues of factors II, VII, IX and X and proteins C, S and Z to γ-carboxyglutamate while oxidising vitamin K hydroquinone to the epoxide [@turck-2017-vitamin-k]. Vitamin K epoxide reductase reduces the epoxide to the quinone and then to the hydroquinone, and warfarin inhibits both steps [@liu-2021-vkor].

::pathway{id="vitamin-k-cycle"}

*VKORC1* encodes the reductase; its promoter variant c.-1639G>A lowers expression and the warfarin dose required [@dean-2026-warfarin]. Vitamin K deficiency prolongs the prothrombin time and causes bleeding [@turck-2017-vitamin-k]. Without prophylaxis, late vitamin K deficiency bleeding occurred in 4.4 to 7.2 per 100,000 births in western Europe; the estimated relative risk was 81 [@turck-2017-vitamin-k].

## Supplements in adults without deficiency

In trials of adults without known deficiency, vitamin supplements gave little or no protection against cardiovascular disease, cancer or death [@oconnor-2022-supplements].

In an evidence review for the US Preventive Services Task Force, two trials of vitamin C 500 mg daily for 3 to 8 years in 15,031 participants found no benefit [@oconnor-2022-supplements]. Multivitamins were associated with less cancer (odds ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.87 to 0.99; 4 trials, 48,859 participants), with absolute differences of −0.2 to −1.2 percentage points in adequately powered trials [@oconnor-2022-supplements].

:::industry
The Physicians' Health Study II was funded by NIH and by BASF, which supplied the vitamin C and had no role in the analysis [@sesso-2008-phs2]. Among 14,641 male physicians followed for a mean 8.0 years, major cardiovascular events occurred in 619 of 7329 (8.45%) assigned vitamin C 500 mg daily and 626 of 7312 (8.56%) assigned placebo (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.89 to 1.11), a difference of −0.1 percentage points [@sesso-2008-phs2].
:::

:::consensus
The US Preventive Services Task Force found the evidence insufficient to assess multivitamins or single or paired nutrients other than β-carotene and vitamin E, and recommends against those two for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer [@mangione-2022-uspstf].
:::

:::unresolved
Whether multivitamins lower cancer incidence is unsettled: the pooled estimate rested on three adequately powered trials, one with a median 3.6 years of use and one limited to antioxidants [@oconnor-2022-supplements].
:::
