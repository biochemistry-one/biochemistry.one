## Why funding is recorded

When a manufacturer pays for a study of its drug or device, favourable results are reported more often [@lundh-2017-sponsorship].

In a Cochrane review, favourable efficacy results were more frequent with industry sponsorship: 638 against an assumed 502 per 1000 (risk ratio 1.27, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.37) [@lundh-2017-sponsorship].

## Why regulatory documents are read

Trials with disappointing results are published less often than those with favourable results [@turner-2022-antidepressants].

Manufacturers must report all phase II and III trial results to the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for approval [@turner-2022-antidepressants]. Of trials judged negative by the FDA, 4 of 37 (11%) for older antidepressants and 7 of 15 (47%) for newer ones were published in agreement with it [@turner-2022-antidepressants].

:::unresolved
In a 2008 analysis of the older antidepressants, 48 of 51 published trials (94%) were reported as positive, against 38 of 74 (51%) in FDA reviews [@turner-2008-antidepressants].
:::

## The classes

Each cited publication is assigned one of six classes.

| Class | Criterion |
|---|---|
| independent | Public or charitable funding, without industry money or an industry role. Donated products are permitted. |
| industry | Any industry funding, or an industry role in design, analysis or publication |
| regulatory | Assessments, labels and safety communications of regulators and statutory risk assessors: FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, EFSA, FSANZ, JECFA |
| consensus | Guidelines, consensus statements, position papers, expert-panel classifications such as IARC |
| reference | Textbooks and curated databases |
| undetermined | Funding not determinable from the publication |

:::industry
LEADER, a liraglutide trial funded by Novo Nordisk and National Institutes of Health (NIH) grants, is industry [@marso-2016-leader].
:::

:::consensus
In 2011, an Institute of Medicine committee judged the evidence on vitamin D and cancer or cardiovascular disease inconclusive as to causality [@ross-2011-vitamin-d].
:::

:::unresolved
No funding source is stated in the 2008 antidepressant analysis or in a 2015 count of registered trials, so both are classed undetermined [@turner-2008-antidepressants; @ehrhardt-2015-trial-funding].
:::

## The layers

On every page, independent findings are kept apart from other evidence.

| Layer | Permitted classes |
|---|---|
| Main text | independent, regulatory, reference |
| Industry | industry, regulatory |
| Consensus | consensus, regulatory |
| Unresolved | all classes |

The same evidence is required for a statement against consensus as for one in agreement with it. The absence of independent evidence is stated in the unresolved layer.

## Absolute and relative effects

A relative effect is the fraction by which a treatment changes a risk. An absolute effect is the change in the number of people per hundred who have the outcome.

In SPRINT, 9361 adults were assigned a systolic target below 120 or below 140 mm Hg [@wright-2015-sprint]. The primary cardiovascular outcome occurred in 243 of 4678 participants (5.2%) and 319 of 4683 (6.8%) over a median 3.26 years (hazard ratio 0.75) [@wright-2015-sprint]. The relative reduction is 25%, the absolute reduction 1.62 percentage points, and the reported number needed to treat 61 [@wright-2015-sprint]. Serious adverse events possibly or definitely related to the intervention occurred in 220 (4.7%) and 118 (2.5%) participants [@wright-2015-sprint]. The number needed to harm, the reciprocal of the 2.18 percentage-point difference, is 46 [@wright-2015-sprint].

## Limits of this method

Funding is one source of error among several [@lundh-2017-sponsorship]. Trials without industry money can also be at risk of bias [@lundh-2017-sponsorship].

Study design is not graded by class. Low serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D had been linked with cancer and cardiovascular risk in observational studies [@manson-2019-vital]. In VITAL, an independent trial, colecalciferol reduced neither invasive cancer (6.1% of 12,927 against 6.4% of 12,944; hazard ratio 0.96) nor major cardiovascular events (3.1% against 3.2%; 0.97) over a median 5.3 years [@manson-2019-vital].

:::unresolved
Among interventional trials newly registered in ClinicalTrials.gov, 1376 were NIH-funded in 2006 and 1048 in 2014, against 4585 and 6550 industry-funded trials [@ehrhardt-2015-trial-funding].
:::
