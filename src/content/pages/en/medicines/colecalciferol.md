description: Colecalciferol harms from hypercalcaemia, kidney damage, stones, falls after large doses and infant toxicity; its reach into parathyroid, bone, FGF23, immunity.
summary: Too much colecalciferol raises blood calcium, which can damage the kidneys and, when severe, disturb heart rhythm or cause coma and death; with calcium it increased kidney stones, very large yearly or monthly doses led to more falls in older people, and infants with certain inherited defects develop severe hypercalcaemia on preventive doses. Its active hormone also acts on the parathyroid glands, bone, the phosphate hormone FGF23 and immune cells, and in mice on the hormones that regulate blood pressure. In large trials of adults who mostly had enough vitamin D it did not prevent fractures, falls, cancer or heart disease; with calcium it prevented about 1 hip fracture per 1000 older people a year in the community and 9 per 1000 in institutions.

## Serious harms

Colecalciferol is vitamin D3, prescribed to prevent and treat vitamin D deficiency and alongside osteoporosis treatment [@mhra-2026-fultium-d3; @mhra-2016-invita-d3].

| Condition | How often | Who is at risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypercalcaemia; if severe, arrhythmia, coma, death | Uncommon (1 in 1000 to 1 in 100); 9 of 102 on 10,000 IU daily against 0 of 109 on 400 IU over 3 years, all mild | Overdose; leukaemia, lymphoma, sarcoidosis; thiazide users | [@mhra-2026-fultium-d3; @burt-2019-bone-density; @fda-2026-fosamax-plus-d; @mhra-2016-invita-d3] |
| Irreversible kidney damage; soft-tissue calcification | Not quantified | Chronic overdose; impaired kidney function | [@mhra-2026-fultium-d3; @mhra-2016-invita-d3] |
| Urinary tract stones | 449 of 18,176 against 381 of 18,106 over 7 years (HR 1.17, 1.02 to 1.34) | Postmenopausal women taking calcium | [@wallace-2011-urinary-stones] |
| Falls and fractures | 500,000 IU yearly: falls 83.4 against 72.7, fractures 4.9 against 3.9 per 100 person-years (2256 women); 100,000 IU monthly against a standard dose: falls 1.47 against 0.63 per person-year (107 residents) | Women 70 or older; care residents | [@sanders-2010-annual-vitamin-d; @ginde-2017-long-term-care] |
| Serious falls; falls with hospitalisation | 7.6 against 4.1 and 5.4 against 2.0 per 100 person-years on 1000 against 200 IU daily (647 people); at 2000 IU, hospitalisation or death 33.6 against 12.1, arm stopped | 70 or older, raised fall risk | [@appel-2021-sturdy] |
| Infantile hypercalcaemia with nephrocalcinosis | Estimated 1 in 32,465 births in Poland (*CYP24A1* form) | Biallelic *CYP24A1* or *SLC34A1* loss | [@pronicka-2017-cyp24a1] |
| Infant overdose with coma, arrhythmia, permanent kidney impairment | Not quantified | Oversized droppers | [@fda-2010-liquid-vitamin-d] |
| Anaphylaxis; angio-oedema | Not quantified | Peanut or soya allergy (imported capsules); otherwise not stated | [@mhra-2012-imported-vitamin-d-allergens; @mhra-2026-fultium-d3] |

In intoxication, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, or 25(OH)D, accumulates and parathyroid hormone (PTH) is suppressed; bone resorption and intestinal absorption raise calcium [@turck-2023-vitamin-d]. A UK label puts the adult intoxication threshold at 40,000 to 100,000 IU daily for 1 to 2 months; infants may react to far less [@mhra-2016-invita-d3]. Calcium normalises over weeks to months after stopping; soft-tissue damage may be permanent [@mhra-2016-invita-d3; @turck-2023-vitamin-d].

:::unresolved
Harms were under-measured: VITAL excluded people with previous hypercalcaemia or renal failure, had a placebo run-in and mailed side-effect questionnaires; D2d omitted 24-hour urine calcium; WHI stones were self-reported; 14 of 34 high-dose trials monitored urine calcium [@manson-2019-vital; @costenbader-2024-vital-autoimmune; @pittas-2019-d2d; @wallace-2011-urinary-stones; @turck-2023-vitamin-d]. Bolus results conflict: falls 52% against 53% with 100,000 IU monthly in ViDA (5108 adults, post hoc); 66.9% on 60,000 IU monthly against 47.9% on 24,000 IU in a partly industry-funded trial (200 adults) [@khaw-2017-vida-falls; @bischoff-ferrari-2016-monthly].
:::

## Other pathways it affects

Calcitriol acts in many tissues and directly or indirectly regulates about 3% of the genome [@turck-2023-vitamin-d].

| Tissue | Mechanism | Measured in people |
|---|---|---|
| Parathyroid glands | Calcitriol suppresses PTH transcription and parathyroid cell proliferation [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d; @efsa-2016-vitamin-d] | PTH 17.1 ng/L during hypercalciuria against 20.2 otherwise [@turck-2023-vitamin-d] |
| Bone | With PTH, calcitriol activates osteoclasts [@efsa-2016-vitamin-d] | Radial density fell 3.5% on 10,000 IU daily against 1.2% on 400 IU over 3 years; estimated strength unchanged, relevance unclear [@burt-2019-bone-density; @turck-2023-vitamin-d] |
| Phosphate | Calcitriol stimulates FGF23, which suppresses CYP27B1 and induces CYP24A1; *Fgf23*-deficient mice have hyperphosphataemia [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d] | FGF23 not significantly changed by 2800 IU daily (181 adults, 8 weeks) [@trummer-2019-fgf23] |
| Immune cells | In cells, calcitriol induces cathelicidin and impairs dendritic-cell maturation [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d] | Autoimmune disease 0.95% against 1.20% over 5.3 years (HR 0.78, 0.61 to 0.99); 0.85 (0.70 to 1.04) with later-confirmed cases [@hahn-2022-vital-autoimmune; @costenbader-2024-vital-autoimmune] |
| Heart and vessels | Mice lacking the vitamin D receptor (VDR) or CYP27B1 have raised renin, hypertension and cardiac hypertrophy [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d] | Cardiovascular events not reduced in VITAL (HR 0.97) or ViDA (HR 1.02) [@manson-2019-vital; @scragg-2017-vida] |
| Fetus | High doses teratogenic in animals [@mhra-2026-fultium-d3] | Up to 28,000 IU weekly in 1300 pregnancies: asymptomatic postpartum hypercalcaemia in 0.7% of women and of infants [@roth-2018-mdig] |

:::unresolved
Muscle VDR expression is contested in mouse studies [@wang-2011-muscle-vdr; @girgis-2014-muscle-vdr]. Tibial artery calcification did not progress differently on 400 to 10,000 IU daily over 3 years (302 adults) [@billington-2020-arterial-calcification]. For calcium with or without vitamin D, 6 heart attacks or strokes caused and 3 fractures prevented per 1000 over 5 years were estimated; UK advisers judged the evidence unconvincing [@bolland-2011-calcium-cardiovascular; @mhra-2011-calcium-vitamin-d].
:::

## What it is for and how it works

Vitamin D is needed to absorb calcium from food [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d].

Vitamin D3 400 IU daily prevents deficiency rickets in children, in which 25(OH)D is usually below 10 ng/mL [@christakos-2016-vitamin-d]. Liver CYP2R1 and kidney CYP27B1 hydroxylate colecalciferol to calcitriol, which binds VDR and increases intestinal TRPV6 and calbindin-D9k [@cheng-2004-cyp2r1; @christakos-2016-vitamin-d].

## In the body

Colecalciferol is stored mainly in fat [@turck-2023-vitamin-d].

The half-life of 25(OH)D is 13 to 15 days, of serum vitamin D3 about 24 hours [@turck-2023-vitamin-d; @ema-2026-fosavance]. Thiazides reduce urinary calcium excretion and raise hypercalcaemia risk; vitamin D3 may increase digitalis toxicity [@mhra-2016-invita-d3; @mhra-2026-fultium-d3].

## Genetic variation

Some infants inherit defects that make preventive vitamin D raise blood calcium severely [@pronicka-2017-cyp24a1].

Biallelic loss of *CYP24A1*, which degrades calcitriol, or of *SLC34A1*, a renal phosphate transporter, causes this; in Poland, cases rose with prophylaxis of 300,000 units three times a year, and all 11 historical cases tested carried mutations [@pronicka-2017-cyp24a1].

## Benefit against harm

Across 81 trials, vitamin D did not reduce fractures or falls [@bolland-2018-musculoskeletal].

Of those tested in VITAL, 12.7% had 25(OH)D below 20 ng/mL; in ViDA, 24.9% [@manson-2019-vital; @scragg-2017-vida].

| Group | Benefit | Serious harm |
|---|---|---|
| Older adults, with calcium | Hip fracture 1 fewer per 1000 a year in the community (from 8), 9 fewer in institutions (from 54) [@avenell-2014-fractures] | Stones or kidney insufficiency 9 against 8 per 10,000 a year [@avenell-2014-fractures] |
| Adults 50 or older, 2000 IU daily, 5.3 years (25,871) | Cancer 793 against 824; fracture 769 against 782 [@manson-2019-vital; @leboff-2022-vital-fractures] | Hypercalcaemia and stones not significantly increased [@manson-2019-vital] |
| 25 trials, ages 0 to 95 (10,933) | Respiratory infection NNT 33; NNT 4 below 25 nmol/L with daily or weekly doses [@martineau-2017-respiratory] | Hypercalcaemia 21 of 3850, evenly distributed [@martineau-2017-respiratory] |

:::consensus
USPSTF (2018) judged the stone harm small and recommends against 400 IU or less with 1000 mg calcium or less for primary fracture prevention after menopause [@uspstf-2018-fractures].
:::

:::unresolved
Mortality conflicts: 11.0% against 11.4% in 38 vitamin D3 trials (RR 0.94, 0.91 to 0.98), but with more than 8% dropout, worst-case analyses allowed a large increase or decrease; 5.3% against 5.1% in D-Health (HR 1.04, 0.93 to 1.18) [@bjelakovic-2014-mortality; @neale-2022-d-health].
:::

## Without the medicine

Exercise prevents falls in older people living in the community [@sherrington-2019-exercise-falls].

Falls were 0.77 times control (59 trials, 12,981 people), 195 fewer per 1000 a year; fall-related fractures RR 0.73 (low certainty) [@sherrington-2019-exercise-falls]. Sunlight makes vitamin D3 in skin; UVB intoxication has not been reported [@efsa-2016-vitamin-d].

## How the recommendations were set

Europe's upper limit derives from the lowest dose that repeatedly raised urine calcium [@turck-2023-vitamin-d].

EFSA divided 250 µg (10,000 IU) daily, with recurrent hypercalciuria in 11.3% against 4.0% on 10 µg, by 2.5, giving 100 µg daily [@turck-2023-vitamin-d].

:::consensus
The IOM (2011) set 600 IU daily to age 70 and 800 IU from 71 [@ross-2011-vitamin-d]. The Endocrine Society (2011), whose chair declared interests including Merck, Amgen and Dairy Management Inc., defined deficiency below 20 ng/mL, which IOM members said inflates the number deemed deficient [@holick-2011-endocrine-society; @rosen-2012-iom-response]. In 2024 the Society endorsed no sufficiency level and suggested against routine testing [@demay-2024-endocrine-society].
:::

:::unresolved
Among 156 articles cited by 47 bone-health guidelines, those with missing or unclear conflict declarations more often favoured vitamin D or calcium (RR 1.56, 1.05 to 2.31) [@baram-2022-guideline-conflicts].
:::

## Stopping

After stopping, blood levels fall over weeks to months [@turck-2023-vitamin-d; @sanders-2010-annual-vitamin-d].

Two years after VITAL ended, autoimmune disease no longer differed: 255 cases against 259 (HR 0.98) [@costenbader-2024-vital-autoimmune].

:::unresolved
A PubMed search in October 2026 located no randomised withdrawal trial; evidence comes from post-trial follow-up and 25(OH)D kinetics [@costenbader-2024-vital-autoimmune; @sanders-2010-annual-vitamin-d].
:::

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber.
