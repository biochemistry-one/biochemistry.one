## Serious harms

Statins are prescribed to lower LDL cholesterol and the risk of heart attack and stroke [@fda-2025-simvastatin-label].

| Condition | How often | Who is at risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rhabdomyolysis, with kidney injury and rare deaths | 1 (SE 1) extra per 10,000 in trials; 4.4 admissions per 100,000 users a year | Age 65 or older, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, renal impairment, interacting drugs, higher dose | [@ctt-2010-haemorrhagic-stroke; @fda-2011-simvastatin-80mg; @fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings] |
| Myopathy on simvastatin 80 mg | 0.9% against 0.02% on 20 mg, rhabdomyolysis 0.4% against none, of 12,064 over 6.7 years | First year; a liver transporter variant associated with about 60% of cases | [@fda-2011-simvastatin-80mg; @fda-2025-simvastatin-warnings] |
| Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy | 20.4 and 24.1 per million users a year in UK and Australian regions | After 1 month to 23 years of use (median 3 years) | [@khoo-2025-imnm] |
| Liver injury and rare liver failure | 22 of 1188 cases in a US drug-induced liver injury registry; 4 became chronic, 1 with cirrhosis died | Unpredictable; latency 34 days to 10 years | [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings; @russo-2014-dilin; @fda-2012-statin-label-changes] |
| New-onset diabetes | 1 extra per 255 treated for 4 years | Raised fasting glucose, triglycerides or body mass index; hypertension | [@mhra-2012-statin-diabetes] |
| Haemorrhagic stroke | Perhaps a few extra per 10,000 a year | Recent stroke, above all haemorrhagic: 16% on atorvastatin 80 mg against 4% on placebo (post hoc) | [@ctt-2010-haemorrhagic-stroke; @fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings] |
| Admission for acute kidney injury | 1 extra per 1700 treated for 120 days, high against low potency | Without chronic kidney disease | [@dormuth-2013-kidney] |
| Myasthenia gravis | 10 UK reports in 28 years; more than 9.5 million UK users in 2022 | With or without prior myasthenia | [@mhra-2023-myasthenia-gravis] |
| Interstitial lung disease, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome | Lung disease very rare; others not always reliably estimable | Not stated | [@mhra-2009-statin-class-effects; @fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings] |

The kidney injury is secondary to myoglobinuria, and muscle symptoms may resolve on stopping [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings]. Necrotising myopathy, with antibodies against HMG-CoA reductase, persists after stopping and has recurred with the same or another statin [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings]; creatine kinase had normalised in 50 of 105 patients at a median 2.3 years [@khoo-2025-imnm].

:::unresolved
The Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' Collaboration received individual data only for its own meta-analyses and sought only myopathy that had progressed to rhabdomyolysis [@ctt-2022-run-in-data-sharing; @ctt-2010-haemorrhagic-stroke]. A few trials used an active run-in that may exclude people with early adverse effects; BMJ editors stated that the trials were not designed specifically to measure harms [@collins-2016-interpretation; @parish-2015-data-access].
:::

## Other pathways it affects

The enzyme that statins block is found in every tissue and supplies more than cholesterol [@uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches].

::pathway{id="mevalonate"}

Its downstream intermediates supply:

- coenzyme Q10, which transports electrons in mitochondria and requires COQ2, expressed most in skeletal muscle, adrenal glands and heart [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb; @uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches];
- haem A of cytochrome c oxidase, through COX10 and COX15, the latter mainly in oxidative tissues such as muscle, heart and brain [@uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches];
- dolichol phosphate, to which sugars are attached for protein glycosylation [@uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches];
- farnesyl and geranylgeranyl groups attached to proteins [@uniprot-2025-uniprotkb];
- a tRNA modification by TRIT1, which is required for selenoprotein expression [@uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches].

::pathway{id="respiratory-chain"}

In confirmed statin myalgia, serum coenzyme Q10 fell from 1.3 to 0.8 µg/mL in 8 weeks on simvastatin 20 mg; supplements of 400 or 600 mg/day did not reduce myalgia in independent trials [@taylor-2015-coq10; @dohlmann-2022-coq10]. Biallelic *HMGCR* variants cause limb-girdle muscular dystrophy 28; in the first family, the authors describe the muscle imaging as highly reminiscent of end-stage statin myopathy [@uniprot-2025-isoprenoid-branches; @yogev-2023-hmgcr-myopathy]. LDL-lowering *HMGCR* alleles were associated with type 2 diabetes (odds ratio 1.029 per allele) [@lotta-2016-diabetes].

The central nervous system, 2.1% of body weight, holds 23% of the body's sterol; the cholesterol for its growth apparently comes exclusively from local synthesis [@dietschy-2004-brain-cholesterol]. In rare reports, memory loss reversed on stopping (median 3 weeks) [@fda-2012-statin-label-changes]; in 9971 people aged 70 or older, atorvastatin 40 mg did not change the composite of death, dementia or persistent physical disability (HR 0.94, 0.84 to 1.05) [@zoungas-2026-staree].

:::industry
In 25 patients, 28 days of atorvastatin 80 mg lowered leukocyte Rho kinase activity by about 49% against placebo, uncorrelated with LDL change, which the authors attribute to fewer isoprenoids [@nohria-2009-rho-kinase].
:::

:::unresolved
In 11 trials, mostly by industry-affiliated or industry-funded teams, statins lowered testosterone by 0.66 nmol/L in 501 men and 0.40 nmol/L in 368 women with polycystic ovary syndrome [@schooling-2013-testosterone].
:::

## What it is for and how it works

People who inherit lower LDL cholesterol develop less coronary heart disease [@cohen-2006-pcsk9].

Statins competitively inhibit HMG-CoA reductase [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-label]. In cells and animals, lower cholesterol in the endoplasmic reticulum releases SREBP-2, which activates the LDL receptor gene, so liver cells remove more LDL [@brown-2018-scap; @goldstein-2015-century].

## In the body

Atorvastatin, rosuvastatin and simvastatin are taken up into the liver by a transporter protein, OATP1B1 [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-label; @fda-2025-simvastatin-label; @fda-2026-rosuvastatin].

CYP3A4 metabolises atorvastatin and simvastatin, but not rosuvastatin to a clinically significant extent [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-label; @fda-2025-simvastatin-label; @fda-2026-rosuvastatin]. Simvastatin is contraindicated with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, ciclosporin, danazol and gemfibrozil [@fda-2025-simvastatin-warnings]. The MHRA advises against systemic fusidic acid with statins after suspected fatal rhabdomyolysis [@mhra-2011-fusidic-acid].

## Genetic variation

Ancestry and inherited variants alter blood statin levels and muscle risk [@fda-2026-rosuvastatin-warnings; @fda-2011-simvastatin-80mg].

In pharmacokinetic studies, Asian subjects had about twice the median rosuvastatin exposure of White controls [@fda-2026-rosuvastatin-warnings]. On simvastatin 40 mg over a median 3.9 years, myopathy occurred in about 0.24% of 5468 Chinese and 0.05% of 7367 non-Chinese patients [@fda-2025-simvastatin-warnings].

## Benefit against harm

In a review of trials in people without cardiovascular disease, one death was avoided per 286 treated for up to 6 years [@chou-2022-uspstf-review].

| Group | Benefit | Serious harm |
|---|---|---|
| 21 trials, median 4.8 years | Major vascular events 2.8% against 3.6% a year [@ctt-2010-intensive] | Rhabdomyolysis 1 extra per 10,000 [@ctt-2010-haemorrhagic-stroke] |
| Recent stroke or transient ischaemic attack, atorvastatin 80 mg (2365) against placebo (2366), median 4.9 years | Ischaemic stroke 9.2% against 11.6% (post hoc) [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings] | Haemorrhagic stroke 2.3% against 1.4% (post hoc); diabetes 6.1% against 3.8% [@fda-2024-atorvastatin-warnings] |
| Aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia, median 5.9 years | Cardiovascular events 10.9 against 15.5 per 1000 person-years [@zoungas-2026-staree] | Serious adverse events 2.7% in both groups; more musculoskeletal, hepatobiliary and diabetes-related events [@zoungas-2026-staree] |

Only 3 of its 22 trials reported no industry funding; the reviewers had no individual data [@chou-2022-uspstf-review]. In 46,864 Catalans aged 75 or older without diabetes, new statin use was not associated with fewer cardiovascular events (HR 0.94 at 75 to 84, 0.93 from 85) [@ramos-2018-older].

## Without the medicine

In 7447 Spanish adults at high risk, a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil or nuts lowered heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death from 4.4% to 3.8% and 3.4% over a median 4.8 years, without a difference in all-cause mortality [@estruch-2018-predimed]. The first report was retracted for randomisation irregularities; this reanalysis gave similar results [@estruch-2018-predimed].

## How the recommendations were set

Of 288 members of 14 US and Canadian lipid and diabetes guideline panels from 2000 to 2010, 52% had financial conflicts of interest [@neuman-2011-guideline-conflicts].

:::consensus
The 2004 US update made LDL cholesterol below 70 mg/dL an option for very high risk; eight of its nine authors disclosed ties to manufacturers of lipid-lowering drugs [@grundy-2004-atp3-update].
:::

## Stopping

In an open-label trial in people aged 75 or older taking a statin to prevent a first heart attack or stroke, 35 of 484 (7.2%) died within 3 years after stopping and 48 of 604 (7.9%) with continuation [@bonnet-2026-saga-site].

In an unblinded trial in people with a life expectancy of 1 month to 1 year, 60-day mortality was 45 of 189 (23.8%) after stopping and 39 of 192 (20.3%) with continuation, so non-inferiority was not shown [@kutner-2015-discontinuation].

A prescription is changed only together with the prescriber.
