title: Omeprazole and other proton-pump inhibitors
description: Kidney injury, low magnesium, infection, fracture, B12 and skin harms of PPIs, and their effects on gastrin, gut bacteria, minerals and drug activation.
summary: Proton-pump inhibitors can inflame the kidneys, lower blood magnesium enough to cause seizures or heart-rhythm disturbances, and cause severe skin reactions; they are linked with bowel infection and, after long use, with fractures and vitamin B12 deficiency. Removing stomach acid raises the hormone gastrin, changes gut bacteria and reduces absorption of vitamin B12 and some medicines, and omeprazole reduces activation of the anti-clotting drug clopidogrel. In trials they healed acid damage to the gullet in three of four people within 8 weeks, against one in seven on placebo, and cut ulcers in people taking anti-inflammatory painkillers from 119 to 36 per 1000 over about 6 months.

## Serious harms

Proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) are prescribed for acid reflux and peptic ulcers, and to prevent ulcers and bleeding [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @scally-2018-gastroprotectants; @garegnani-2025-nsaid; @cook-2024-revise].

| Condition | How often | Who is at risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acute interstitial nephritis, hospitalised or fatal | 11.98 against 1.68 per 100,000 person-years, current against past use | Any time in therapy | [@blank-2014-interstitial-nephritis; @fda-2024-prilosec-label] |
| Acute kidney injury | 13.49 against 5.46 hospitalisations per 1000 person-years | Adults 66 or older, first 120 days | [@antoniou-2015-kidney-injury] |
| Chronic kidney disease | 10-year risk 11.8% against 8.5% expected | US adults; causality not established | [@lazarus-2016-ckd] |
| Low magnesium: tetany, seizures, arrhythmia | 19.4% of users against 13.5% of non-users; 1 excess hospitalisation per 76,591 treated for 90 days | Use beyond 3 months, mostly a year; diuretics; hypoparathyroidism | [@srinutta-2019-hypomagnesaemia; @zipursky-2014-hypomagnesaemia; @fda-2011-ppi-magnesium; @fda-2024-prilosec-label] |
| *Clostridioides difficile* diarrhoea | Higher in 23 of 28 studies, mostly 1.4 to 2.75 times | Hospitalised patients | [@fda-2012-ppi-cdad; @fda-2024-prilosec-label] |
| Hip, wrist or spine fracture | 10% to 40% higher; hip 2.02 against 1.51 per 1000 person-years in women | 50 or older; high doses; a year or longer | [@mhra-2012-ppi-fracture; @khalili-2012-hip-fracture; @fda-2010-ppi-fractures] |
| Fracture in children | 20.2 against 18.3 per 1000 person-years | Highest after a year; not significant against H2-receptor antagonists (H2RAs) | [@wang-2020-child-fracture] |
| Asthma in children | 21.8 against 14.0 per 1000 person-years | Highest when started before age 2 | [@wang-2021-asthma] |
| Vitamin B12 deficiency | At least 2 years' supply in 12.0% of 25,956 deficient patients against 7.2% of 184,199 controls | Use beyond 3 years; higher doses | [@lam-2013-b12; @fda-2024-prilosec-label] |
| Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS | Not known; some fatal | Not specified | [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @ema-2023-pantozol-control] |
| Lupus, cutaneous or systemic | OR 2.9 for subacute cutaneous lupus | Weeks to years after starting | [@mhra-2015-ppi-lupus; @fda-2024-prilosec-label] |
| Agranulocytosis, pancreatitis, liver failure | Agranulocytosis 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 1000; some fatal | Not specified | [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @ema-2023-pantozol-control] |

Nephritis can occur without fever, rash or joint pain [@fda-2024-prilosec-label]. Magnesium normalised a median 1 week after stopping and fell again a median 2 weeks after restarting [@fda-2011-ppi-magnesium]. Lupus mostly improves 4 to 12 weeks after stopping [@fda-2024-prilosec-label]. Fractures did not increase in trials, generally 6 months long [@fda-2010-ppi-fractures].

Observational and randomised estimates are discordant.

| *C. difficile* infection | OR 1.99 in 56 studies [@trifan-2017-clostridioides] | 60 of 14,946 against 49 of 14,934 [@floria-2025-clostridioides] |

| Dementia | HR 1.44 from claims, 0.88 with adjudicated diagnosis [@gomm-2016-dementia; @mehta-2023-dementia] | Similar in COMPASS [@moayyedi-2019-compass-safety] |
| Death | 45.20 excess per 1000 over 10 years against H2RAs [@xie-2019-mortality] | Not significantly increased in four trials [@moayyedi-2019-compass-safety; @krag-2018-sup-icu; @cook-2024-revise; @young-2020-peptic] |
:::

:::industry
In Bayer-sponsored COMPASS, 17,598 patients took pantoprazole or placebo for a median 3.01 years; only enteric infections differed significantly, 1.4% against 1.0% [@moayyedi-2019-compass-safety]. In a Takeda-sponsored trial in 162 infants, serious adverse events occurred in 10 on lansoprazole against 2 on placebo, while 54% responded in each arm [@orenstein-2009-infants].
:::

## Other pathways it affects

Less acid raises gastrin and stomach bacteria and reduces vitamin B12 freed from food [@ema-2023-pantozol-control; @fda-2024-prilosec-label; @ito-2024-b12].

| Pathway or tissue | Mechanism | Measured in people |
|---|---|---|
| Gastrin and ECL cells | Gastrin stimulates acid secretion through CCK2 receptors of brain, pancreas and stomach, and acts on ECL cells [@uniprot-2025-gastric-acid; @fossmark-2019-adverse-effects] | Median gastrin 1.3- to 3.6-fold; ECL-cell hyperplasia; fundic gland polyps in 1 in 100 or more [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @ema-2023-pantozol-control] |
| Gut bacteria | Acid suppression raises gastric bacterial counts [@fda-2024-prilosec-label] | Lower faecal diversity and more oral bacteria in 1827 twins [@jackson-2016-microbiome] |
| Vitamin B12 | Pepsin, which frees food-bound B12, is not activated above pH 3.5 [@ito-2024-b12] | 21% of 175 with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome deficient after a mean 10.2 years [@ito-2024-b12] |
| Calcium and bone | Acid aids mineral absorption [@engevik-2020-parietal-cell] | Calcium absorption not reduced in 21 women after 30 days of omeprazole 40 mg [@hansen-2010-calcium] |
| Endothelium | DDAH1 hydrolyses ADMA, an inhibitor of nitric oxide synthase [@uniprot-2025-gastric-acid] | No significant ADMA rise or loss of vasodilation in 21 adults after 4 weeks of lansoprazole [@ghebremariam-2015-vascular] |

Rats given omeprazole for 24 months at about 0.4 to 34 times a 40 mg human dose developed ECL-cell carcinoids, attributed by the EMA to massively raised gastrin [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @ema-2023-pantozol-control].

:::industry
In mice and human tissue, PPIs inhibited DDAH and impaired vasodilation; the study had partial industry gift funding and patent-holding authors [@ghebremariam-2013-adma].
:::

:::unresolved
The magnesium mechanism is unproven; colonic TRPM6 regulation by pH is proposed [@fda-2011-ppi-magnesium; @william-2016-hypomagnesaemia].
:::

## What it is for and how it works

Acid rising into the gullet causes heartburn; acid can damage the stomach and duodenal lining, where ulcers form [@sigterman-2013-gord; @scally-2018-gastroprotectants].

PPIs are acid-activated prodrugs that bind cysteine 813 of ATP4A, the catalytic subunit of the parietal-cell acid pump, covalently [@shin-2008-ppi-pharmacology; @uniprot-2025-gastric-acid].

::pathway{id="ppi-activation"}

## In the body

A dose leaves the blood within hours, yet acid stays low for about two days because the drug binds the pump [@shin-2008-ppi-pharmacology].

Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19, which contributes substantially to clopidogrel activation, and at 80 mg reduced its antiplatelet effect even 12 hours apart; the FDA advises against the combination [@sangkuhl-2010-clopidogrel; @fda-2024-prilosec-label; @fda-2010-clopidogrel-omeprazole].

::pathway{id="clopidogrel-activation"}

Omeprazole lowers rilpivirine and atazanavir exposure and may prolong high-dose methotrexate levels; INR rises reported with warfarin may lead to bleeding and death; less acid reduces levothyroxine, mycophenolate and iron-salt absorption [@fda-2024-prilosec-label; @abbvie-2024-synthroid-label].

## Genetic variation

An inherited slow form of CYP2C19, a liver enzyme that breaks these drugs down, raises blood levels and effect: poor metabolisers, 3% of Caucasians and 15% to 20% of Asians, have about 7.5 times the omeprazole exposure of homozygous extensive metabolisers [@shin-2008-ppi-pharmacology].

:::unresolved
CPIC judges data insufficient for strong recommendations, yet advises considering half the dose in intermediate and poor metabolisers after 12 weeks of effective therapy [@lima-2021-cpic-ppi].
:::

## Benefit against harm

Benefit was measured over weeks to months; in prevention trials of median 180 days, gastroprotectants prevented 12 bleeds per 1000 [@scally-2018-gastroprotectants].

| Group | Benefit | Serious harm |
|---|---|---|
| Erosive oesophagitis | Healed at 8 weeks in 74% of 83 against 14% of 43 [@fda-2024-prilosec-label] | Fracture and magnesium risks rise after a year [@mhra-2012-ppi-fracture; @fda-2011-ppi-magnesium] |
| NSAID users, about 6 months | Ulcers 119 to 36 per 1000; complications 11 to 4, uncertain [@garegnani-2025-nsaid] | Adverse events not pooled; 7 of 12 trials company-funded [@garegnani-2025-nsaid] |
| Ventilated in intensive care | Bleeding 3.5% to 1.0% in 90 days [@cook-2024-revise] | No excess death (29.1% against 30.9%); pneumonia similar [@cook-2024-revise] |

:::unresolved
Reliable long-term safety data are lacking [@scally-2018-gastroprotectants]. Among 116,377 veterans without a documented indication, PPI use was associated with 22.91 excess cardiovascular deaths per 1000 over 10 years [@xie-2019-mortality].
:::

## Without the medicine

:::unresolved
Randomised evidence on non-drug measures is small, a scarcity reviewers attribute probably to commercial interests [@ness-jensen-2016-lifestyle]. A bed wedge reduced acid exposure from 21% to 15% of the time in 15 patients [@ness-jensen-2016-lifestyle]; five head-of-bed trials were at high risk of bias [@albarqouni-2021-bed-elevation].
:::

## How the recommendations were set

Non-prescription courses are limited to 14 days in the US, 4 weeks in the UK and EU [@fda-2026-prilosec-otc-label; @mhra-2012-ppi-fracture; @ema-2023-pantozol-control].

:::consensus
NICE (2014, updated 2019) advises the lowest effective dose and annual review [@nice-2019-dyspepsia]. The AGA (2017) advised against routine magnesium monitoring; the FDA and MHRA advise considering it [@freedberg-2017-aga; @fda-2011-ppi-magnesium; @mhra-2012-ppi-hypomagnesaemia]. ACG (2022) authors disclose ties with Phathom, Medtronic, Takeda and Ironwood [@katz-2022-acg-gerd].
:::

## Stopping

After stopping, acid secretion recovers within days, and symptoms return in some people [@shin-2008-ppi-pharmacology; @boghossian-2017-deprescribing]. Symptoms were uncontrolled more often with on-demand than daily use (RR 1.71, low quality) [@boghossian-2017-deprescribing].

:::unresolved
Of 59 symptom-free volunteers who stopped 8 weeks of esomeprazole, 26 developed acid symptoms, against 9 of 59 on placebo [@reimer-2009-rebound].
:::

:::consensus
The AGA bases de-prescribing on lack of an indication, not on concern about adverse events [@targownik-2022-aga-deprescribing].
:::

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber [@fda-2011-ppi-magnesium].
