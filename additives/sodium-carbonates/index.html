Food additives

Sodium carbonates (E500)

In brief

Sodium carbonates, baking soda among them, make dough rise and control acidity in foods such as biscuits and cakes. In the body they neutralise stomach acid and supply sodium, and the kidneys regulate the body's sodium and bicarbonate. In older people with advanced kidney disease, bicarbonate tablets did not improve physical function.

What it is

Sodium carbonates are alkaline sodium salts used as raising agents and acidity regulators; baking soda is one 123.

E 500 comprises sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃ (i); sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃ (ii); and sodium sesquicarbonate, Na₂CO₃·NaHCO₃·2H₂O (iii); the INS numbers are the same 4125. Sodium carbonate, diphosphates and lecithins occurred together in 1,667 French products, mostly biscuits 6.

How often it is used

In 2019, 8,587 of 126,556 French packaged foods contained E 500, fourth among additives 6.

Of 106,489 NutriNet-Santé adults in 2009–2020, 67.10% consumed it; mean intake was 1267.46 mg/day, 95th percentile 4950 mg/day 7.

In the body

Baking soda neutralises stomach acid, and the kidneys regulate the body’s sodium and bicarbonate 8910.

The proximal tubule reclaims about 80% of filtered bicarbonate 10.

What studies show

Bicarbonate tablets did not improve physical function in older people with advanced kidney disease 11.

BiCARB randomised 300 adults aged 60 or over, with eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² and serum bicarbonate below 22 mmol/L, to up to 3 g/day or placebo; mean follow-up was 16.4 months 11. The 12-month physical performance score differed by −0.4 points with bicarbonate (95% CI −0.9 to 0.1) 11. 131 of 152 participants on bicarbonate had 457 adverse events; 132 of 148 on placebo had 400 11.

JECFA’s acceptable daily intake is “not limited” for sodium carbonate and hydrogen carbonate, provided cation intake is considered 1212.

Industry-funded results

In a study partly funded by Church and Dwight, 7 days of 240 mmol/day sodium chloride raised blood pressure by 16/8 mm Hg in 5 salt-sensitive hypertensive men; equimolar sodium citrate did not 13.

Consensus statements

WHO strongly recommends under 2 g sodium a day for adults 14. Citing rat studies, proposed mechanisms and few clinical observations, EFSA noted evidence that chloride can contribute to the blood-pressure effect of sodium chloride 15.

Unresolved questions

In a week-long trial of undetermined funding, sodium bicarbonate mineral water lowered systolic pressure by 5 mm Hg in 10 hypertensive adults; sodium chloride did not 16. The NutriNet-Santé investigators judge human evidence on regular additive intake lacking 7. In a case series of undetermined funding, 12 of 192 symptomatic baking-soda misusers were admitted with electrolyte imbalance, metabolic alkalosis or respiratory depression 17.

  9. Independent research

    Palmer LG, Schnermann J. Integrated control of Na transport along the nephron Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology, 2015.

    doi:10.2215/CJN.12391213PubMed 25098598

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    L.G. Palmer: National Institutes of Health grant R01-DK27847. J. Schnermann: Intramural Research Program of the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases.
    Finding
    Review. More than 500 g of sodium is filtered daily; under most conditions the kidneys excrete more than 95% of ingested sodium. The proximal tubule reabsorbs 60-70% (apical NHE3 the main mechanism), the ascending loop of Henle, mostly the thick limb, 25-30% (NKCC2 and NHE3), and the distal convoluted tubule and collecting ducts no more than 5-10%, through thiazide-sensitive NCC (early DCT) and amiloride-sensitive ENaC (late DCT, connecting tubule, collecting duct); distal reabsorption is the regulated part. Two-thirds or more of renal oxygen uptake is spent on the Na,K-ATPase. With plausible tenfold changes in intake, fractional reabsorption varies between about 97% and 99.7%. Sodium depletion increases ENaC activity, an effect mimicked by aldosterone. Potassium loading lowers NCC phosphorylation and abundance and raises ENaC activity, shifting sodium reabsorption to segments where principal cells secrete K+ in exchange for Na+. Changes in body Na content and blood volume are the likely cause of the relation between Na intake and blood pressure.

  10. Independent research

    Skelton LA, Boron WF, Zhou Y. Acid-base transport by the renal proximal tubule Journal of Nephrology, 2010.

    PubMed 21170887

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    US National Kidney Foundation Fellowship (FLB795) to L.A.S.; US National Institutes of Health grants P01-DK17433 and R01-DK081567 to W.F.B.; American Heart Association Scientist Development Grant 0735432N to Y.Z.
    Finding
    Narrative review. The kidneys filter some 4,300 mmol of HCO3- a day; the proximal tubule reclaims about 80% and distal segments nearly all of the remainder. Proximal-tubule cells secrete H+ mainly through the Na-H exchanger NHE3, which exchanges 1 H+ for 1 Na+, and through the vacuolar H+-ATPase. Carbonic anhydrase IV, GPI-anchored to the outer leaflet of the apical membrane, converts secreted H+ and luminal HCO3- to CO2 and H2O, which re-enter the cell, in part through aquaporin 1 according to preliminary data. Cytosolic carbonic anhydrase II, about 95% of renal carbonic anhydrase activity, converts CO2 and H2O back to HCO3- and H+; HCO3- exits with Na+ across the basolateral membrane through the electrogenic cotransporter NBCe1-A at a stoichiometry of 3:1. Increases in basolateral HCO3- lower HCO3- reabsorption in isolated perfused proximal tubules, and HCO3- infusion in rats lowers it on free-flow micropuncture.

    EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Turck D, Castenmiller J, de Henauw S, Hirsch-Ernst KI, Kearney J, et al. Dietary reference values for sodium EFSA Journal, 2019.

    Funding

    Finding
    Sodium chloride is the main constituent of table salt; 1 g of sodium chloride provides 0.4 g of sodium and 0.6 g of chloride. In healthy people almost all dietary sodium is absorbed, along most of the small and large intestine; apical co-transporters couple sodium entry to the uptake of solutes, the basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase exchanges three intracellular Na+ for two extracellular K+, net sodium and water absorption occurs in the distal ileum and colon, and distal absorption responds to aldosterone. Renin is released from the juxtaglomerular apparatus in response to reduced afferent arteriolar pressure, increased sympathetic activity and decreased distal tubular NaCl; renin hydrolyses angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, which angiotensin-converting enzyme converts to angiotensin II; angiotensin II stimulates proximal sodium reabsorption, arteriolar constriction and adrenal aldosterone release; aldosterone stimulates sodium recovery in exchange for potassium in distal tubule, collecting ducts and distal colon. Genotypes with increased ENaC function cause volume expansion, hypertension and hypokalaemia (e.g. Liddle's syndrome). A sodium intake of 2.0 g/day is considered safe and adequate for adults, including pregnant and lactating women.

    Of 192 cases, the most common reasons for misuse were antacid use (60.4%), attempts to 'beat a urine drug test' (11.5%) and treatment of urinary tract infection (4.7%). 55.2% had significant symptoms warranting medical evaluation; 12 patients required hospital admission and developed electrolyte imbalances, metabolic alkalosis or respiratory depression.

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    US Food and Drug Administration; the text is federal regulation codified by the Office of the Federal Register.
    Finding
    Part 184 lists direct food substances affirmed as generally recognized as safe (GRAS), in these sections affirmed at 48 FR 52442 to 52443, 18 November 1983. Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3, CAS 144-55-8, section 184.1736) and sodium carbonate (Na2CO3, CAS 497-19-8, section 184.1742) are used in food with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice; sodium carbonate as antioxidant, curing and pickling agent, flavoring agent and adjuvant, pH control agent and processing aid. Sodium sesquicarbonate (Na2CO3·NaHCO3·2H2O, CAS 533-96-0, section 184.1792) is used as a pH control agent in cream, at a level sufficient to control lactic acid before pasteurization and churning of cream into butter. Each ingredient meets the specifications of the Food Chemicals Codex, 3rd edition (1981).
