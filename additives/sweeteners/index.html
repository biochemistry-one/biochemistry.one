Food additives

Sweeteners

In brief

Sweeteners make food and drinks taste sweet, some of them hundreds of times more strongly than sugar. Most are excreted largely unchanged, but aspartame is digested to two amino acids and methanol, which matters for people with phenylketonuria. In trials, sugar-free drinks slowed weight gain in children, whereas links with diabetes, heart disease and cancer come from observational studies.

What they are

Sweeteners are food additives that taste sweet, some of them 200 to 500 times more intensely than sugar 123.

Aspartame is L-aspartyl-L-phenylalanine methyl ester, sucralose a trichlorinated sucrose derivative, erythritol a four-carbon polyol, and steviol glycosides constituents of Stevia rebaudiana leaves 4567.

The sweet receptor is a TAS1R2–TAS1R3 heterodimer; sucralose and aspartame bind the same pocket of its TAS1R2 subunit 8.

In the body

Most sweeteners are excreted largely unchanged; aspartame is hydrolysed to two amino acids and methanol 21564.

Intestinal esterases and peptidases, including aminopeptidase A, hydrolyse aspartame with a half-life of minutes, yielding up to 10% methanol by weight 4.

In phenylketonuria, an autosomal recessive deficiency of hepatic phenylalanine hydroxylase, phenylalanine from food and aspartame accumulates and, untreated, causes intellectual impairment 4.

Acesulfame K, saccharin and, within 24 hours, 78 to 92% of erythritol are excreted unchanged in urine 216. Only 8.8 to 21.7% of sucralose is absorbed 5. Colonic bacteria release steviol from steviol glycosides; steviol is excreted as glucuronide, mainly in urine 7.

What studies show

In trials, sugar-free drinks slowed children’s weight gain, and replacing them with water did not lower blood glucose in type 2 diabetes 910.

Over 18 months, 641 children given a daily sugar-free or sugar-sweetened drink gained 6.35 and 7.37 kg (difference −1.01 kg, 95% CI −1.54 to −0.48) 9. In 181 adults with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c over 24 weeks rose from 7.20% to 7.44% with water and went from 7.19% to 7.14% with habitual sweetened beverages (adjusted difference 0.29 percentage points, P = 0.013) 10. Without randomisation, 30 g erythritol raised plasma erythritol over 1000-fold and, unlike 30 g glucose, enhanced platelet aggregation in all 10 volunteers 11.

Industry-funded results

In SWITCH, funded by the American Beverage Association, which could review the manuscript, 52-week weight loss was 1.4 kg greater with sweetened drinks than with water in completers (90% CI 0.2 to 2.6) and 0.8 kg (−0.1 to 1.7) with imputation for all 493 12.

In three cardiac cohorts, one partly funded by Sanofi, top-quartile against bottom-quartile plasma erythritol had adjusted hazard ratios of 1.80 to 2.95 for 3-year death, myocardial infarction or stroke; in vitro, erythritol enhanced platelet activation 13.

Consensus statements

WHO suggests that non-sugar sweeteners not be used for weight control or disease prevention (conditional, low certainty) 14. Its review found 0.71 kg lower weight in trials mostly of 3 months or less, unchanged fasting glucose, and cohort hazard ratios, over an average 13 years, of 1.23 to 1.34 for type 2 diabetes and 1.32 for cardiovascular disease 14. IARC classified aspartame as possibly carcinogenic (Group 2B, a hazard rather than risk rating) on limited human and animal evidence 15.

Unresolved questions

IARC could not exclude bias or confounding in the liver cancer cohorts; JECFA found the human cancer evidence unconvincing 153. EFSA judged a causal role of dietary erythritol in cardiovascular disease undemonstrated 6.

Regulation

In 2023 JECFA, a United Nations expert committee, kept the aspartame limit at 40 mg per kg body weight a day, above estimated intakes 3.

The limit does not apply in phenylketonuria, and EU labels must declare aspartame a source of phenylalanine 4.

    Funding and finding
    Funding

    Finding
    Acesulfame K is 6-methyl-1,2,3-oxathiazin-4(3H)-one-2,2-dioxide potassium salt, approximately 200 times as sweet as sucrose; it is authorised in 34 food categories (47 uses), many of them restricted to energy-reduced foods or foods with no added sugar. The 95th percentile exposure estimates were generally below the ADI in all population groups.

  3. Regulator

    Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives (JECFA). Evaluation of certain food additives: ninety-sixth report of the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives World Health Organization and Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, Geneva; WHO Technical Report Series, No. 1050, 2023.

    https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240083059

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    Not stated. The Joint Secretariat states that all experts participating in the meeting completed declaration-of-interest forms and that no conflicts of interest were identified. Sponsors submitted unpublished toxicity study reports and dietary exposure information.
    Finding
    Aspartame, approximately 200 times sweeter than sucrose, is fully hydrolysed in the gastrointestinal tract by esterases and peptidases to phenylalanine, aspartic acid and methanol (approximately 50, 40 and 10% by weight); no aspartame enters the systemic circulation. Cohort associations with hepatocellular, breast and haematological cancers were inconsistent, reverse causality, chance, bias and confounding could not be ruled out, and the Committee concluded that the evidence of an association between aspartame consumption and cancer in humans is not convincing; the same conclusion was reached for type 2 diabetes and other non-cancer end-points. The ADI of 0 to 40 mg/kg bw, based on the NOAEL of 4000 mg/kg bw per day in a 104-week rat study and a 100-fold uncertainty factor, was reaffirmed. Dietary exposure estimates of up to 10 mg/kg bw per day for children and 5 mg/kg bw per day for adults at the mean, and up to 20 and 12 mg/kg bw per day for high consumers, do not exceed the ADI.

  5. Regulator

    EFSA Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings (FAF), Castle L, Andreassen M, Aquilina G, Bastos ML, Boon P, et al. Re-evaluation of sucralose (E 955) as a food additive and evaluation of a new application on extension of use of sucralose (E 955) in fine bakery wares EFSA Journal, 2026.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2026.9854PubMed 41710869

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority; requestor: European Commission (question numbers EFSA-Q-2011-00724 and EFSA-Q-2021-00375). Adopted 10 December 2025.
    Finding
    Sucralose is 1,6-dichloro-1,6-dideoxy-beta-D-fructofuranosyl-4-chloro-4-deoxy-alpha-D-galactopyranoside, produced by a process that selectively replaces three hydroxyl groups of sucrose with chlorine. Oral absorption in humans varied between 8.8% and 21.7% of the dose; systemically available sucralose is eliminated mainly in urine, with less than 10% of urinary radioactivity in glucuronide-like metabolites; in two volunteers about 85% of a radiolabelled dose was recovered in faeces after 120 h; no 14CO2 was detected in expired air. From human studies, the Panel concluded that sucralose does not adversely affect glucose and insulin homeostasis (moderate level of confidence). Reference point BMDL 55 mg/kg bw per day for decreased body weight in rats; no need to revise the ADI of 15 mg/kg bw per day established by the SCF in 2000; exposure estimates did not exceed the ADI.

  6. Regulator

    EFSA Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings (FAF), Younes M, Aquilina G, Castle L, Degen G, Engel KH, et al. Re-evaluation of erythritol (E 968) as a food additive EFSA Journal, 2023.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8430PubMed 38125972

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority; requestor: European Commission (question numbers EFSA-Q-2011-00730 and EFSA-Q-2022-00219). Adopted 25 October 2023.
    Finding
    Erythritol is 1,2,3,4-butanetetrol (C4H10O4), a polyol obtained by fermentation, with a sweetness of approximately 60% to 80% that of sucrose. It is absorbed dose-dependently, 78% to 92% is excreted unchanged in urine within 24 h, less than 1% of the dose is metabolised to erythronate, the half-life of excretion is about 4 h, and human gut microbiota metabolise it negligibly; in humans erythritol is also formed endogenously through the pentose phosphate pathway. ADI 0.5 g/kg bw per day (500 mg/kg bw per day), the lower bound of the range of NOAELs for diarrhoea in human studies, without uncertainty factors; 95th percentile acute and chronic exposure estimates were at or above the ADI in all populations, and the Panel considered the exposure assessments overestimates. The Panel considered that the cohort associations of circulating erythritol with cardiovascular disease (Witkowski et al. 2023 and others) do not demonstrate a causal relationship with dietary erythritol, that fasting erythritol may be a biomarker of metabolic disturbance, and that no long-term interventional study is available; the in vitro platelet studies provided insufficient basis to predict effects in humans.

    EFSA Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings (FAF), Castle L, Andreassen M, Aquilina G, Bastos ML, Boon P, et al. Scientific opinion on the extension of the authorisation of use of the food additive steviol glycosides (E 960a–d) and the modification of the acceptable daily intake (ADI) for steviol EFSA Journal, 2024.

    doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9045PubMed 39502654

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    European Food Safety Authority; requestor: European Commission (question number EFSA-Q-2023-00594), following an application to extend uses and modify the ADI. Adopted 25 September 2024.
    Finding
    Steviol glycosides from stevia (E 960a) are obtained by water extraction of the leaves of Stevia rebaudiana Bertoni and contain not less than 95% steviolbioside, rubusoside, dulcoside A, stevioside and rebaudiosides A, B, C, D, E, F and M. Steviol glycosides are not hydrolysed by digestive enzymes of the upper gastrointestinal tract; colonic microbiota release the aglycone steviol, which is absorbed and glucuronidated, and steviol glucuronide is excreted in urine and partly via bile into faeces. In eight men given rebaudioside A or stevioside, steviol glucuronide in urine accounted for 59% and 62% of the doses. The Panel found insufficient justification to raise the ADI of 4 mg/kg bw per day, expressed as steviol equivalents, to 6 or 16 mg/kg bw per day.

  8. Independent research

    Juen Z, Lu Z, Yu R, Chang AN, Wang B, Fitzpatrick AWP, et al. The structure of human sweetness Cell, 2025.

    doi:10.1016/j.cell.2025.04.021PubMed 40339580

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    C.S.Z. is an investigator of the Howard Hughes Medical Institute. The early phase of this project was funded in part by a grant from the Berrie Foundation to Rudy Leibel and C.S.Z.
    Finding
    Cryo-EM structures of the human sweet receptor, a TAS1R2-TAS1R3 heterodimer, bound to sucralose and to aspartame. Both sweeteners bind the same pocket in the Venus flytrap domain of TAS1R2; alanine substitutions of pocket residues (Y103, D142, S165) markedly reduced responses to sucralose in cell-based assays. TAS1R2 binds the ligand and couples to the G protein, and TAS1R3 is an obligatory partner of TAS1R2 for receptor function. The authors state that this single receptor mediates sweet taste in humans and other mammals.

  14. Expert consensus

    World Health Organization. Use of non-sugar sweeteners: WHO guideline World Health Organization, Geneva, 2023.

    PubMed 37256996

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    WHO gratefully acknowledges the financial support provided by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare of the Government of Japan for the guideline development work, including the systematic reviews, and by Qingdao University in China for hosting the 13th meeting of the WHO Nutrition Guidance Expert Advisory Group - Subgroup on Diet and Health in December 2019.
    Finding
    WHO suggests that non-sugar sweeteners not be used as a means of achieving weight control or reducing the risk of noncommunicable diseases (conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence overall). The recommendation does not apply to individuals with existing diabetes, or to sugar alcohols and low-calorie sugars. In the systematic review, randomised trials in adults, most lasting 3 months or less, gave body weight MD -0.71 kg (95% CI -1.13 to -0.28; 29 trials, 2433 participants; low certainty) and fasting glucose MD -0.01 mmol/L (-0.05 to 0.04; 16 trials, 1494 participants; moderate certainty). Prospective cohorts associated higher intake with type 2 diabetes (beverages, HR 1.23, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.32; 13 studies, 408,609 participants), cardiovascular diseases (HR 1.32, 1.17 to 1.50; 3 studies, 166,938 participants) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.12, 1.05 to 1.19). Reverse causation and residual confounding were considered possible contributors.

  15. Expert consensus

    IARC Working Group on the Identification of Carcinogenic Hazards to Humans. Aspartame, methyleugenol, and isoeugenol IARC Monographs on the Identification of Carcinogenic Hazards to Humans, volume 134, 2024.

    PubMed 39715313

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    This programme has been supported since 1982 by Cooperative Agreement U01 CA33193 with the United States National Cancer Institute, Department of Health and Human Services. Additional support has been provided since 1986 by the European Commission Directorate-General for Employment, Social Affairs, and Inclusion, and since 1992 by the United States National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences. The contents of this volume are solely the responsibility of the Working Group.
    Finding
    Aspartame is possibly carcinogenic to humans (Group 2B): limited evidence in humans, from positive associations of artificially sweetened beverage consumption, used as a proxy for aspartame, with hepatocellular carcinoma in three studies including four cohorts, in which bias or confounding could not be ruled out; limited evidence in experimental animals; limited mechanistic evidence. A minority of the Working Group supported Group 2A. In humans, ingested aspartame is hydrolysed by esterases and peptidases in the gastrointestinal tract to aspartic acid, phenylalanine and methanol; phenylalanine is partly converted to tyrosine in the liver by phenylalanine hydroxylase. The Monographs assess the strength of evidence that an agent is a cancer hazard, which the Preamble distinguishes from a cancer risk, the probability of cancer at a given exposure.
