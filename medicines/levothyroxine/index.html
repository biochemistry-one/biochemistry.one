Medicines

Levothyroxine

In brief

Too much levothyroxine can cause atrial fibrillation, angina, heart failure and heart attack, and doses that push the pituitary hormone TSH very low are linked with more strokes, fractures, memory disorders and deaths; started before steroid replacement in adrenal failure it can trigger an adrenal crisis, and in children overtreatment can close skull sutures or growth plates early. Thyroid hormone also acts on bone turnover, cholesterol handling in the liver, energy use in muscle, the brain and blood sugar control. In trials in people with a mildly raised TSH, it brought the blood test into range but did not improve symptoms or quality of life, and in older adults it did not significantly change heart events or deaths.

Serious harms

Levothyroxine is prescribed when the thyroid gland makes too little hormone, and in thyroid cancer to suppress thyrotropin (TSH), the pituitary hormone that stimulates the gland 12.

Most adverse reactions are those of excess hormone, which may raise heart rate, cardiac wall thickness and contractility 4. Excess is common: 41.0% of 339 users aged 65 or more had TSH below 0.45 mU/l 10. In people, hyperthyroidism shifts bone remodelling towards resorption, with a net loss of about 10% per cycle 11. A dose change may take 4 to 6 weeks to act fully 1.

Unresolved questions

Independent cohorts disagree: in UK THIN data, TSH below 0.1 mIU/l was associated with death but not with atrial fibrillation or fractures, and the authors note possible reverse causality 9. Among 8,573,313 Swedes, levothyroxine use was associated with cancer (HR 1.08 in women, 1.06 in men); funding could not be read 12.

Thyroid hormone acts through two receptors, TRα and TRβ, whose amounts differ between tissues 13.

Unresolved questions

Despite normal TSH, 15.2% of 1811 athyreotic patients on levothyroxine had free T3 below the control range 17.

What it is for and how it works

Levothyroxine is synthetic thyroxine (T4), the main hormone the thyroid releases 118. In subclinical hypothyroidism TSH is raised and free T4 normal 19.

Thyroid peroxidase iodinates thyroglobulin and couples its iodotyrosines into T4; DIO1 and DIO2 convert T4 to active T3, and DIO3 inactivates both 2018. Pituitary DIO2 forms the T3 through which T4 suppresses TSH, so TSH guides the dose in primary hypothyroidism 181.

In the body

Levothyroxine is absorbed best from an empty stomach and is cleared slowly, with a half-life of 6 to 7 days 1.

Proton pump inhibitors, antacids, calcium carbonate, ferrous sulfate, bile acid sequestrants and dietary fibre may reduce absorption 4. With tricyclic antidepressants levothyroxine may increase arrhythmia risk, and with sympathomimetics the risk of coronary insufficiency in coronary artery disease 4. The EMA judged biotin interference with thyroid tests causal, with significant potential for mismanagement 21.

Consensus statements

The American Thyroid Association calls the 4-hour separation from calcium and iron traditional but untested 22.

Genetic variation

Rare mutations show which tissues depend on each thyroid hormone receptor 23.

A child with a dominant-negative THRA mutation had growth retardation, constipation and bradycardia despite near-normal thyroid tests; thyroxine suppressed TSH but improved growth, heart rate and gut function little 23. DIO2 Thr92Ala was associated with lower enzyme velocity in human thyroid and muscle 18.

Benefit against harm

In 21 trials with 2192 adults with mildly raised TSH, thyroid hormone brought the test into range without improving quality of life over 3 to 18 months 24.

Consensus statements

The 2019 BMJ panel recommended strongly against treating most adults with subclinical hypothyroidism; it found harms measured in only one trial 27.

Without the medicine

Selenium tablets did not improve well-being: in 412 Danish adults with autoimmune hypothyroidism on levothyroxine, 12-month quality-of-life scores were 28.8 against 28.0 on placebo 28.

Unresolved questions

Long-term outcomes have been studied mostly in subclinical disease, because overt hypothyroidism is generally treated; myxoedema coma, from long-standing untreated severe hypothyroidism, carries 40% mortality despite treatment 29.

How the recommendations were set

TSH rises with age in people without thyroid disease 30.

Among 52,298 UK adults, median TSH at initiation fell from 8.7 to 7.9 mIU/l between 2001 and 2009 31. Of 58,706 US starters in 2008 to 2018, 30.5% had normal thyroid function 32.

Consensus statements

A 2004 US panel did not recommend routine treatment at TSH 4.5 to 10 mIU/l 33. NICE (2019) advises considering treatment at 10 or more, judging the balance under 65 from committee experience 34. The 2013 ETA guideline cited a database study by two of its own authors 35.

Stopping

Some people stay in the normal range after stopping, more often after a mildly raised TSH than after overt hypothyroidism 36.

In 17 observational studies with 1103 patients, at moderate to high risk of bias, 35.6% first treated for subclinical and 11.8% for overt hypothyroidism stayed euthyroid 36. In 370 Dutch adults aged 60 or more, open-label stepwise reduction left 95 (25.7%) off treatment at 1 year; 142 halted when TSH reached 10 mIU/l or more 37.

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber.

    Synthetic T4, chemically identical to thyroidal T4, indicated for primary, secondary and tertiary hypothyroidism and as an adjunct for TSH suppression in thyrotropin-dependent well-differentiated thyroid cancer. Narrow therapeutic index. Once daily on an empty stomach, half to one hour before breakfast, and at least 4 hours apart from drugs that interfere with absorption, including phosphate binders such as calcium carbonate and ferrous sulfate; gastric acidity is required for absorption, and proton pump inhibitors, sucralfate and antacids may reduce it. Absorption 40% to 80%, mostly in the jejunum and upper ileum, increased by fasting. Adult full replacement 1.6 mcg/kg/day, titrated by 12.5 to 25 mcg every 4 to 6 weeks; TSH is checked 6 to 8 weeks after a dose change; peak effect may take 4 to 6 weeks; in primary hypothyroidism the dose is titrated to a normal TSH, in secondary or tertiary hypothyroidism to free T4. Atrial fibrillation is the most common arrhythmia of overtreatment in the elderly; over-replacement may increase bone resorption and decrease bone mineral density, particularly in postmenopausal women. If cardiac symptoms develop or worsen, the dose is reduced or withheld for one week and restarted lower. Replacement therapy is generally taken for life.

    Review. Thyroid hormone is synthesised in thyroid follicles by iodination of tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin; TSH, secreted by the anterior pituitary in response to feedback from circulating thyroid hormone, acts on the TSH receptor of follicular cells and regulates iodide uptake by the sodium/iodide symporter.     Finding
    Review. Thyroid hormone receptor isoforms alpha and beta are differentially expressed in tissues and have distinct roles; local activation of T4 to T3 by D2, expressed in hypothalamus, white fat, brown fat and skeletal muscle, is a key mechanism of metabolic regulation and is required for adaptive thermogenesis. Thyroid hormone acts on metabolism mainly through brain, white fat, brown fat, skeletal muscle, liver and pancreas. Indication for treatment and thyroid antibodies were not recorded, and the design had no control group.
