Salbutamol

In brief

Salbutamol can tighten the airways instead of opening them, cause lactic acidosis and low blood potassium and disturb heart rhythm; deaths have been reported with excessive use, and its use to delay labour has been linked to fluid on the lungs and heart attacks in mothers. Its receptor is also found in the heart, muscle, fat and brain, so doses can shift potassium, glucose and lactate across the body. It opens narrowed airways within five minutes but leaves airway inflammation untreated; in mild asthma from age 12, 109 in 1000 people using only a reliever of this kind needed steroid tablets or injections for an attack over up to a year, against 52 using a reliever that also contains a steroid.

Salbutamol, albuterol in the US, is prescribed for narrowed airways in asthma and other reversible airway disease 1.

Potassium possibly shifts into cells; the fall is usually transient, lowest a mean 12 hours after presentation 14. In 47 reported patients, lactate fell by 3.0 mmol/l or more, or to 2.5 mmol/l or less, after a median 13 hours (3 to 56) 3. Excessive-use deaths are suspected to follow cardiac arrest in a severe attack with hypoxia 1. None of 12 placebo-controlled trials of delaying labour reported cardiac arrest or intensive care admission 10.

Unresolved questions

In pooled trials of 3 days to 1 year, cardiovascular events were more frequent (RR 2.54), mostly sinus tachycardia; only trials reporting an adverse event were included 11. Major cardiovascular events were associated with starting the agonist in COPD (HR 2.02), not significantly in asthma, and with 3 or more canisters a year in Taiwan (2.82 against 0.99 per 100 person-years) 1213. In Japanese reports, inhaled salbutamol was associated with Takotsubo cardiomyopathy 14. Pregnancy data are observational: a cleft palate association (OR 1.50; 28,481 cases) was unconfirmed in 519,242 births, and an autism association (1.29) was non-significant with confirmed maternal asthma 1151617.

Other pathways it affects

The receptor salbutamol binds is found in many tissues besides airway muscle 181.

  • Lung: epithelium, glands, vessels and immune cells express it; an anti-inflammatory effect is not indicated 18.
  • Heart: beta2 receptors are 10% to 50% of cardiac beta-adrenoceptors; protein kinase A phosphorylates phospholamban, RyR2 and troponin I, and cyclic AMP activates the HCN4 pacemaker channel 119.
  • Muscle and potassium (animal): in rat muscle at high potassium, salbutamol with warming and lactic acid restored force, recovery depending on the Na+-K+ pump 20.
  • Muscle and heart (healthy people): oral 16 mg daily for 11 weeks in 26 trained men added 1.8 kg lean mass, blunted endurance gains and thickened heart walls on echocardiography, not magnetic resonance 21; inhaled doses lowered fat mass in women only 22.
  • Glucose and lactate (people): glucose exceeded 11 mmol/l in 56 of 154 children, 95% of all 154 also given corticosteroid; lactate is ascribed to stimulated glycolysis, fatty acids inhibiting pyruvate dehydrogenase 23.
  • Brain (cells, animals): salbutamol lowered alpha-synuclein through reduced H3K27 acetylation at SNCA; clenbuterol protected dopaminergic neurons in MPTP-treated mice 23.
  • Ovary and fetus (animals): dietary doses caused benign mesovarian leiomyomas in rats, subcutaneous doses cleft palate in 4.5% and 9.3% of mouse fetuses 1.

Industry-funded results

In ten men, intravenous salbutamol raised brown-fat glucose uptake; a GSK chair part-funded the study 24.

Unresolved questions

Salbutamol use was associated with less Parkinson’s disease in Norway (rate ratio 0.66), not significantly in a 2025 meta-analysis (0.88), where causation was judged unsupported 2325.

What it is for and how it works

Inhaled salbutamol relaxes the muscle of narrowed airways within 5 minutes, for 3 to 6 hours 18. Its receptor couples to Gs; adenylyl cyclase forms cyclic AMP, and protein kinase A lowers myosin phosphorylation and cytosolic calcium 181.

Receptor kinases and arrestin uncouple the occupied receptor from Gs, desensitising airway cells, tissue and mice 1826; in people, bronchoprotection fell by 0.75 doubling doses with regular use (3 trials, 50 people) 27.

Unresolved questions

No tolerance was detected in studies of 165 and 108 patients, funding unread, against a 17.8% smaller response across 22 trials 282930.

In the body

More salbutamol reaches the blood from a tablet than from inhaled doses 19.

Peak plasma levels were about 3 ng/ml after 12 inhaled puffs and 18 ng/ml after a 4 mg tablet; half-life is about 4.6 to 6 hours 19. SULT1A3, most active in the small intestine, sulfates it 31. Nebulised doses above 3 mg raised children’s heart rate by more than 10% without more bronchodilation 5. Beta-blockers may provoke severe bronchospasm; loop or thiazide diuretics can worsen hypokalaemia and ECG change 1.

Genetic variation

Routine genetic testing for this drug class is judged unwarranted by a pharmacology reference 18.

Industry-funded results

In a GSK part-funded meta-analysis, ADRB2 haplotypes were not associated with exacerbations in 973 young people using as-required short-acting agonists 32.

Benefit against harm

Salbutamol relieves symptoms but leaves airway inflammation untreated 1833.

Without the medicine

In a randomised trial of 655 adults, breathing retraining raised asthma quality-of-life scores by 0.28 and 0.24 at 12 months; attacks were not significantly fewer (9% and 11% against 15%) 37.

How the recommendations were set

Reliever limits were set from studies linking heavy use with severe attacks and death 3338.

Among 195 UK asthma deaths, 65 of 165 (39%) were issued more than 12 relievers in the final year, without a comparison group 38. In 2023 the EU warned that overuse may raise the risk of severe exacerbations and death, setting re-evaluation above twice-weekly use 33.

Consensus statements

GINA dropped the reliever alone for adults and adolescents in 2019, and BTS, NICE and SIGN at any age in 2024 3940.

Industry-funded results

In AstraZeneca’s Swedish SABINA cohort, asthma was the recorded cause of 28 of 2564 deaths 41.

Unresolved questions

Pooled mortality (RR 2.04, I² 98.3%) rests on three cohorts, two from SABINA; confounding by severity remains possible 4243. Both GINA chairs declared AstraZeneca fees in 2021, and three SYGMA 1 authors co-wrote GINA 2019 444539.

Stopping

In two trials, airway calibre and responsiveness did not differ significantly 1 and 4 weeks after regular salbutamol was stopped 27.

Needing more doses than usual calls for re-evaluation 1.

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber.

  39. Expert consensus

    Reddel HK, FitzGerald JM, Bateman ED, Bacharier LB, Becker A, Brusselle G, et al. GINA 2019: a fundamental change in asthma management European Respiratory Journal, 2019.

    doi:10.1183/13993003.01046-2019PubMed 31249014

    Funding and finding
    Funding
    No support statement is given. The authors are members of the GINA Science Committee or the GINA Board; the article states that GINA does not accept donations and that its work is supported only by the sale and licensing of GINA reports and other publications and by the voluntary work of committee members.
    Finding
    In April 2019 the Global Initiative for Asthma stopped recommending treatment of asthma in adults and adolescents with short-acting beta2-agonists alone, recommending instead symptom-driven treatment in mild asthma or daily inhaled corticosteroid-containing treatment, to reduce the risk of serious exacerbations. The stated grounds include case-control studies linking overuse of short-acting agonists to asthma death, randomised trials finding no advantage of regular over as-needed use, the finding of the UK National Review of Asthma Deaths that 9% of asthma deaths occurred in patients treated with a short-acting agonist alone and 39% were associated with excess prescriptions for such agonists, and an almost two-thirds reduction in severe exacerbations with as-needed low-dose budesonide-formoterol compared with as-needed short-acting agonist. In the SYGMA 1 trial 12% of patients receiving as-needed short-acting agonist had a severe exacerbation within 12 months. Among adults with apparently mild asthma, 30-37% of those with acute asthma, 16% of those with near-fatal asthma and 15-20% of those dying of asthma had symptoms less than weekly in the previous three months.
