Medicines

Sertraline

In brief

Sertraline can raise suicidal thinking under the age of 25 and cause serotonin syndrome, bleeding, dangerously low blood sodium and withdrawal reactions; newborns exposed late in pregnancy can need breathing support, and falls, fractures and seizures are more frequent among older users. The transporter it blocks also clears serotonin in the gut lining and loads it into blood platelets; serotonin receptors are found in the heart, lung arteries, gut and immune cells, and pineal enzymes turn serotonin into melatonin. In trials the average gain over placebo was small, and an estimated 15 in 100 participants benefited substantially beyond placebo.

Serious harms

Sertraline is prescribed for depression and for panic, social anxiety, obsessive–compulsive, post-traumatic stress and premenstrual dysphoric disorders 1.

In regulators’ clinical study reports, 27 suicide attempts and 32 ideation events had been coded as emotional lability or worsening depression; in 16 sertraline trials, a terminology unable to code akathisia or suicidal ideation was used 10.

MAO inhibitors raise serotonin syndrome risk by impairing serotonin breakdown 2. Sertraline blocks serotonin uptake into platelets, and impaired platelet aggregation is the proposed cause of bleeding 15. Hyponatraemia is often attributed to inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion 2.

In older people, most risks were highest in the first 28 days after starting and after stopping; confounding by indication is possible 6. In a randomised trial of fluoxetine, another SSRI, fractures were recorded in 45 of 1564 against 23 of 1563 stroke patients over 6 months, about 1 extra per 71 treated 11. Among women with depression, the adjusted odds ratio for newborn pulmonary hypertension after late-pregnancy SSRI use was 1.10 (95% CI 0.94 to 1.29) 12. Transient, asymptomatic aminotransferase rises and rare clinically apparent liver injury are linked to sertraline 13.

Shown in mice: 6 weeks of fluoxetine caused bone loss through raised sympathetic output, prevented by propranolol 16.

Measured in people: new weight gain of 5% or more was recorded at 11.2 against 8.1 per 100 person-years with and without antidepressants, 1 extra per 59 patient-years 17. Ejaculation failure was reported in 8% of men against 1% on placebo, rates described in the label as possibly underestimated 2.

Unresolved questions

5-HT1A is found in lymph nodes, thymus, spleen and activated T cells, and pineal AANAT and ASMT convert serotonin to melatonin; sertraline’s effects there were unmeasured in the studies cited here 14.

What it is for and how it works

Sertraline blocks the transporter that returns serotonin, a signalling molecule, into the nerve cell that released it 151.

SERT (SLC6A4) moves serotonin back with Na+ in exchange for K+, and MAO-A then deaminates it 15.

Unresolved questions

In an umbrella review, depression was not consistently associated with serotonin concentrations or transporter genotype 18. Critics cite tryptophan depletion 19, and reduced serotonin release was inferred from PET in 17 patients 20.

In the body

Half of the sertraline in blood is cleared in about a day 1.

CYP2C19 and CYP2B6 are regarded as the most relevant metabolising enzymes 2122. Exposure was about threefold higher in 10 patients with mild hepatic impairment 1. Sertraline inhibits CYP2D6 and may raise exposure to flecainide, propafenone and metoprolol 1.

Genetic variation

Inherited enzyme variants alter the amount of sertraline in the blood 21.

Serum sertraline was 2.68-fold higher in CYP2C19 poor metabolisers among 1202 patients 21.

Consensus statements

CPIC suggests a 50% lower maintenance dose or another drug for CYP2C19 poor metabolisers 23.

Benefit against harm

On average, people improved slightly more on antidepressants than on placebo 24.

The mean difference was 1.75 Hamilton points, 1.1 at a baseline of 16 and 2.5 at 29.6 24. Drug companies funded 409 of 522 trials in a network meta-analysis of 21 antidepressants 26. In the NIHR-funded PANDA trial of 655 primary-care adults, depression scores on sertraline were not significantly lower at 6 weeks, 13% lower at 12 weeks, and anxiety scores 21% lower at 6 weeks 27.

Unresolved questions

All 131 SSRI trials in one review were at high risk of bias, and long-term harm data were almost absent 25.

Without the medicine

Exercise and talking therapy were compared with antidepressants in independent randomised trials 2829.

In SMILE, 16-week remission was 45% with supervised exercise, 40% at home, 47% with sertraline and 31% with placebo (p=0.057), among 202 adults 28. In five trials of 660 patients, response was 44.2% with second-generation antidepressants and 45.5% with cognitive behavioural therapy 29.

How the recommendations were set

Selective publication of SSRI trials was documented in regulators’ files 30.

Mean scores were missing from FDA reviews for four non-significant sertraline trials, with 486 patients, 38% of those in sertraline trials 31.

Consensus statements

In 2010 the APA recommended an antidepressant or psychotherapy as initial treatment for mild to moderate depression; all six Work Group members disclosed drug-company ties 32. In 2022 NICE ranked SSRIs ninth of eleven options for less severe depression (PHQ-9 below 16) 33.

Stopping

Dizziness, electric-shock sensations, anxiety and seizures have been reported after stopping, particularly abruptly; gradual reduction is recommended 2.

Across 79 studies, discontinuation symptoms were recorded in 31% after stopping an antidepressant and 17% after placebo, 8 percentage points more in randomised trials; in 5 sertraline studies, 18% 9. In ANTLER, 56% of 240 people tapered to placebo over 1 to 2 months relapsed within 52 weeks, against 39% of 238 who continued, and withdrawal scores were higher 34. In a Cochrane review of 33 trials, most tapers were 4 weeks or shorter and withdrawal was generally not assessed 35.

Unresolved questions

In 74% of the cohorts in that review, funding was definitely or probably pharmaceutical; in the 5 studies measuring withdrawal systematically, pooled incidence was 55% 36. Early relapse after rapid switches to placebo is possibly attributable to withdrawal reactions 37.

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber.

    Finding
    Indicated for major depressive disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, panic disorder, posttraumatic stress disorder, social anxiety disorder and premenstrual dysphoric disorder; starting dose 50 mg per day for major depressive disorder, maximum 200 mg per day. Sertraline potentiates serotonergic activity in the central nervous system through inhibition of neuronal reuptake of serotonin; at clinically relevant doses it blocks the uptake of serotonin into human platelets; in vitro studies in animals suggest potent and selective inhibition of neuronal serotonin reuptake with only very weak effects on norepinephrine and dopamine reuptake; no significant affinity for adrenergic, cholinergic, GABA, dopaminergic, histaminergic, serotonergic (5HT1A, 5HT1B, 5HT2) or benzodiazepine receptors; does not inhibit monoamine oxidase. Peak plasma concentrations 4.5 to 8.4 hours after dosing; average terminal elimination half-life about 26 hours; steady state after one week of once-daily dosing; 98% bound to serum proteins. Extensive first-pass metabolism; the principal initial pathway is N-demethylation; N-desmethylsertraline is substantially less active and has a terminal half-life of 62 to 104 hours. Drugs that interfere with serotonin reuptake, including sertraline, increase the risk of bleeding events, and aspirin, NSAIDs, other antiplatelet drugs, warfarin and other anticoagulants may add to this risk. Hyponatraemia may occur with SSRIs and SNRIs; in many cases it appears to result from the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion; elderly patients, patients taking diuretics and volume-depleted patients may be at greater risk. Adverse reactions after discontinuation, particularly abrupt discontinuation, include nausea, sweating, dysphoric mood, irritability, agitation, dizziness, sensory disturbances such as electric shock sensations, tremor, anxiety, confusion, headache, lethargy, emotional lability, insomnia, hypomania, tinnitus and seizures; gradual dose reduction rather than abrupt cessation is recommended whenever possible, and patients are advised not to stop abruptly and to discuss any tapering regimen with their healthcare provider. In pooled randomised placebo-controlled trials in 3066 adults exposed to sertraline for 8 to 12 weeks across six indications: nausea 26% against 12% on placebo (n=2293), diarrhoea or loose stools 20% against 10%, ejaculation failure 8% against 1% of men; 368 of 3066 (12%) discontinued for an adverse reaction against 93 of 2293 (4%) on placebo.
