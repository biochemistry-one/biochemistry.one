Medicines

Folic acid

In brief

Folic acid can correct the anaemia of vitamin B12 deficiency while nerve and spinal cord damage progresses, and it can cause rare severe allergy and lower blood levels of some epilepsy drugs; in trials with added B vitamins, kidney function fell faster and arteries narrowed again more often after stenting, and in some trials cancer was more frequent. Human gut and liver convert it slowly, unconverted folic acid is detectable in the blood of most people where food is fortified, and it changes DNA methylation and the action of drugs that block folate enzymes. After a previous affected pregnancy, 4 mg daily prevented about 1 neural tube defect per 40 women treated, while in trials of heart disease and cognition benefit was small or absent.

Serious harms

Folic acid is prescribed for folate-deficiency anaemia, to prevent a recurrent neural tube defect in pregnancy and to reduce methotrexate side effects 123.

Folic acid enters the cell’s folate pool through dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), independently of vitamin B12 48. In B12 deficiency, it restarts DNA synthesis in bone marrow but cannot restore the methionine cycle 4. The neuropathy, which can occur without anaemia, is attributed to lost methionine synthase activity 48.

Neurological relapses typically occurred within 2 years; signs often resolved with cobalamin but became irreversible in several cases 4. Under the US label, doses above 0.1 mg daily may obscure pernicious anaemia 1. EFSA rated the certainty that folic acid itself worsens the neuropathy as low (15 to 50% probability) 4. B12 deficiency was associated with 2 years or more of proton pump inhibitor use (OR 1.65; 25,956 cases, 184,199 controls) and may affect up to 1 in 10 metformin users 910.

Across 13 trials, first cancers occurred in 7.7% of 24,799 given folic acid and 7.3% of 24,822 controls over a mean 5.5 years (RR 1.06, 0.99 to 1.13); only cancers during treatment were counted 11.

Industry-funded results

In Norwegian trials supported by Alpharma, 0.8 mg with B12 for a median 39 months in 6837 patients with ischaemic heart disease was followed over a median 78 months by cancer in 10.0% against 8.4% (HR 1.21) and all-cause death in 16.1% against 13.8% (HR 1.18), 1 per 44 12. Under the UK label, folate should not be used routinely with coronary stents 2. In a trial part-funded by Medice, 1.2 mg with B6 and B12 after stenting was followed by repeat revascularisation in 50 of 316 (15.8%) against 34 of 320 (10.6%) by 250 days, 1 per 19 13.

Unresolved questions

In observational registers, children of mothers with epilepsy exposed in pregnancy to a mean 4.3 mg folic acid had cancer by age 20 in 1.4% against 0.6% (5934 exposed; HR 2.7, 1.2 to 6.3); without maternal epilepsy, HR was 1.1 (0.9 to 1.4) 14.

Other pathways it affects

Under US fortification, unmetabolised folic acid was detectable in the blood of over 95% of people, with or without supplements 15.

The reduced folate carrier, the main route into tissues including brain, has about 100 times lower affinity for folic acid than for reduced folates 4.

In people, cord-blood methylation at LINE-1, IGF2 and BDNF was lower when 400 µg was continued in later pregnancy (86 samples), without a shown health effect 164. In one rat study, folic acid at one dietary level increased colon aberrant crypt foci when started before a carcinogen and reduced them when started after 4.

Unresolved questions

Natural killer cell cytotoxicity was about 23% lower with detectable unmetabolised folic acid in 105 women, and fell on 5 mg daily in 30 adults without controls 1718.

What it is for and how it works

Folate is needed to make DNA; dietary deficiency causes anaemia with large red cells 19.

DHFR reduces folic acid to tetrahydrofolate, which binds the one-carbon units used in DNA synthesis 419.

In the body

Human gut and liver convert folic acid slowly; above 1 mg daily, most of the excess is excreted unchanged in urine 201.

In 5 adults, 80 ± 12% of labelled portal-vein folate was unchanged folic acid 15 minutes after 220 µg 20. DHFR activity per gram of 6 human livers was under 2% of rat values 21. Fluorouracil toxicity may occur with folic acid; trimethoprim or sulphonamides may reduce its effect, potentially seriously in megaloblastic anaemia 2. Methotrexate and trimethoprim inhibit DHFR; fluorouracil inhibits thymidylate synthase 19.

Genetic variation

A common gene variant is associated with lower blood folate; it alters MTHFR, an enzyme other than DHFR 2219.

About 10 to 15% of people of European or Japanese origin are homozygous for 677C>T (TT) 23. TT women had 16% lower red cell folate than CC women 22.

Consensus statements

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics finds no evidence that specific treatments reduce genotype-related risks 24.

Benefit against harm

Folic acid before conception lowers the risk of neural tube defects, malformations of brain and spinal cord 25.

After a previous affected pregnancy, with 4 mg daily to 12 weeks, defects occurred in 6 of 593 (1.0%) against 21 of 602 (3.5%) pregnancies (RR 0.28), 1 prevented per 40 women 26. Across 5 trials (6708 births), RR was 0.31 (0.17 to 0.58); only one trial was at low risk of bias, and a single trial of 261 tested 0.4 mg or less 25.

Unresolved questions

In a non-randomised Chinese campaign of 400 µg, calculated pregnancies per defect avoided were about 263 in the high-risk north and 2500 in the south 28.

Without the medicine

Folate occurs in dark green vegetables, legumes, nuts, liver and yeast, with considerable cooking losses 4.

Unresolved questions

In four food-folate trials, three with industry co-funding, blood folate and homocysteine were measured; neural tube defects were not 29303132.

How the recommendations were set

The European upper level of 1000 µg a day, retained in 2023, is based on the risk that neurological symptoms progress in B12 deficiency 4.

Consensus statements

The Institute of Medicine (1998) took 5 mg as the lowest-observed-adverse-effect level and divided it by 5; its funders included a corporate fund with Roche Vitamins among donors 33. The US Preventive Services Task Force finds adequate evidence that usual doses are not associated with serious harms 34.

Unresolved questions

Wald and colleagues note that the IOM drew on 23 studies, mostly from the 1950s, and attribute the neuropathy to untreated B12 deficiency 35. The periconceptional studies were not designed to assess adverse effects, and side effects were reported in only one trial of the Cochrane review 3325.

Stopping

Blood folate falls when folic acid is stopped in pregnancy 36.

In 60 women randomised to stop 400 µg at 14 weeks, serum folate fell from 45.7 to 19.5 nmol/L by week 36 and homocysteine rose from 6.6 to 7.6 µmol/L 36.

Unresolved questions

In the adenoma trial, risk ratios were attenuated and null in the 7.5 years after treatment 4.

A prescription is changed together with the prescriber.

    Finding
    Folates consist of a pterin moiety linked to p-aminobenzoic acid and one or more glutamates, substituted at N5 or N10 by one-carbon units; natural folates are reduced polyglutamates usually with five to seven glutamates. A jejunal hydrolase (EC 3.4.17.21) converts polyglutamates to monoglutamates, which PCFT (SLC46A1), the most important intestinal transporter, and RFC (SLC19A1) move into enterocytes; 5-methyl-THF is the main circulating form; RFC, the main transporter from blood to tissues, has about 100 times lower affinity for folic acid than for reduced folates. Folylpolyglutamate synthase retains folates as THF polyglutamates; 5-methyl-THF monoglutamate and folic acid are poor substrates, and 5-methyl-THF is lost from cells unless methionine synthase converts it to THF. Folic acid, a fully oxidised monoglutamate, is reduced by DHFR via dihydrofolate to THF. The MTHFR reaction is essentially irreversible under physiological conditions and SAM is its allosteric inhibitor; in cobalamin deficiency 5-methyl-THF is metabolically trapped, SAM falls, MTHFR becomes fully active, and a functional folate deficiency develops with raised plasma 5-methyl-THF and homocysteine, depleted erythrocyte folate and fewer methyl groups for transmethylation. The megaloblastic anaemia of cobalamin deficiency is identical to that of folate deficiency; neurological manifestations (myeloneuropathy from demyelination) can occur with or without anaemia, are almost never seen in folate deficiency, and have an unknown mechanism, with congenital defects of MTHFR and methionine synthase pointing to methionine synthase rather than methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Folic acid can initiate DNA synthesis in cobalamin deficiency through DHFR but cannot correct the methylation defect. MTHFR 677TT is associated with lower serum folate and higher homocysteine; in a genome-wide study of 2232 Irish adults aged 18 to 28 years MTHFR 677C>T was the only significant modifier of red cell and serum folate. ULs for folic acid retained: 1000 ug/day for adults including pregnant and lactating women, with progression of neurological symptoms in cobalamin-deficient patients as critical effect.

    Finding
    Review. Mammals have two B12-dependent enzymes: methionine synthase, which uses methylcobalamin, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase, which uses adenosylcobalamin to convert methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. Methionine synthase works in two half-reactions: methylcobalamin methylates homocysteine to methionine, leaving cob(I)alamin, which removes the methyl group from 5-methyl-THF to give THF and methylcobalamin; occasional oxidation to cob(II)alamin is repaired by reductive methylation needing NADPH, methionine synthase reductase and S-adenosylmethionine. Food cobalamin binds haptocorrin; in the duodenum pancreatic proteases release it, intrinsic factor binds it, and intestinal cells take it up by cubilin/AMN receptor-mediated endocytosis. About 20% of circulating cobalamin is bound to transcobalamin, which delivers it to cells through the transcobalamin receptor. Mutations in the cblG (methionine synthase) and cblE (reductase) loci cause isolated hyperhomocysteinaemia without methylmalonic aciduria.

    Finding
    Decreased vitamin B12 levels, or vitamin B12 deficiency, is now considered a common adverse drug reaction of metformin. After a European review of the brand leader Glucophage with MHRA input, the product information was updated to state that vitamin B12 deficiency is common and may affect up to 1 in 10 people who take it, and that the risk increases with higher dose, longer treatment duration and pre-existing risk factors. The agency advises testing serum vitamin B12 when deficiency is suspected, for example in patients presenting with megaloblastic anaemia or new-onset neuropathy, considering periodic monitoring in patients with risk factors, and continuing metformin while the deficiency is corrected. The mechanism is described as multifactorial, comprising altered intestinal motility, bacterial overgrowth and reduced uptake of vitamin B12 in the small intestine. Patients with deficiency can be asymptomatic or present with megaloblastic anaemia, neuropathy or both; other features include depression, irritability, cognitive impairment, glossitis, mouth ulcers and visual and motor disturbances, and the statement notes that anaemia or neuropathy caused by deficiency should be diagnosed and treated as soon as possible to avoid permanent symptoms. Listed risk factors: baseline B12 at the lower end of the normal range; conditions reducing absorption, including older age, total or partial gastrectomy, Crohn's disease and other inflammatory bowel disorders and autoimmune conditions; diets low in B12 such as strict vegan and some vegetarian diets; concomitant medicines impairing B12 absorption, including proton pump inhibitors and colchicine; and genetic predisposition such as Imerslund-Grasbeck syndrome and transcobalamin II deficiency. The four cited references are Aroda 2016, Beulens 2015, de Jager 2010 and Miller 2018.

    Results read from the published abstract. 119 women completed (59 folic acid, 60 placebo). In women who stopped, serum folate fell from 45.7 ± 21.3 to 19.5 ± 16.5 nmol/L between gestational weeks 14 and 36 and homocysteine rose from 6.6 ± 2.3 to 7.6 ± 2.3 µmol/L; continuation prevented these changes and raised red cell folate from 1203 ± 639 to 1746 ± 683 nmol/L. Cord red cell folate 1993 ± 862 against 1418 ± 557 nmol/L (P = 0.001). The authors state that benefits for pregnancy outcomes or early childhood require additional study.
